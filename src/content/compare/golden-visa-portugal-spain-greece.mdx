Three years ago this comparison had a simple answer: buy property in whichever of the three you liked, collect a residence permit, and wait five years. All three parts of that sentence are now wrong.

Spain abolished its programme. Portugal removed property from its own. And in May 2026 Portugal doubled the naturalisation period for most foreign nationals. What follows is where each of the three actually stands, and which question each one answers.

## Where the Three Stand

| | Portugal | Spain | Greece |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme status | Open | Closed 3 April 2025 | Open |
| Property qualifies | No, since October 2023 | Not applicable | Yes |
| Entry investment | EUR 500,000 fund | Not applicable | EUR 250,000 to EUR 800,000 |
| Minimum stay | About 7 days a year | Not applicable | None |
| Residence for citizenship | 10 years, 7 for EU and CPLP | Not applicable | 7 years |
| Stay needed for that clock | The programme minimum suffices | Not applicable | 183 days a year |
| Language for naturalisation | A2 Portuguese | Not applicable | B1 Greek |

## Spain: The Programme Is Gone

Organic Law 1/2025 was published in the Boletín Oficial del Estado on 2 January 2025 and eliminated the investor residence permit with effect from 3 April 2025. Applications were accepted until 2 April. The political argument was the same one Portugal used in 2023: investor visas were held to be adding pressure to housing markets in Madrid, Barcelona and the coastal costas.

Existing holders were not stripped of anything. Investors who applied before the cut-off keep their permits and their renewal rights, and their path to permanent residence and naturalisation is unaffected by the abolition itself.

For anyone deciding now, Spain simply is not on the list. The routes that remain are the non-lucrative visa, which requires proven passive income and does not permit work, and the digital nomad visa for remote earners. Both require you to live in Spain, which is a materially different proposition from an investment permit.

The practical consequence for this comparison is narrower than it looks. Spain's departure removed the largest property-based competitor to Greece, and the demand that used to split three ways now splits two.

## Greece: Property Still Works, at a Price That Depends on the Map

Greece kept the structure Portugal abandoned. A qualifying property purchase still produces a residence permit, and the permit carries no minimum stay at all.

| Zone | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Attica, including Athens and the Athens Riviera | EUR 800,000 |
| Greater Thessaloniki | EUR 800,000 |
| Mykonos, Santorini, and islands above 3,100 population | EUR 800,000 |
| Everywhere else in Greece | EUR 400,000 |
| Commercial-to-residential conversion, anywhere | EUR 250,000 |
| Listed-building restoration, anywhere | EUR 250,000 |

Two conditions sit underneath the headline numbers and both bite. The EUR 400,000 and EUR 800,000 tiers require a single property with a minimum living area of 120 square metres, which rules out the small high-yield apartment that would otherwise be the obvious qualifying purchase. And the EUR 250,000 conversion tier requires the conversion to be completed, which is a construction project rather than a purchase.

The 120 square metre floor deserves particular attention from anyone modelling rental income. Yield per square metre falls as unit size rises in almost every Mediterranean market, so the rule pushes the qualifying investor into the lower-yielding class of stock by design. A quoted city yield is usually a one-bedroom yield, and a compliant 120 square metre property will not achieve it.

## Portugal: No Property, and a Clock That Just Doubled

Portugal removed the real estate acquisition and capital transfer routes in October 2023 under the Mais Habitação package, and excluded funds that themselves invest in Portuguese real estate. The full account is in [the Golden Visa real estate route, ended](/guides/portugal-golden-visa-real-estate-ended/).

What remains, per AIMA's current guidance, is five routes: investment funds, company investment, arts and heritage support, research funding, and job creation. In practice almost all volume goes through the EUR 500,000 CMVM-regulated fund, whose selection criteria are set out in [Portugal Golden Visa investment funds](/guides/portugal-golden-visa-fund-investment-2026/).

The stay requirement remains the lightest in Europe at roughly seven days a year on average. The processing is not light: AIMA has been taking 18 to 24 months to issue permits.

Then came the second change. Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, published on 18 May 2026 and in force from 19 May, raised naturalisation from five years to seven for EU and CPLP nationals and ten for everyone else, and moved the start of the clock from the application date to the date the permit is issued. Parliament passed it on 1 April 2026 by 152 votes to 64. The detail, including who the transitional rule protects, is in [Portuguese citizenship by investment](/move-to-portugal/citizenship-by-investment/).

## The Comparison That Actually Matters

Put the two open programmes side by side on the question most investors are really asking, and the answer is not the one the headline thresholds suggest.

| Objective | Greece | Portugal |
|---|---|---|
| A residence permit without moving | Yes, and no stay at all | Yes, at about 7 days a year |
| Own a property with the capital | Yes | No, a fund unit |
| Lowest entry cost | EUR 400,000 outside premium zones | EUR 500,000 |
| Schengen travel from day one | Yes | Yes |
| A citizenship clock while living abroad | No | Yes |
| Time to citizenship if you do move | 7 years at 183 days | 10 years for most non-EU nationals |

That fifth row is the structural difference and it is rarely stated plainly. Greece's golden visa has no stay requirement, and Greek naturalisation requires seven years of physical residence at 183 or more days a year. A Greek permit held for a decade by someone living in London or Dubai produces renewals and nothing else: no accrued time toward a passport.

Portugal's programme minimum, seven days a year, is itself sufficient to maintain legal residence, and legal residence is what the naturalisation clock counts. The clock is now ten years long for an American, British, Canadian or Emirati applicant, but it runs.

So the choice separates cleanly. If the objective is a European base, an asset you control, and Schengen mobility, Greece is cheaper, admits property, and asks nothing of your calendar. If the objective is eventually a passport without relocating, Portugal is the only one of the three that offers a route, and it is a ten-year one.

## No EU Country Sells a Passport

Worth stating because the phrase persists in marketing. On 29 April 2025 the Court of Justice of the European Union held in Commission v Malta, case C-181/23, that Malta's investor citizenship scheme breached EU law, describing it as a commercialisation of Union citizenship. Malta's programme closed.

That was the last direct citizenship-for-investment scheme in the European Union. Every remaining programme, Portugal's and Greece's included, sells residence. Citizenship is a separate national process with its own conditions, and both countries have tightened those conditions rather than loosened them.

Anyone quoting a European passport in exchange for a defined sum is describing a product that ceased to exist in April 2025.

## The Other Markets Buyers Ask About

Portugal, Spain and Greece are the three that get compared directly, but buyers routinely ask about six more. Their positions differ enough that lumping them together is the mistake.

| Market | Status in 2026 | Entry | Citizenship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cyprus | Permanent residency by investment, open | EUR 300,000 plus EUR 50,000 annual income | After 8 years of legal residence |
| Malta | Citizenship scheme closed 29 April 2025 | Residency programme only | No investment route |
| Turkey | Citizenship by investment, open | USD 400,000 property, 3-year no-sale rule | Direct, within months, but not EU |
| UAE | Golden visa, open | AED 2 million property | No route at all |
| Italy | Investor visa open, thresholds vary by route | Startup, company, bonds or philanthropy | Ordinary naturalisation, 10 years |
| United Kingdom | Tier 1 Investor closed 17 February 2022 | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| France | No investor visa | Talent passport routes only | Ordinary naturalisation |

Three of those deserve a sentence each, because they answer genuinely different questions.

**Cyprus** is the closest structural match to what Greece offers, at a lower threshold, with an income test attached and a visit obligation of once every two years rather than a stay requirement. Its citizenship-by-investment scheme closed in 2020 and naturalisation now takes eight years of legal residence, so the same physical-presence problem applies as in Greece.

**Turkey** is the only one on this list that still sells a passport directly, at USD 400,000 in property held for three years. It is also outside the European Union, which is usually the whole point of the exercise, so it answers a mobility question rather than an EU one.

**The UAE** grants a renewable residence visa against AED 2 million of property and removed its 50 per cent upfront payment rule in February 2026, opening the route to staged off-plan purchases. It offers no naturalisation path of any kind, which makes it a base rather than a passport plan.

France and the United Kingdom have no investment route to residence at all, and a comparison that presents them as alternatives is comparing a programme against its absence.

## What the Capital Is Doing

The immigration comparison is only half of the decision, and for most buyers it is the less consequential half.

| | EUR 500,000 Portuguese fund | EUR 400,000 Greek property |
|---|---|---|
| What you own | Units in a CMVM-regulated vehicle | Freehold real estate |
| Control | None over asset selection | Full |
| Income | Fund distributions, if any | Rent, subject to local rules |
| Liquidity | Locked for the hold period | Saleable, subject to the permit |
| Ongoing charges | Management and performance fees | IMI-equivalent taxes, maintenance, management |
| Exit | Fund wind-up or secondary sale | Property sale, which may end the permit |
| Currency and market risk | Fund strategy dependent | Greek residential market |

Neither column is safer. A fund diversifies away single-asset risk and hands you no control; a property gives control and concentrates the risk in one building in one market. What the comparison should settle is whether you would hold the position on its investment merits alone. If a EUR 500,000 fund is something you would own anyway, the permit is a benefit on top. If it is only tolerable because of a passport a decade away, the plan is carrying ten years of legislative risk with no hedge, and 2023 and 2026 both showed that risk is real.

## Where Buying Portuguese Property Still Fits

Nowhere in the immigration decision, which is the point most often lost. Since October 2023 a Portuguese purchase has no bearing on any residence route.

It still fits everywhere else. Non-residents completing on or after 1 September 2026 pay a flat 7.5% IMT plus 0.8% stamp duty, set out in [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/), and the regional picture is in [property for sale in Portugal](/property-for-sale/). If you intend to live in the country, [the D7 visa](/move-to-portugal/d7-visa/) reaches the same permit and the same clock without the fund, and the wider route map is in [the Portugal Golden Visa guide](/move-to-portugal/golden-visa/).

## Pros and Cons

Greece, in favour:

- Real estate still qualifies, so the capital buys an asset you control
- EUR 400,000 outside the premium zones, below Portugal's fund
- No minimum stay at all
- EUR 250,000 tier for conversions and listed restorations

Greece, against:

- 120 square metre floor pushes you into lower-yielding stock
- Naturalisation needs 183 days a year for seven years, which the visa does not build
- B1 Greek is a materially higher language bar than A2
- Selling the qualifying property can end the permit

Portugal, in favour:

- Seven days a year maintains legal residence, and that residence counts
- The only one of the three with a citizenship route for a non-resident investor
- A regulated fund removes single-asset and single-building risk

Portugal, against:

- Ten years for most non-EU nationals, doubled in May 2026
- AIMA takes 18 to 24 months before the clock even starts
- EUR 500,000 illiquid for a horizon that just lengthened
- No property route, directly or through real estate funds

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Any comparison that still lists Spain.** The programme closed on 3 April 2025.
- **A Greek five-year or seven-year passport claim.** Seven years of physical residence, not seven years of permit.
- **Portuguese property sold as a residency route.** Closed in October 2023, funds included.
- **A Greek EUR 400,000 yield model on a small apartment.** The 120 square metre rule forbids it.
- **A Portuguese timeline that omits AIMA.** Eighteen to twenty-four months sit in front of the ten years, not inside them.

Before committing capital:

- Confirm which naturalisation group your nationality falls in
- Decide whether permanent residency, not citizenship, already meets the objective
- Price the position as an investment you would hold without the permit
- Confirm the family composition rules for your specific dependants
- Model what happens if the rules change again, because they have twice in three years

## Who Each One Suits

**The investor who wants an asset and no calendar obligation.** Greece, at EUR 400,000 outside the premium zones, with the 120 square metre rule priced into the yield expectation.

**The investor who wants a passport without relocating.** Portugal, and only Portugal of these three, at ten years and EUR 500,000. See [Portuguese citizenship by investment](/move-to-portugal/citizenship-by-investment/) before committing.

**The household that intends to move to Portugal.** Neither golden visa. [The D7](/move-to-portugal/d7-visa/) or the D8 reaches the same destination for a fraction of the capital.

**The buyer who just wants a Portuguese home.** Buy it on its own merits. It has not been an immigration instrument since 2023, and pretending otherwise is how people overpay.
