Quick Answer: Castilho 203 is a completed 19-apartment building by Vanguard Properties on Rua Castilho in Avenidas Novas, Lisbon, with asking prices from about €1.2 million. Because the building is finished and occupied, a purchase here is a resale transaction rather than an off-plan one: no bank guarantee applies, the condominium accounts already exist, and the energy certificate can be read before you offer.

Castilho 203 is the easiest kind of Portuguese purchase to diligence and one of the hardest to price. Everything a buyer normally has to take on trust in an off-plan scheme is already visible here: the finishes exist, the neighbours are in place, the lift works or it does not, and two years of condominium accounts are sitting with the administrator. What replaces construction risk is liquidity risk, because Lisbon's €1 million-plus resale band is thin and slow by design.

## Why does a completed building change the whole checklist?

A completed building removes the single largest legal protection in Portuguese off-plan buying, and removes the need for it at the same time. Decreto-Lei 67/2003 requires a bank guarantee on money paid before the licença de utilização exists; where that licence has already been issued, there is nothing to guarantee and no longstop date to negotiate.

| Off-plan concern | Status at a completed building |
|---|---|
| DL 67/2003 guarantee on deposits | Not applicable, licence already issued |
| Longstop completion date | Not applicable |
| Render versus as-built plan | Inspect the actual apartment |
| Condominium budget estimate | Two years of real accounts exist |
| Energy certificate | Issued, valid 10 years |
| Snagging liability | Runs from the original handover, not your purchase |

The last row is the one buyers miss. Structural defect liability under Article 1225 of the Civil Code runs for five years from the original completion, not from your escritura, so a building delivered in 2023 has already spent part of that window. Ask the administrator for the record of defect claims made against the promoter before you rely on the warranty as a comfort.

## What does the condominium actually cost at this level?

Ultra-premium Lisbon buildings carry service charges that a yield model has to take seriously, because concierge cover, lift maintenance, common-area climate control and a reserve fund do not scale down with occupancy. In a 19-unit building the fixed costs are divided by nineteen rather than by two hundred, which is the arithmetic that makes small luxury schemes expensive to run.

Request three documents before offering, and read them in this order:

- **Two years of condominium accounts.** Look for the reserve fund balance, not just the annual charge. A thin reserve means the next façade or lift job arrives as a special assessment.
- **Minutes of the last two general meetings.** Approved works bind the incoming owner. A vote passed before your escritura is your bill after it.
- **The condominium regulation.** This is where any restriction on short-term letting lives, and in a building of this class the restriction is often stricter than the municipal rules.

## How deep is the resale market above €1 million?

Portugal recorded 169,812 residential transactions in 2025 worth €41.2 billion, but the €1 million-plus segment is a small fraction of that volume and behaves differently from the market the headline describes. Non-resident purchases fell 13.3% to 8,471 in the same year, and the prime Lisbon band leans disproportionately on exactly that buyer pool.

The practical consequence is exit timing. An apartment at this price finds its buyer through a narrow international channel rather than through local demand, so a seller who needs a defined completion date has less room than the national price index suggests. Underwrite a holding period measured in years and treat a fast sale as an upside, not an assumption.

## What tax does a non-resident actually pay here?

At a €1.2 million purchase price, a non-resident completing after 1 September 2026 pays flat 7.5% IMT under DL 97/2026, which is €90,000, plus 0.8% stamp duty at €9,600 and roughly 2% to 3% in legal, notary and registry work. Total cash to close lands near €110,000 to €125,000 above the price.

| Line | On €1.2M | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IMT, non-resident flat rate | €90,000 | Refundable if you become tax resident within 24 months |
| Stamp duty | €9,600 | Not refundable |
| Legal, notary, registry | €24,000 to €36,000 | 2% to 3% |
| AIMI, annual | Depends on VPT | 1% above €1M of taxable value per owner |

AIMI is the line that separates this building from the rest of the site's coverage. Above €1 million of taxable value per owner the rate is 1%, and joint ownership splits the allowance so a couple holding equally each gets their own €600,000 threshold. Check the VPT on the caderneta before completing, because the taxable value and the asking price are different numbers and only one of them drives the annual bill. Work the figures through the [AIMI guide](/guides/aimi-wealth-tax-portugal-property/) and the [buying cost calculator](/guides/portugal-buying-costs-calculator-examples/).

## How does a completed building price against the off-plan pipeline?

A finished apartment and an off-plan one at the same address are not the same product, and the price gap between them is the market's estimate of construction risk plus the time value of waiting. In Lisbon that gap typically runs 10% to 20% in favour of off-plan at reservation, which is the discount a buyer accepts for taking on delivery risk, a longstop date and two or three years without occupancy or income.

Castilho 203 is useful to an off-plan buyer precisely because it is finished. It shows what this developer's specification actually looks like once built rather than rendered, what the real service charges settled at, and how the fractions were finally registered. A buyer weighing [Tomás Ribeiro 79](/projects/tomas-ribeiro-79/) or [Terraços do Monte](/projects/terracos-do-monte/) can walk a completed building by the same promoter before committing money to one that does not exist yet, which is worth more than any brochure.

## Who is this building actually for?

Castilho 203 suits a buyer who wants a finished prime Lisbon apartment with no construction exposure and can hold it through a slow resale market. It suits poorly an investor optimising for yield, because gross returns in the Avenidas Novas €1M-plus band sit well below the 4% to 6% available in [Porto](/guides/porto-property-investment-guide/) or mainstream Lisbon stock, and the AIMI charge subtracts from net.

## What to verify before you offer

1. Certidão permanente showing clean title and no charges on the fraction
2. Caderneta predial with the current VPT, for the AIMI calculation
3. Two years of condominium accounts and the reserve fund balance
4. Minutes of the last two general meetings for approved or pending works
5. Condominium regulation clauses on letting and on short stays
6. Record of defect claims against the promoter within the five-year window
7. Three escritura comparables in the parish, obtained by your lawyer
