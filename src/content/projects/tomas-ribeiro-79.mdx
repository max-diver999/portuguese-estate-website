Avenidas Novas is where Lisbon goes to work. Offices, embassies, hospitals and the Gulbenkian sit inside a grid of early twentieth-century avenues, and the rental demand that follows is corporate, year-round and indifferent to the tourist season. That makes Tomás Ribeiro 79 a different proposition from a scheme priced on views or beaches, and it should be underwritten on different assumptions.

## Who actually rents in Avenidas Novas?

The tenant profile here is a relocating professional, a diplomatic posting or a medical secondment, usually on a twelve-month contract and frequently with the employer paying. That demand is less spectacular than short-let headlines and considerably more reliable, because it does not depend on the season, the airline schedule or the licensing map.

| Demand source | What it means for the model |
|---|---|
| Corporate relocations | Twelve-month contracts, employer covenant, low arrears |
| Embassies and consulates | Long postings, furnished preference |
| Hospitals and universities | Steady replacement demand each academic year |
| Domestic professionals | Price-sensitive, sustains the floor |

The practical consequence is that a furnished, parked, lift-served two-bedroom apartment lets faster here than a larger unfurnished one, because the tenant is arriving from abroad with suitcases rather than furniture. Specify the unit accordingly at reservation, while the developer can still change it.

## Should the short-let route be part of the model?

No, not as a base case. Avenidas Novas is not where visitors choose to stay, and Lisbon's Alojamento Local containment framework has in any case removed the assumption that a new registration can simply be obtained. Model long-term letting at the 25% autonomous rate on gross rent and treat anything else as speculative.

That is not a weakness. Long-term letting has lower management cost, no cleaning or turnover overhead, no seasonality and no licensing exposure, and it produces the predictable net figure a mortgage underwriter wants to see. The arithmetic is set out in [gross versus net yield](/guides/gross-vs-net-yield-portugal/) and the tax treatment in [non-resident rental income tax](/guides/non-resident-rental-income-tax-portugal/).

## What protects money paid before completion?

Decreto-Lei 67/2003 requires a bank guarantee or insurance covering every payment made before the licença de utilização is issued, in the amount paid. The protection is standard; the failures are always in the detail.

- **The beneficiary must be you**, named individually, not the marketing brand or an agency.
- **The amount must be cumulative** and reissued at each tranche, covering everything paid to date rather than the latest instalment alone.
- **The issuer must be a bank or insurer**, and you must hold the certificate rather than be told one exists.
- **The seller entity on the guarantee must match** the NIPC on the CPCV, the construction licence and the land registry.
- **The longstop date must be a calendar date** with stated refund mechanics, not a phase description.

Read the [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-property-portugal-guide/) and the [CPCV guide](/guides/cpcv-promissory-contract-portugal/) before transferring anything, and have a lawyer who is not recommended by the seller check the chain.

## What does completion cost, and what comes back?

At €850,000, a non-resident completing after 1 September 2026 pays flat 7.5% IMT under DL 97/2026, which is €63,750, plus €6,800 stamp duty at 0.8% and roughly €17,000 to €25,500 in legal, notary and registry work.

| Line | On €850,000 |
|---|---|
| IMT, non-resident flat rate | €63,750 |
| Stamp duty | €6,800 |
| Legal, notary, registry | €17,000 to €25,500 |
| Cash to close, approximate | €937,500 to €946,000 |

The IMT is refundable where the buyer becomes Portuguese tax resident within 24 months of the purchase. On an off-plan unit that is unusually easy to plan for, because the completion date is known in advance and the residency clock can be started to fit it. A buyer already considering a move should treat that €63,750 as a timing decision rather than a sunk cost, and read the [IMT refund guide](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/).

## What does the exit look like from Avenidas Novas?

Resale in this grid draws on two pools at once, which is unusual in central Lisbon. Owner-occupiers want the schools, the hospitals and the metro; investors want the corporate tenancy. A property that satisfies both sells faster than one aimed at either alone, and specification decides which category a unit falls into: parking, a lift, a second bathroom and a workable second bedroom move a unit into the family pool as well as the rental one.

Capital gains follow ordinary Portuguese rules, with 50% of the net gain taxed at 14.5% to 48% and the acquisition charges deductible. On this purchase the €63,750 IMT and €6,800 stamp duty both reduce the eventual taxable gain, provided the receipts survive the intervening years. Keep the completion file intact from day one; the sequence is set out in the [mais-valias guide](/guides/mais-valias-portugal-capital-gains/) and the seller-side costs in [selling as a non-resident](/guides/sell-property-portugal-non-resident/).

One timing note specific to off-plan. The holding period for the inflation coefficient runs from the escritura, not from the CPCV, so the two or three years spent waiting for delivery do not count toward it. A buyer who sells shortly after handover gets no indexation relief at all.

## Who should buy here, and who should not?

Tomás Ribeiro 79 suits a buyer who wants central Lisbon exposure with a dependable long-term tenant and can wait for delivery. It suits poorly an investor who needs income now, or one whose return assumptions rest on nightly rates.

Before committing, walk the same developer's completed [Castilho 203](/projects/castilho-203/) to see what the specification looks like built, and read the [Vanguard Properties review](/developers/vanguard-properties-portugal/).

## What to verify before paying a deposit

1. DL 67/2003 guarantee certificate naming you, covering cumulative payments
2. Seller NIPC matching the CPCV, the construction licence and the land registry
3. Calendar longstop date with refund mechanics
4. Registered plans compared against the marketing renders
5. Parking and storage deeded to the fraction rather than allocated later
6. Draft condominium budget, including lift and lobby maintenance
7. Whether later phases obstruct light or outlook from your unit
8. Three escritura comparables in the parish, obtained by your own lawyer
