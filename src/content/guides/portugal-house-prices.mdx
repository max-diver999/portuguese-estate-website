Quick Answer: Portuguese house prices vary more inside the country than between Portugal and most buyers' home markets, so a national average describes nothing. The figure that decides your annual bill is not the price at all, it is the rateable value, and on older stock the two are far apart.

Search this question and you get two different answers. Five of the top results are portals showing you listings, and three are data sources showing you a series, one of which is a Federal Reserve database. That split tells you the question is really two questions, and most pages answer neither properly.

This page answers the data one, and then does the thing none of the top results do: connects the price to what the house costs to hold.

## Where the Numbers Come From, and Why They Disagree

| Source | What it measures | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| INE, the national statistics institute | Registered transactions, median value per square metre by municipality | The authoritative series |
| Bank of Portugal and OECD | Derived indices from the same underlying data | Long-run trend, international comparison |
| Property portals | Asking prices on current listings | What sellers hope for, and current supply |
| Valuation reports | One property, one date, one valuer | Financing and tax disputes |

Portal averages and INE medians answer different questions. A portal average tells you what is being asked in that market today. INE tells you what buyers actually paid. In a rising market the first runs ahead of the second, and in a falling one it lags.

Three more reasons published figures disagree on the same market:

- **Asking versus achieved.** The gap widens when supply builds up.
- **Gross versus net floor area.** The same apartment can differ by around a fifth depending on which area the rate is calculated on. Always ask which.
- **Municipal averaging.** A single municipality can contain a riverside parish and an inland one whose values differ by more than two different cities do.

For the current absolute figures and the year-on-year movement, use [our read of the INE market record data](/guides/portugal-property-market-record-2025-ine-data/) rather than a number typed into an article, because this is the figure that goes stale fastest.

## The Four Price Tiers

| Tier | Where | Level, Lisbon = 100 |
|---|---|---|
| Capital belt | Lisbon, Cascais, Estoril, Oeiras | 85 to 100 |
| Second city | Porto, Vila Nova de Gaia, Matosinhos | 55 to 70 |
| Value belt | Setubal, Aveiro, Braga, Coimbra | 40 to 55 |
| Interior and Alentejo | Evora, Castelo Branco, Guarda, inland north | 20 to 35 |

Read the index as relative and take absolutes from the INE series with a date attached.

Two structural facts sit behind it. Lisbon decoupled from the rest of the country after 2015, driven by foreign demand and short-term letting against constrained supply, which is why its ratio to local wages is unusually wide. And the interior is cheap because it is losing population, which is also why its services are thinning.

The Algarve does not fit the tiers, because it is several markets. The central resort strip prices on seasonal demand, the eastern towns on a year-round resident population, and the west on scarcity. Averaging them produces a number that describes no part of it.

## The Number That Actually Decides Your Tax Bill

This is the section the price comparisons never reach.

IMI, the annual municipal property tax, is charged on the **VPT**, the rateable value assigned by Financas, not on what you paid. The VPT is calculated from a formula: base value per square metre, floor area, age, location coefficient and quality coefficient.

| | Market price | Rateable value (VPT) |
|---|---|---|
| Set by | The transaction | A Financas formula |
| Changes when | The market moves | The property is revalued, or works are declared |
| Used for | Your purchase and your capital gain | IMI, AIMI, and stamp duty on transfers |
| Typical relationship | Higher | Often materially lower on older stock |

Three consequences follow.

Two houses that sold for the same price can carry very different IMI bills, because their VPTs differ on age, location coefficient and declared works. That is not an anomaly, it is how the formula works.

A renovation that is properly declared raises the VPT, and therefore the annual IMI, at the same time as it raises the market value and reduces a future capital gain. That trade is worth modelling before the works, not after.

And AIMI, at 0.7% on the share above EUR 600,000 per owner, is also assessed on taxable value rather than market price, so a property that sells above the threshold does not automatically attract it. The detail is in [IMI, the municipal property tax](/guides/imi-property-tax-portugal/) and [AIMI, the wealth surcharge](/guides/aimi-wealth-tax-portugal-property/).

One more wrinkle worth knowing. A VPT set decades ago and never revised can sit far below current values, and a sale does not automatically trigger a revaluation. Buyers sometimes read a low IMI bill as a permanent feature of the property when it is a feature of when the valuation was last done. Declared works, an extension, or a general municipal revision can all move it.

Ask for the current VPT and the IMI actually charged last year on the specific property, in writing, before you make an offer. The municipal band tells you a range; the assessment tells you the number.

## Price Behaves Differently by Property Type

The tier table averages across stock that does not move together.

| Type | How its price behaves | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Modern lift-served apartment | The most liquid, tracks the municipal index closely | Condominium budget and any pending assessment |
| Renovated older apartment | Prices on the quality of the renovation, not the age | Whether works were declared, which sets the VPT |
| Unrenovated older stock | The widest spread, priced on what it needs | Licensing queue and the real cost per square metre of works |
| Villa with land and a pool | Thinner market, slower exit | Running costs rise faster than value |
| New build off plan | Prices to a schedule set by the developer | Completion risk and the gap to comparable resale |

The unrenovated line is where buyers most often misprice. A low headline price on a building needing structural work is not a discount until the works, the obras licence and the licence queue are all costed. On coastal or older masonry stock those three together frequently exceed the apparent saving.

The villa line is the one owners misprice later rather than at purchase. Maintenance, pool servicing, insurance and grounds scale with the property rather than with the rent it earns, and the market for a large house is thinner in both directions.

## What About the Forecast

Every question about prices becomes a question about the future within two sentences, so it is worth stating what can and cannot be said.

What can be said: Portugal has run a structural supply shortfall against foreign and domestic demand since 2015, and government policy has been directed at that shortfall rather than at demand, with the Golden Visa property route removed in 2023 as the clearest example.

What cannot be said: which quarter turns, and by how much. Anyone printing a percentage for next year is producing a scenario, and it should be read as one. Our own view is set out in [the market forecast](/guides/portugal-property-market-forecast-2026-2027/) on exactly that basis.

The practical response is not to forecast but to build the holding period into the decision. Acquisition costs of 9% to 11% plus exit costs mean a short hold needs unusual growth simply to break even, and that arithmetic does not depend on any forecast being right.

## From Price to Budget

A price is not a budget. Here is the rest of it for a non-resident buyer.

| Layer | Items | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Flat 7.5% IMT, 0.8% stamp duty, notary, registry, legal | Roughly 9% to 11% of price |
| Annual | IMI on VPT, AIMI above the threshold, condominium, insurance | Recurring, arrives whether occupied or not |
| Irregular | Maintenance, condominium special assessments | Unbudgeted by almost everyone |
| Exit | Agency commission plus VAT, capital gains, certificates | On the way out |

The acquisition layer is covered in [the cost of buying property in Portugal](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-portugal/), and the annual and irregular layers in [Portugal property running costs](/guides/property-management-portugal-cost/).

The practical effect is that a holding period long enough to absorb roughly nine to eleven percent on the way in, plus the exit costs, is the minimum the arithmetic tolerates. Price growth below that over your holding period is not a gain.

## Pros and Cons of Buying on Today's Numbers

Advantages:

- Prices outside Lisbon and Cascais remain well below comparable northern European and US coastal markets
- The value belt offers city access at roughly half the capital cost
- INE publishes a genuinely usable transaction-based series, which many markets do not
- Older stock frequently carries a VPT well below market price, which keeps annual tax modest

Disadvantages:

- Lisbon has decoupled from local wages, which constrains the tenant pool at the top
- The Algarve strip prices on seasonality that a full-year owner does not capture
- Acquisition costs of 9% to 11% make short holding periods expensive
- A declared renovation raises IMI as well as value
- Interior value comes with a slow exit

## Risks and Red Flags

- **A single national average.** It is describing a country that does not exist. Ask which municipality and which quarter.
- **Portal averages presented as market prices.** They are asking prices, and the gap moves with supply.
- **Rate per square metre with no area basis.** Gross and net differ enough to invalidate the comparison.
- **Assuming IMI follows the purchase price.** It follows the VPT, and the difference is routinely large.
- **Undated figures.** This is the fastest-staling number in the market, and most articles carry no date at all.

Checklist before you make an offer:

- The current VPT and the IMI actually charged last year, in writing
- Whether the advertised area is gross or net
- Comparable registered sales in the same parish, not the municipality average
- The condominium budget and any approved works not yet billed
- Your total acquisition cost modelled at the non-resident rate

## Which Question Are You Actually Asking

**"What will this house cost me?"** Then the price is the smallest part of the answer. Model acquisition, annual and exit costs together, and get the VPT before the offer.

**"Is this a fair price?"** Then you want registered comparables in the same parish, not a municipal median and not a portal average.

**"Is the market going up?"** Then you want the INE index over several years rather than a headline, and you should treat any forecast, including [our own market forecast](/guides/portugal-property-market-forecast-2026-2027/), as a scenario rather than a number.

**"Where do I get the most house?"** Then the value belt, and the comparison is in [the best places to live guide](/move-to-portugal/best-places-to-live/).
