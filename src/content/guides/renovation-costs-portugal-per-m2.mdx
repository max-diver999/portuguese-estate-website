Renovation pricing in Portugal is quoted per square metre, argued per line item, and blown per surprise behind the wall. The market context makes budgeting harder than the arithmetic: 41,592 residential licences were issued in 2025, up 20.1% on the prior year, and the same contractors serving that construction boom price private renovation work with 1–3 month queues in Lisbon, Porto and the Algarve. Three numbers frame every project before a single quote arrives: the €600–2,200/m² band your work depth selects, the 40–60% that materials add over labour, and the 23%-versus-6% VAT question that DL 97/2026 opened until the end of 2029. The cost bands below are split the way empreiteiros actually quote: by work depth first, then by region, then by who supplies the materials, with the licensing and tax layers that most budgets meet too late.

## What does renovation cost per m² in 2026?

Renovation in Portugal costs €600 to €800 per m² for a light refresh, €800 to €1,200 for an intermediate renovation, and €1,200 to €2,200 for a full gut with quality finishes in Lisbon, with interior regions typically 15–25% cheaper across this market and the Algarve tracking Lisbon within about 10%.

| Work depth | What it includes | Lisbon/Porto €/m² | Interior €/m² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh | Paint, floors sanded, bathroom refit | €600–800 | €450–650 |
| Intermediate | Kitchen + bathrooms, electrics upgrade, some walls | €800–1,200 | €650–950 |
| Full gut | Everything to slab: plumbing, wiring, layout | €1,200–2,200 | €950–1,600 |
| Structural | Gut plus structure, roof, façade | €1,600–2,500+ | €1,300–2,000 |

Worked example: an 80 m² Lisbon T2 at intermediate depth prices at €64,000–€96,000 all-in for labour and materials. Add design fees of 5–10% if an architect runs the project, licensing costs where structure is touched, and VAT, 23% standard, or [6% under the DL 97/2026 housing-works window](/guides/portugal-6-percent-vat-housing-works/) when the property qualifies.

Insider tip: contractors quote the band's bottom to win the job and reach its top through extras. The realistic planning number is the band's midpoint plus contingency, 15% for post-1990 buildings, 20–25% for pre-1960 stock, where wiring, damp and structural timber surface only after demolition. Most pre-1960 projects spend their contingency.

## How do labour and materials split the budget?

Labour-only rates in this market run €150–250/m² for cosmetic work, €300–500/m² standard, €500–800/m² turnkey and €800+/m² for design-grade execution, materials then add 40% to 60% on top. Understanding the split is what makes quotes comparable, because empreiteiros mix the two lines differently.

The checklist for comparing quotes:

- **Same scope document to every bidder.** Three itemised quotes minimum; differences then mean price, not interpretation.
- **Materials specified by brand and model**, "sanitários Roca série X", not "louças de qualidade". Vague lines are where 20–30% creep lives.
- **Labour and materials split visible per chapter**, demolition, plumbing, electrics, carpentry, finishes. A single-line total is unauditable.
- **Trabalhos a mais protocol in the contract:** written approval above a threshold (€500 works), unit prices pre-agreed.
- **Payment schedule against milestones,** never calendar dates, 10–20% start, staged completions, 5–10% retention for 60–90 days post-handover.

Programme length prices into the budget too, alongside the [running costs that continue regardless](/guides/property-maintenance-costs-portugal/). A cosmetic refresh runs 4–8 weeks, an intermediate renovation 2–4 months, and a full gut 4–8 months on site, before the 1–3 month contractor queue and any licensing lead time. Every extra month of works on a financed property costs a month of mortgage interest and lost rent; on a €300,000 loan at 3.8% with a €1,400 achievable rent, schedule slip prices at roughly €2,350 a month, which is why milestone-based contracts with delay penalties of €100–€300 per day beat gentleman's agreements.

Client-supplied materials trim 10–15% from the total but shift workmanship risk: most contractors guarantee their own supply chain and exclude yours. On kitchens and bathrooms, where materials run 50–60% of chapter cost, the saving is real; on hidden trades like plumbing and wiring, buying your own is a false economy that voids the guarantee where failure is most expensive.

## Which works need a licence, and what does it add?

Licensing in Portugal splits renovation into three regimes: exempt interior works, comunicação prévia for defined alterations, and full licença de obras where structure, façade or use classification changes, with câmara response times of 30 to 90 days in Lisbon and Porto and longer in high-volume Algarve municipalities. Foreign buyers routinely under-plan this stage.

| Regime | Typical works | Cost | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isenção (exempt) | Interior finishes, kitchens, bathrooms, no structure | €0 | None |
| Comunicação prévia | Layout changes, non-structural walls | €200–€800 municipal fees | 20–60 days |
| Licença de obras | Structure, extensions, façade, roof | €500–€3,000+ fees + technical project | 30–90+ days |

Timing the application matters as much as choosing the regime. Submit the comunicação prévia or licence request the day the technical project is signed off, run contractor tendering in parallel with the câmara's clock rather than after it, and the municipal wait disappears inside the procurement period instead of adding to it. Sequential planning — design, then licence, then tender — is how a four-month project becomes seven.

Red flag: "no licence needed" from a contractor is a cost transfer, not a legal opinion. Unlicensed structural work blocks the updated caderneta, complicates insurance claims, and surfaces in the buyer's due diligence when you eventually sell, regularisation through the câmara afterwards costs multiples of the original fee and can require partial demolition. Regularising an unlicensed terrace closure can cost several times the original works.

Where a licença applies, the technical project needs an architect and often an engineer; their fees run 5–10% of works value. Municipal fees themselves vary widely, Lisbon's tables price a mid-size residential alteration at €600–€1,500, while smaller câmaras charge a third of that, and the taxa is due on approval, not application. The [housing-supply pipeline](/guides/portugal-housing-supply-licensing-2025/) explains why câmara queues lengthened through 2025, private renovation competes with 41,592 licensed new-build projects for the same municipal desks.

## How does the 6% VAT window change the maths?

VAT on qualifying housing construction and rehabilitation contracts drops from 23% to 6% under DL 97/2026, for projects initiated between 25 September 2025 and 31 December 2029, where the property respects the moderate caps of €660,982 sale value or €2,300 monthly rent. On renovation budgets in this market, the swing is the largest single line.

Concrete effect on the worked T2 example: at €80,000 of works, VAT at 23% adds €18,400; at 6% it adds €4,800, a €13,600 saving, enough to fund the entire bathroom chapter. On a €150,000 full gut, the spread reaches €25,500. Eligibility mechanics, the empreitada wording the invoice needs, and the 24-month sale-or-let condition sit in the dedicated [6% VAT guide](/guides/portugal-6-percent-vat-housing-works/).

Worked scenario: a French couple buying a €340,000 Caldas da Rainha townhouse with €90,000 of planned rehabilitation works. The property sits comfortably under the €660,982 moderate-value cap, the works are contracted as a single rehabilitation empreitada rather than loose trades, and the couple will occupy the house as their permanent home within 24 months of the licença de utilização, all three conditions met, so the contractor invoices the full works at 6% and the file saves €15,300 against the 23% the couple had originally budgeted. The saving covered their architect, the licensing fees and most of the kitchen chapter. Structured the other way, materials bought personally, works split across three unrelated tradesmen, occupation left undocumented, the identical project would have paid standard VAT on every line, because the reduced rate attaches to the qualifying empreitada as a whole, not to the property.

One boundary matters: the 6% rate attaches to construction and rehabilitation empreitadas, not to loose purchases. Materials you buy yourself carry standard VAT, one more reason the contractor-supplies model wins under the window.

## How do you run a renovation from abroad?

Remote renovation management is standard practice in this market, roughly half of foreign-owned projects run with the owner abroad, and the working model costs 8–15% of works value in supervision fees while reliably saving more than that in prevented drift. The mechanism is independent eyes on site, paid by you and answerable to nobody else.

The remote structure that works, as a checklist:

- **Fiscalização de obra**, an architect or engineer contracted separately from the builder to inspect weekly and certify milestone completion before each payment releases. Typical cost 3–6% of works; the single highest-value line in a remote file.
- **Milestone payments only.** Money moves on certified completion, demolition done, first fix passed, waterproofing tested, never on calendar dates. A 10–20% start payment is normal; 40% up front is not.
- **Photo and video protocol in the contract:** dated walkthroughs at each milestone, stored where both sides can see them. Disputes shrink when the record is contemporaneous.
- **Retention of 5–10%** held for 60–90 days after handover, released after the snagging list clears.
- **A single decision channel.** One WhatsApp group, one weekly written summary, decisions confirmed in writing, trabalhos a mais approved verbally on a Tuesday site visit are exactly how budgets grow 20–30%.
- **Power of attorney scoped to the works** if licensing signatures are needed, limited to the câmara process, not a general mandate.

Contractor credentials are checkable before any money moves. Works above the small-jobs threshold require the builder to hold an alvará or registration issued by IMPIC, the public construction regulator, and the permit class must cover both the works category and your contract value — a class 1 alvará does not lawfully carry a €200,000 empreitada. Verify the number on IMPIC's public register, then verify insurance: works liability cover and, on structural jobs, the decennial exposure that Article 1225 of the Código Civil creates — five years of contractor liability for structural defects on renovation works, counted from handover. A builder who cannot produce the alvará class and the policy certificate in 48 hours has answered your tender question for free.

Worked comparison: two €85,000 gut renovations in Porto, one owner in Toronto with fiscalização at 4%, one self-managing by video call. The supervised project closed 6% over budget and three weeks late; the self-managed one closed 24% over and four months late, with a €6,800 dispute over bathroom waterproofing that the missing inspection record made unwinnable. Supervision cost €3,400 and returned roughly €15,000 of avoided drift, the best yield line in the whole project.

Payment protection has one more layer worth its paperwork: invoices. Every payment against a fatura with your NIF is simultaneously a consumer-protection record, a works-guarantee anchor and a future tax deduction, while every envelope of cash is none of the three. The 23%-or-6% VAT line tempts owners toward informal deals precisely when the DL 97/2026 window has made the honest route cheaper than it has been in decades, which is the quiet logic of the whole reform: at 6%, the discount for paying under the table stops being worth what it costs.

Insider tip: schedule the fiscalização contract before choosing the builder, and let the supervisor sit in on the quote comparison. Contractors price differently when they know certified inspections gate the payments, first quotes tend to come in tighter on supervised tenders.

## How do renovation invoices cut your tax when you sell?

Renovation faturas are deferred tax assets: improvement works invoiced within the 12 years before a sale are deductible from the mais-valias gain, and at Portugal's 50%-inclusion progressive rates a €50,000 documented renovation typically cuts the eventual bill by €7,000 to €12,000. Foreign buyers throw this money away by paying builders informally.

The paperwork discipline, as a checklist:

- **Every fatura carries your NIF and the property's article number** from the caderneta, AT cross-checks against e-fatura and rejects what does not match.
- **Empreitada contracts name the property** and describe the works; quotes and payment proofs stay in the same file.
- **Cash-without-invoice "discounts" cost more than they save:** a 10% cash discount on €50,000 saves €5,000 today and surrenders €7,000–€12,000 of deduction later, before considering the missing guarantee.
- **Keep the file 12 years minimum**, the [mais-valias worked examples](/guides/mais-valias-portugal-capital-gains/) show the deduction entering the formula line by line.

Worked comparison: two identical Porto T3s sold in 2026, each renovated for roughly €60,000 in 2019. Owner A holds faturas; owner B paid half informally. At assessment, A deducts €60,000 and B defends €28,000, the difference adds about €7,400 to B's tax at a 46% marginal band. The informal discount B negotiated in 2019 was €4,500. Renovation receipts, filed properly, out-earn most negotiation wins in this market.
