Net proceeds are the only number a seller actually receives, and Portuguese listings never mention them: the price on the portal is the gross figure before 4% to 7% of transaction costs and before a capital gains calculation that runs on its own arithmetic. The structure of the stack matters as much as its size. Commission dominates, at 3% to 5% plus 23% VAT it is routinely 10 times every other pre-tax line combined, which makes the mediation contract the highest-leverage document on the selling side. And the stack interacts with tax in a way buyers' costs never do: selling costs are deductible from the mais-valias gain, so a seller facing tax at the top bands effectively buys these services at up to 24% off. The worked stacks below run three price points, then each line in turn, then the arithmetic that turns a gross price into money in an account.

## What does the full stack look like at three price points?

Seller costs before tax typically land between 4% and 7% of the price across this market, and the percentage eases as prices rise because half the lines are flat fees: the same €300 certificate and €250 mortgage release weigh less on €800,000 than on €300,000, while the commission scales exactly with the price.

| Cost line | €300,000 sale | €500,000 sale | €800,000 sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commission 4% | €12,000 | €20,000 | €32,000 |
| VAT on commission, 23% | €2,760 | €4,600 | €7,360 |
| Energy certificate | €250 | €300 | €400 |
| Certidão permanente | €15 | €15 | €15 |
| Mortgage release (distrate) | €250 | €250 | €250 |
| Legal support ≈0.8% | €2,400 | €4,000 | €6,400 |
| **Total before tax** | **€17,675 (5.9%)** | **€29,165 (5.8%)** | **€46,425 (5.8%)** |
| **Net before CGT** | **€282,325** | **€470,835** | **€753,575** |

Remote sellers add a power of attorney at €150 to €500 with consular fees, and non-EU sellers carry fiscal representation at €150 to €500 a year through the tax year of the sale. Neither changes the shape of the table; both belong in the plan.

Capital gains tax then runs on its own base. The €500,000 seller who bought at €340,000 six years ago has a gain calculation that starts from an indexed €360,000, not from the sale price, and the [mais-valias formula](/guides/mais-valias-portugal-capital-gains/) decides the final net far more than any line above.

## Why does commission dominate, and what moves it?

Commission is 70% to 85% of every pre-tax stack in this market, so the mediation contract deserves the negotiation energy most sellers spend on the €15 certidão: the spread between 3% and 5% on a €500,000 sale is €12,300 including VAT, more than every other cost line combined.

What actually moves the rate:

- **Mandate type.** Exclusive mandates price lower, typically 3% to 4%, because the agent's investment is protected; open mandates run 4% to 5% and produce less marketing effort per agent.
- **Price band.** Above roughly €750,000 negotiated rates of 2.5% to 3.5% are common, because the absolute fee stays large.
- **Duration and exit clauses.** A 6-month exclusive with a 30-day termination clause keeps pressure on performance; a 12-month lock without exit invites drift.
- **What the rate includes.** Professional photography, floor plans and portal placement belong inside the fee, itemised in the contract, not sold back separately.
- **Dual representation.** An agent introducing their own buyer earns both sides of the transaction's goodwill; the contract should say what happens to the rate then.

Worked case study: two identical €480,000 Cascais apartments, one on an open mandate at 5%, one exclusive at 3.5% for 6 months with a marketing schedule written into the contract. The open-mandate seller pays €29,520 with VAT; the exclusive seller pays €20,664, a difference of €8,856 for one negotiation conversation held before signing anything. The exclusive seller also received the professional media the contract required, because written deliverables get delivered and verbal ones evaporate.

## What do the certificates and releases really involve?

Certificate costs are small and their timing is not: the energy certificate at €135 to €465 is legally required at listing rather than at deed, the €15 certidão must be current when the buyer's lawyer checks title, and a mortgage release ordered late is the classic cause of a rescheduled escritura in this market.

The line-by-line reality:

- **Energy certificate, €135 to €465, valid 10 years.** An ADENE-registered assessor visits, the certificate number goes into the advertisement, and fines for marketing without one run €250 to €3,740. A certificate from your own purchase may still be valid; check the date before paying twice.
- **Certidão permanente, €15 online.** The buyer's side will pull one anyway; holding a current copy yourself means seeing what they see, including any charge you forgot existed.
- **Distrate, €50 to €290 at the registry.** The bank confirms the payoff figure, issues the release, and the cancellation registers at or immediately after the deed. Start the request 3 to 4 weeks out; bank internal processing is the slow half.
- **Caderneta predial, free from the portal.** Not a cost line but a checklist item: the VPT on it sets the floor for the tax calculation if it exceeds your price.

Red flag: a charge on the certidão that the sale proceeds cannot clear, a second mortgage, a penhora, a usufruct. Every one of these is resolvable, and none is resolvable in the week of the deed. Pull the certidão the day you decide to sell, not the day an offer arrives, and the timeline stays yours rather than the registry's.

## How do selling costs interact with the tax bill?

Deductibility converts selling costs into partially refunded costs for any seller in this market with a taxable gain: commission with its VAT, the certificate, mediation and legal fees all reduce the net gain before the 50% inclusion, so a seller taxed at the 48% top band recovers 24 cents of every deductible euro.

The arithmetic on the €500,000 worked stack, assuming a €120,000 pre-cost gain:

1. Selling costs of €29,165 reduce the taxable gain to €90,835.
2. Half of that, €45,418, enters the progressive scale.
3. At a 35% marginal band, the tax falls by roughly €5,100 versus the same sale with no deductions claimed.
4. Effective cost of the €29,165 stack: about €24,000.

The recovery is automatic only if the paperwork exists: faturas for the commission and legal fees naming the seller, the certificate invoice, the registry receipts. Invoices lost between deed and filing season are the difference between the 5.8% stack and the 4.9% effective one. The filing itself, Anexo G's fields and deadlines, is walked through in the [Modelo 3 guide](/guides/modelo-3-anexo-g-property-sale/), and the exemption routes that can zero the tax entirely, reinvestment into a main home or the 2026-2029 rental reinvestment window, live in the [selling process guide](/guides/sell-property-portugal-non-resident/).

Insider tip: sellers below the tax line still benefit from the invoices. A sale at a loss filed with full documentation creates a loss carried forward 5 years against future Portuguese gains, which turns this year's disappointing exit into a deduction against the next one.

## Which costs belong to the buyer, and where do sellers overpay?

Cost allocation in a Portuguese sale is clean by convention: the buyer pays IMT, stamp duty, notary and registration, roughly 8% to 10% for a non-resident after the flat 7.5% IMT, while the seller pays the stack above, and attempts to shift lines across that boundary are negotiating noise in this market rather than practice.

Seller scenario: a €500,000 Lisbon sale carries roughly €30,000 to €35,000 of direct selling costs at a negotiated 5% commission plus VAT and certificates, before the tax provision — which is why the net-proceeds calculation belongs before the listing price is set, not after the CPCV.

| Line | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| IMT and stamp duty | Yes | No |
| Notary and registration of purchase | Yes | No |
| Agency commission and its VAT | No | Yes |
| Energy certificate | No | Yes |
| Mortgage release on the old loan | No | Yes |
| Each side's legal fees | Own | Own |

Where sellers genuinely overpay is inside their own column. The recurring patterns: paying for a second energy certificate because nobody checked the first one's 10-year validity; accepting 5% on an exclusive mandate that should price at 3.5%; buying add-on marketing packages the mediation fee already covers; and paying premium legal rates for a standard file because the lawyer was engaged in deed week rather than at listing. Each is a checklist item, and together they run €3,000 to €12,000 on a mid-market sale.

Currency conversion is the quiet final line for sellers repatriating proceeds, and it dwarfs most of the visible stack: converting €470,000 to sterling or dollars through a retail bank at a 2% to 3% spread costs €9,400 to €14,100, while a specialist provider at 0.3% to 0.5% takes €1,400 to €2,400 for the same transfer on the same day. The €7,000 to €12,000 difference exceeds the entire legal and certificate spend combined, yet it is decided by default in the week the money lands, when the seller is least inclined to open a new account. Booked at CPCV instead, the transfer can also be forward-contracted, fixing the rate for the 30 to 90 days to completion and removing the exchange-rate swing from the net-proceeds calculation entirely. The mechanics and provider due diligence sit in the [currency transfer guide](/guides/currency-transfer-portugal-property/).

## How does the stack shift for special cases?

Special-case sales bend the standard stack in predictable directions: off-plan assignments compress it, rural and unusual properties stretch it, and encumbered or inherited files add lines the standard table never shows. Across this market the swing is real money — the extras below range from €300 to €5,000 per line before time costs are counted.

- **Off-plan assignment before the deed** replaces the full stack with the cession's own economics: no energy certificate, no distrate, but developer consent fees that run €1,000 to €5,000 and legal work on the assignment contract. Commission still applies where an agent sources the assignee.
- **Rural and atypical property** stretches exposure time and marketing cost: the buyer pool is thinner, 9 to 18 months on market is normal, and sellers fund photography, drone work and sometimes access improvements that urban sales never need, €500 to €3,000 of lines the table omits.
- **Inherited property** adds the succession's own paperwork upstream, habilitação de herdeiros and registration at €300 to €800, and every heir signs or grants powers, multiplying the POA line by the number of signatures abroad.
- **Encumbered files**, a second charge, a penhora, an old usufruct, add resolution costs and weeks; each is cheaper solved before listing than negotiated against a buyer's deadline.
- **Tenanted sales** carry the lease to the buyer with legal notification duties, and the price reflects the tenancy: the discount for selling occupied routinely exceeds every fee in the stack combined, which makes vacancy timing a selling cost in all but name.

The common thread is that complexity prices in time as much as money, and time is the one line the seller controls before listing: a file cleaned in the quiet month before the mediation contract costs hundreds, and the same file cleaned under a CPCV deadline costs thousands, the pattern every section of this stack keeps repeating.

## How should a seller sequence the spending?

Sequencing the stack is free and prevents the 2 expensive failure modes, a stalled deed and an unclaimed deduction worth €3,000 to €12,000: documents move early, the commission is negotiated before any signature, and every invoice survives to the following spring's filing season. In this market the checklist runs in 6 steps.

1. **At the decision to sell**: pull the certidão and caderneta, check the energy certificate's validity, request the bank's payoff figure. Total spend: €15.
2. **Before the mediation contract**: negotiate rate, mandate type, duration and deliverables in writing. Spend: nothing; saving: frequently €5,000 to €12,000.
3. **At listing**: commission the certificate if needed, €135 to €465, engage the lawyer at 0.5% to 1% so the CPCV is drafted on your paper, not the buyer's.
4. **At CPCV**: distrate request to the bank; power of attorney if completing remotely.
5. **At deed**: releases register, commission and VAT invoice against the mediation contract, all receipts into one folder.
6. **The following spring**: file with every invoice attached to the calculation, converting the stack into its deductible, cheaper self.

Sellers comparing this stack with what they paid to get in will notice the asymmetry: [buying costs](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-portugal/) are tax-heavy and fixed, selling costs are service-heavy and negotiable. The buying side rewards timing; the selling side rewards paperwork, and both reward starting the file before the counterparty forces the pace.
