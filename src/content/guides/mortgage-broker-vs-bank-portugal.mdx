Broker or bank is the first financing decision a foreign buyer makes and the least informed, because the Portuguese mortgage market is opaque from abroad: around 18 retail lenders with different appetites for non-resident business, pricing that moves with insurance bundles rather than headline rates, and an effort-rate test that quietly rejects files a home-country lender would approve. The broker's product is not the loan itself, no intermediary changes what a bank will lend, but the routing: knowing which 2 or 3 of the 18 want your profile this quarter, at 60% to 70% LTV and a spread near 1.2%. That knowledge is worth the most on exactly the files that get declined elsewhere, and worth the least on a salaried EU resident's clean application, which any major bank prices the same way. The decision method below sorts files into those two groups before a single document is sent.

## What does each route actually offer?

Route comparison starts from one fact: brokers and banks sell access to the same 18 or so retail lenders, so the question is never who has better loans but who runs your file better — and across this market that typically splits by file: a standard one clears direct in 6 to 10 weeks, a complicated one needs routing.

| Factor | Regulated broker | Direct to bank |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to borrower | Usually €0, lender-paid commission | €0 |
| Banks reached | Several from one file | One per application |
| Non-standard files | Routed to lenders with appetite | Trial and error per bank |
| Negotiation | Knows current discounts and bundles | Branch discretion only |
| Speed on clean files | No inherent advantage | Equal, sometimes faster |
| Conduct rules | Banco de Portugal register and supervision | Bank's own conduct framework |
| Conflict risk | Commission may vary by lender | None between banks |

Red flag: an introducer missing from the register. The registration check is non-negotiable. Intermediários de crédito are licensed and listed publicly by the Banco de Portugal, and the licence number belongs in the first email. An introducer without one sits outside the disclosure duties, the complaints route and the professional indemnity that the regulated channel carries, whatever the website looks like.

## When does a broker earn their place?

Broker value concentrates in non-standard files, which for Portuguese lenders means most foreign files across this market: self-employment, bonus-heavy or multi-currency income, non-EU residence, age near the loan's end date, or any income the bank haircuts by 10% to 30% before running its effort-rate test at roughly 35% to 40%.

The checklist of profiles where the multi-bank route typically wins:

- **Self-employed and company directors**, where each bank counts income differently and two declines cost a quarter of a year.
- **Multi-currency households**, salary in dollars, bonus in sterling, rent in euros, because banks discount non-euro income by different percentages.
- **Non-EU residents**, including British buyers post-Brexit, where only a subset of lenders is active at any moment.
- **Tight timelines against a CPCV**, where sending one complete file to three matched lenders in parallel beats sequential branch applications by weeks.
- **Older borrowers**, since maximum age at term end varies by bank from 70 to 80 and mis-targeting wastes the application.

Worked case study: a self-employed American designer buying at €450,000 needs 65% LTV, which means a €292,500 loan against €157,500 of equity plus roughly €40,000 of purchase costs. Bank one counts only the most recent year's drawings, computes an effort rate of 46% and declines. Bank two averages 3 years of accounts, lands the same borrower at 34% and approves at Euribor plus 1.4% over 25 years. Nothing about the applicant changed between the two answers; only the routing did, and each sequential attempt costs 4 to 6 weeks of queue and paperwork. Both applications used the same passport, the same deposit and the same 3 years of accounts; the only variable was which bank's income policy read them. A broker who knew both banks' policies sent the file to the second lender first, and the wasted month and a half never happened.

Routing knowledge of that kind is the entire product, and it is worth the most on precisely the files that branches decline.

## When is going direct the better call?

Direct applications win on clean files, and clean means specific things to a Portuguese credit committee in this market: employed income in euros or a strong stable currency, 2 years of tax returns, an effort rate comfortably inside 35% after the stress test, and equity of 30% to 40% of the price ready to evidence.

A salaried German engineer buying at €350,000 with €150,000 of equity fits every lender's box, so the broker's routing adds little: any of the major retail banks prices the file, and the applicant's own two or three parallel applications reproduce the comparison a broker would run. Direct also keeps one genuine advantage, the relationship: the account, salary domiciliation and insurance conversations that later fund the discounts happen with the bank that will hold them.

Running direct properly is a process, not a form:

1. **Pick 3 banks with visible non-resident activity** rather than the nearest branded branch.
2. **Send identical complete files the same week**, so offers arrive comparably and inside the same rate environment.
3. **Ask each for the FINE document**, the standardised European fact sheet, which forces the all-in cost including insurance onto paper.
4. **Negotiate the spread with the FINE sheets in hand**, since branch discretion moves 0.2 to 0.4 points when a competing offer is visible.
5. **Only then fix the valuation**, because each bank charges €200 to €400 for its own and they do not transfer.

Red flag in both routes: any suggestion to sign the CPCV before pre-approval without a financing condition. The deposit at risk is 10% to 20% of the price, and the [purchase timeline](/guides/buy-property-portugal-foreigner/) accommodates a condition clause far more cheaply than a forfeited sinal.

## What numbers should you expect either way?

Lending numbers are set by this market rather than the channel, and in 2026 they cluster tightly: 60% to 70% loan-to-value for non-residents against the lower of price and valuation, spreads of roughly 1.0% to 1.5% over Euribor, mixed and fixed products from the high 3s, and terms running to age 70 or beyond by bank.

| Parameter | Typical non-resident position |
|---|---|
| LTV | 60–70% of the lower of price and valuation |
| Variable pricing | Euribor 6M or 12M + 1.0–1.5% spread |
| Fixed and mixed | High-3% to mid-4% depending on period |
| Effort rate ceiling | ≈35–40% of net income, stress-tested above the offered rate |
| Maximum age at term end | 70–80, varies by lender |
| Valuation fee | €200–400 per bank |
| Arrangement and processing | €250–600, negotiable |
| Stamp duty on the loan | 0.6% on credit over 5 years |

Two lines on that table belong on every buyer's checklist because both are chronically underweighted. The stress test means approval is computed at a rate above the one offered, so a file that fits at the offered rate can still fail the test that matters. And the valuation, not the price, caps the loan: a €400,000 purchase valued at €380,000 lends 70% of €380,000, and the €14,000 gap lands on the buyer's equity at exactly the moment it is least liquid. The full rate landscape sits in the [2026 mortgage rates guide](/guides/portugal-mortgage-rates-foreigners-2026/), and the buyer-side process in the [non-resident mortgage guide](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-portugal/).

## How do insurance bundles change the real price?

Insurance bundling is where Portuguese mortgage pricing actually happens in this market: banks trade spread discounts worth perhaps €600 to €1,800 a year on a €300,000 loan for life cover, building insurance, salary domiciliation and card usage, and the bundle's annual cost frequently exceeds the discount it buys, especially past age 50.

The comparison method that keeps the arithmetic honest:

- **Price the loan both ways in writing**: bundled spread with the bank's policies, and clean spread with external insurance quotes attached.
- **Compare total annual cost**, instalment plus premiums, not the spread alone; the FINE sheet's APRC line does this arithmetic for you.
- **Re-quote life cover externally from age 50**, where bank group policies price steeply: annual premiums of €800 to €2,500 on a €300,000 loan are common, against external quotes frequently 30% to 50% lower.
- **Check the exit terms**: whether dropping a bundled policy later resets the spread, because most contracts say it does.

Insider tip: banks reprice bundles annually but the contract's spread ladder is fixed at signature, so the moment of maximum negotiating power is before the deed and never after. A borrower who arrives with an external life quote in hand typically keeps the discount and the cheaper policy; the [hidden-cost patterns](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-portugal/) around bank products repeat here precisely because nobody reads the ladder until year two.

## How does the timeline really run?

Mortgage timelines for non-residents typically run 6 to 10 weeks from complete file to final approval, and the word complete carries most of the variance: document collection across 2 countries routinely consumes 3 to 4 weeks before any bank clock starts, which is why for foreign buyers the collection belongs before the property choice, not after it.

Buyer scenario: a Dublin buyer signs a CPCV with a 90-day deed date and starts document collection the same week, reaching final approval in week 8 with 2 weeks of margin. The identical file started in week 4 meets the notary date with an extension request instead of a mortgage.

The sequence with realistic durations, worth pinning to the wall as a checklist:

1. **Document collection**, 2 to 4 weeks: payslips or accounts, 2 years of tax returns, statements, credit report, NIF, translations where asked.
2. **Pre-approval in principle**, 1 to 2 weeks from a complete file, and the milestone a CPCV should wait for.
3. **Valuation**, 1 to 2 weeks: €200 to €400, ordered by the bank, capping the loan at 60% to 70% of its result.
4. **Final credit decision and FINE issuance**, 1 to 3 weeks, followed by the statutory reflection period.
5. **Deed scheduling**, 1 to 2 weeks, coordinating notary, bank and any [currency transfer](/guides/currency-transfer-portugal-property/) for the equity.

The broker's timeline advantage, where it exists, sits in steps one and two: a routed file reaches a lender that wants it and arrives complete on the first pass. The direct applicant controls the same variance by treating the document list as the project and the property search as the reward for finishing it. Either way, completion costs beyond the loan, IMT, stamp duty on both purchase and loan, notary and registration, sit in the [full cost stack](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-portugal/) and none of them wait for financing.

## What goes wrong on either route, and how do you prevent it?

Failure patterns in Portuguese mortgage files repeat with remarkable discipline, and most cost between €500 and the full 10% to 20% deposit, which makes prevention the cheapest work in the entire purchase. The table below is the loss ledger this market keeps rewriting: each row is a preventable failure with a named fix.

| Failure | Typical cost | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| CPCV signed before pre-approval, no financing clause | Sinal at risk: €40,000 on a €400,000 deal at 10% | Condition clause or pre-approval first |
| Valuation below price | Equity gap of €5,000–€20,000 at completion | Price the gap into reserves; negotiate on the valuation |
| Effort rate fails at the stressed rate | Weeks lost, application fees burned | Compute the stressed ratio yourself before applying |
| Bundle repricing in year 2 | €400–€1,500 a year quietly added | Read the spread ladder before the deed |
| Documents expiring mid-process | 2–4 week resets | Refresh statements the week of submission |

The deposit row dominates the table, and it is the one both routes can prevent for free. A financing condition in the CPCV, or the discipline of holding signature until pre-approval, converts the worst outcome from a five-figure loss into a delay. Portuguese Estate treats that single clause as the difference between a financed purchase plan and a gamble with the seller holding the stakes.

One more pattern belongs to the broker route specifically: commission structures can differ by lender, which is a mild steering incentive the regulated disclosure regime exists to surface. Ask the intermediary in writing which lenders pay them and whether the rate differs, then judge the shortlist knowing the answer. A good broker answers in one sentence; hesitation is itself information, exactly as it is with an administrator who cannot produce condominium accounts.
