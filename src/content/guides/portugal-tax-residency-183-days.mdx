Residency questions arrive disguised as counting questions and turn out to be evidence questions. The statute is short: Article 16 of the CIRS makes you resident past 183 days of presence in any 12-month window, with any overnight stay counting the day, or immediately under the habitual-home limb if you keep a dwelling in conditions that suggest it is your real base. The 183 half is arithmetic anyone can manage with a calendar. The habitual-home half is a judgment built from utilities, furniture, family and patterns, and it is the limb that catches owners who believed they were safely non-resident at 120 days a year. Both halves matter twice over for property buyers, because residency does not only change what Portugal taxes; inside 24 months of a purchase it refunds the 7.5% non-resident IMT, which converts the residency date into one of the largest single financial levers in the whole relocation.

## What exactly does the law say?

Article 16 of the CIRS sets two independent routes into residency, and meeting either one is enough: presence above 183 days in any 12-month period touching the tax year, or holding a dwelling on any day in conditions suggesting habitual residence — a test that catches foreign buyers earlier than most expect.

| Element | Rule | Common misreading |
|---|---|---|
| Day threshold | More than 183 days | "183 exactly is safe": it is the boundary, not a target |
| Counting window | Any 12 months, rolling | "Per calendar year": an Oct–May stay can trigger it |
| What counts as a day | Any day with an overnight stay | "Part-days never count": the night is what counts |
| Habitual-home limb | Applies from a single day | "Only days matter": the home alone can be enough |
| Start of residency | The day conditions are met | "Resident for the whole year": arrival years split |

The rolling window deserves the emphasis it rarely gets. A stay running from early October to late May crosses two calendar years with neither showing 183 days, yet the 12-month window spanning the stay shows more than 200. Buyers who plan around 31 December are managing the wrong boundary.

Presence counting follows the overnight: a Friday-to-Monday visit contributes its nights, a same-day return trip contributes nothing, and a pattern of long weekends accumulates faster than intuition suggests, at roughly 12 days a month for a fortnightly habit.

## How does the habitual-home limb actually work?

Habitual residence is a picture rather than a number: the law asks whether the dwelling is held in conditions suggesting the intention to keep and occupy it as your habitual home, and in this market that picture decides more close files than the 183 days themselves, assembled from 5 classes of objective signs below.

Signs that build the picture, and their practical weight:

- **Year-round availability.** A furnished home never let out and always ready reads differently from a flat rented to tenants 10 months a year.
- **Consumption patterns.** Electricity and water tracking occupancy across all seasons is the classic indicator; a meter that sleeps from October to May argues the other way.
- **Family anchoring.** A spouse living there or children enrolled in a local school is close to decisive on its own.
- **Registered use.** Declaring the address as morada fiscal, receiving correspondence, holding subscriptions there.
- **Economic centre.** Where the car is registered, where the doctor is, where the gym membership bills.

Worked case study: a Dutch consultant spends 130 days a year in her Algarve villa, comfortably under 183. The villa is furnished, never rented, her car is garaged there, utilities run in her name across all 12 months and her partner lives there 9 months a year. The day-count limb never triggers; the habitual-home limb plausibly does, and if AT raises the question the burden of showing that the centre of life is elsewhere falls on her, against her own records. AT would read the utilities, the car and the partner as 3 of the 5 sign classes fully lit, with no counter-evidence on file. Her morada fiscal still points to Utrecht, but registered use is 1 class out of 5, and the other 4 outvote it on any honest reading of Article 16.

The same 130 days spent across hotels and short lets would build no picture at all, and the same villa let to tenants for 8 months a year would argue against habitual use rather than for it. Ownership plus patterns, not days alone, is what the second limb reads, which is why two owners with identical passports and identical day counts can land on opposite sides of the line.

## What changes on the day residency starts?

Residency consequences begin on the exact day the conditions are met, not on 1 January: Portugal's split-year rule divides the arrival year into a non-resident period, taxed only on Portuguese-source income, and a resident period, taxed on worldwide income at progressive rates of 14.5% to 48% — a split that changes real money for foreign buyers arriving mid-year.

The immediate obligations and their deadlines:

1. **Register the change of address with AT within 60 days**, dating it to the real start; the date drives everything downstream.
2. **Worldwide income enters the Portuguese net from that date**: foreign salary, dividends, rents and gains, allocated between countries by treaties like the [UK convention](/guides/uk-portugal-double-taxation-treaty/).
3. **Regime elections start their clocks**: the [IFICI application](/guides/ifici-portugal-nhr-2-tax-regime/) is due by 15 January of the following year, and a missed deadline burns a year of the 20% rate.
4. **The first resident Modelo 3** files the next spring, between 1 April and 30 June, covering the resident part of the split year.
5. **Non-EU citizens' fiscal representation lapses** in favour of ordinary resident status, one obligation that ends rather than begins.

Sequencing income around the start date is legitimate and valuable: a bonus paid or a gain realised the week before residency begins stays outside the Portuguese net, and the same event a week later lands inside it at up to 48%. The planning question is dated evidence, which is also why backdating an address change to a convenient date is the error that unravels: the registered date must match the pattern the records show.

## How does the residency date interact with a property purchase?

Purchase interaction is where the 183-day question turns into money, because DL 97/2026 refunds the flat 7.5% non-resident IMT to buyers who become Portuguese tax resident within 24 months of completion: €30,000 back on a €400,000 purchase, €37,500 on €500,000, against a residency step many foreign buyers in this market intended anyway.

The clean sequence for a buyer planning both:

- **Complete the purchase as a non-resident**, paying the 7.5% IMT that the refund mechanism anticipates.
- **Establish residency inside the 24-month window**, by days or by habitual home, with the address registration dated correctly.
- **Claim the refund with the evidence assembled**: the residency registration, presence records and the [refund procedure's documents](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/).
- **Hold the residency genuinely.** A registration reversed shortly after the refund invites reassessment of the whole arrangement.

Red flag: treating the refund as a paperwork trick rather than a consequence of real relocation. The mechanism tests substance, and the same evidence that supports the refund, consumption, presence, life patterns, is exactly what AT can read against a claim that does not match reality. A genuine mover collects the refund almost incidentally; a paper mover assembles a contradiction with their own records.

For buyers still deciding whether residency fits their plans at all, the [residency routes overview](/guides/portugal-residency-options-without-golden-visa/) and the [D7's passive-income path](/guides/portugal-d7-visa-property/) sit upstream of this question: immigration status and tax residency are separate systems, and the D7 or D8 visa neither creates nor prevents tax residency by itself.

## How does the test play out for typical owner profiles?

Profile patterns repeat because the 2 limbs interact with immigration status and property use in predictable ways: mapping your situation onto the 5 rows below typically answers the residency question in minutes, and in this market the same rows cover most owners' first 2 years of ownership, whichever visa route brought them in.

Insider tip: decide the target residency answer before completion, not after. The IMT refund under DL 97/2026 needs residency within 24 months, IFICI registration closes on 15 January of the year after arrival, and both deadlines are missed most often by owners who drifted into residency rather than choosing the date.

Buyer scenario: a British couple completing in June spends 150 days in Portugal across the following 12 months, keeps the UK home let, and registers the Portuguese address as morada fiscal. The habitual-home limb is now arguable either way, which is exactly when the evidence file below decides the outcome.

| Profile | Day-count exposure | Habitual-home exposure | Usual outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU citizen, holiday flat, 6 weeks a year | Minimal at ~42 days | Low: seasonal use, often let | Non-resident |
| Non-EU owner at the 90/180 ceiling | Capped near 180 days by Schengen | Rises if the home is the base | Boundary case, evidence decides |
| Remote worker wintering 5 months | High in rolling windows | High with year-round utilities | Frequently resident without noticing |
| Retired couple relocating on a D7 | Above 183 by design | Immediate | Resident, by intention |
| Owner letting the property 10 months | Low | Low: tenants occupy the home | Non-resident |

The remote-worker row is the one that generates disputes. Five months every winter never crosses 183 in a calendar year, crosses it easily in a rolling window when a spring visit extends the stay, and meanwhile builds exactly the consumption curve the habitual-home limb reads. That profile should either embrace residency, often profitably given the [IMT refund](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/) and the IFICI window, or manage the pattern deliberately: shorter blocks, documented exits, the property genuinely closed between stays.

Non-EU owners carry a structural protection they rarely notice: the Schengen 90/180 ceiling keeps a rule-abiding visitor near 180 days a year at maximum, just under the tax threshold. The protection is real and narrow, because it caps only the day-count limb; a Silver Coast house held furnished and central to life can still make its owner resident with 120 days on the passport stamps.

## What evidence decides close cases?

Evidence questions decide residency disputes because the facts are rarely in the state's hands: AT assesses from indicators inside its window of 4 years, the taxpayer proves the pattern, and in this market the file that settles a case in 1 afternoon costs about 12 hours a year to keep against 100+ to reconstruct.

| Evidence class | What it shows | Weight in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Flight and travel records | Physical presence days | High, easy to obtain |
| Card and bank transactions | Where daily life happened | High, granular |
| Utility consumption curves | Occupancy of the dwelling | High for the habitual limb |
| Lease registrations and school records | Anchoring of the household | Close to decisive |
| Tolls, transport passes, phone data | Movement patterns | Corroborative |
| Address registrations | The declared position | Necessary, never sufficient alone |

Insider tip: run the count yourself before anyone else does. A 1-page spreadsheet of arrival and departure dates, kept current, answers the 183-day half in seconds and flags the year a rolling window quietly crosses the line. Pair it with an annual download of flight confirmations and the file that decides most disputes already exists, at a cost of 1 hour a year. The expensive version is reconstructing 3 years of movements from booking emails after an assessment has issued.

Consumption records deserve the same discipline on the habitual-home limb. Utility portals keep 12 to 24 months of history and then truncate it, so a yearly export of the electricity and water curves preserves exactly the evidence that shows a house asleep for 7 months, which is the strongest single answer to a habitual-residence question. The pattern holds across every class in the table: the evidence that wins is boring, dated and collected before anyone asked for it, and almost all of it is free.

Departure runs on the same logic in reverse: leaving Portugal ends residency when the conditions cease and the exit is registered with a new foreign address, splitting the departure year exactly as arrival split its year. An owner who leaves without deregistering stays resident on paper, files nothing, and meets the consequences years later with interest attached; the [selling-side guide](/guides/sell-property-portugal-non-resident/) covers how that status interacts with an eventual exit from the property itself.
