Selling a Portuguese property from abroad is a document game first and a tax game second. The transaction itself, CPCV, deposit, escritura, runs on rails once the paperwork is complete, and a clean file closes in 30 to 90 days from accepted offer. What separates a 4-month sale from a 9-month one is almost always a missing licença de utilização, an unregistered extension, or a seller who discovers the capital gains rules after pricing the property instead of before. Portugal recorded 169,812 residential sales in 2025 with prices up 17.6%, so the demand side rarely causes the delay; the paperwork side does. The sequence below follows where non-resident sales in Lisbon, Porto and the Algarve actually stall, costs first, documents second, then contract, tax and the 2026 exemption that can zero the bill.

## What does selling a property in Portugal cost a non-resident?

Selling a Portuguese property as a non-resident typically costs between 5% and 8% of the sale price before capital gains tax. Agency commission runs 3% to 5% plus 23% VAT, the energy certificate costs €135 to €465 by typology, and mortgage cancellation adds €50 to €290, consistent across this market.

| Cost item | Typical range | Paid to | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency commission | 3–5% + 23% VAT | Estate agent | At escritura |
| Energy certificate (CE) | €135–€465 | ADENE-registered assessor | Before listing |
| Certidão permanente | €15 | Registo Predial (online) | Before CPCV |
| Mortgage distrate + cancellation | €50–€290 | Bank + registry | At/after escritura |
| Lawyer (optional but advised) | 0.5–1% of price | Independent lawyer | Through the sale |
| Power of attorney (if remote) | €150–€500 | Notary/consulate | Before CPCV |
| Fiscal representative (non-EU) | €150–€500/year | Accountant | Ongoing |

A worked example makes the stack concrete. On a €400,000 Lisbon apartment sold at 4% commission: €16,000 commission plus €3,680 VAT, €300 energy certificate, €15 certidão, €800 lawyer, €250 mortgage cancellation, €21,045, or 5.3% of the price. Capital gains tax then applies separately on the net gain, and every euro in this table except the fiscal representative is deductible against that gain.

Two costs surprise sellers most often. First, VAT on commission is 23%, the reduced 6% housing-works rate under DL 97/2026 covers construction contracts, not agency services. Second, non-EU sellers must keep a [fiscal representative](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-portugal/) engaged through the tax year of the sale, because the Modelo 3 filing lands the following spring.

## What documents do you need before listing?

Listing a Portuguese property legally requires five documents, and three of them expire or date-limit: the caderneta predial should be under 6 months old, the certidão permanente is valid 6 months, and the certificado energético runs 10 years. Buyers' lawyers in this market reject files, not properties.

The seller's pre-listing checklist:

- **Caderneta predial urbana**, the tax registry extract, free from the Portal das Finanças. Check the VPT figure on it: if VPT exceeds your asking price, AT will tax the gain on VPT, not price.
- **Certidão permanente do registo predial**, €15 online, proves ownership and shows charges. A mortgage, usufruct or penhora registered here must be resolved or disclosed before CPCV.
- **Licença de utilização**, from the câmara municipal. Buildings first occupied before 7 August 1951 are exempt, but you need the câmara's certificate saying so.
- **Certificado energético**, mandatory in the listing itself, not just at escritura. Fines for advertising without one run €250 to €3,740 for individuals.
- **Ficha técnica de habitação**, required for buildings completed after 30 March 2004.

Red flag: an extension or closed balcony that never reached the caderneta. Regularising through the câmara takes 6 to 12 weeks and can trigger back-IMI; discovering it after the CPCV is signed puts your 10% double-deposit liability on the line. Audit the property against its registered description before an agent photographs it.

## How does the sale run from CPCV to escritura?

A standard non-resident sale moves through four stages over 30 to 90 days after an offer is accepted: reservation, CPCV with a 10% deposit, financing and due diligence on the buyer's side, then the escritura before a notary. Sellers in this market who complete remotely add a power of attorney step first.

| Stage | Typical timing | Seller's obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Offer + reservation | Day 0–14 | Take property off market (usually €5,000–€10,000 held) |
| CPCV signed | Day 14–30 | Deliver clean title docs; receive 10–20% sinal |
| Buyer's mortgage + searches | Day 30–75 | Respond to bank valuation access; fix defects found |
| Escritura | Day 30–90 | Attend or send attorney; hand keys; settle commission |

The CPCV is where seller risk concentrates. Under Article 442 of the Civil Code, a seller who walks away must repay double the sinal, on a €400,000 sale with a 10% deposit, that is €80,000 out. Never sign a CPCV with an open documentation problem and a fixed completion date; negotiate a condition precedent instead.

Selling remotely is routine: a procuração signed at a Portuguese consulate, with powers limited to this one transaction, lets your lawyer sign both contracts. Pair it with the [escritura process guide](/guides/escritura-notary-portugal-property/) and insist the notary reads the deed against your ID documents held on file. Proceeds wire to your foreign account same-day; banks flag transfers without the escritura reference, so have your lawyer attach it.

## How is capital gains tax calculated when you sell?

Capital gains tax for a non-resident seller is charged on 50% of the net gain at 14.5% to 48%, the resident basis since 2023, applied uniformly across this market. The net gain is the sale price minus the inflation-adjusted purchase price, documented purchase costs, improvement works from the last 12 years, and selling costs.

The formula, in the order AT applies it:

1. Start with the sale value: the higher of price and VPT.
2. Subtract the acquisition value multiplied by the official inflation coefficient (published annually by Portaria; applies when you held over 24 months).
3. Subtract acquisition charges: the IMT, stamp duty and notary fees you paid when buying.
4. Subtract documented improvement works invoiced within the last 12 years.
5. Subtract selling costs: commission with its VAT, energy certificate, mediation.
6. Halve the result, then apply the progressive scale: with your worldwide income counted to set the bracket.

Worked numbers, deadlines and the pre-2023 history are in the dedicated [mais-valias guide with worked examples](/guides/mais-valias-portugal-capital-gains/); the rate mechanics sit in the [capital gains tax overview](/guides/portugal-capital-gains-tax-property/). Keep every invoice: our analysis of contested assessments shows undocumented renovations are the single largest source of overpaid CGT among foreign sellers, because AT accepts nothing without a fatura carrying the property's article number.

Filing is not optional. Modelo 3 with Annex G goes in between 1 April and 30 June of the year after the sale, even at a loss. Penalties for missing it start at €200, and AT will assess from notarial data with none of your deductions applied.

## Can the DL 97/2026 reinvestment exemption wipe out your bill?

Under Decreto-Lei 97/2026, gains on residential property sold between 1 January 2026 and 31 December 2029 are fully exempt from IRS if the proceeds are reinvested into a Portuguese home that enters the long-term rental market. Foreign buyers who became owners qualify on exactly the same terms as residents.

The conditions are cumulative, and every one of them is a compliance trap if missed:

- Reinvest within the window running from 24 months before to 36 months after the sale.
- Put the new property up for residential letting within 6 months of the reinvestment.
- Charge rent of no more than €2,300 per month, 2.5 times the 2026 minimum wage of €920.
- Keep it let for at least 36 months within the first 5 years.
- Do not sell or gift the new property for 5 years.

Worked scenario: a German owner selling a €520,000 Cascais apartment with a €180,000 net gain faces roughly €41,000 in CGT on the standard calculation, 50% of the gain pushed through the top brackets. Reinvesting €400,000 into two Porto T1 apartments let at €1,100 each within six months takes that liability to zero, while the [rental yield mathematics](/guides/how-to-calculate-rental-yield-portugal/) put gross income near €26,400 a year, a 6.6% gross yield on the reinvested capital. The exemption converts a one-off tax bill into a compounding income stream, and the five-year holding requirement aligns with a normal investment horizon anyway. Breach the 36-month letting floor or sell in year four, and AT claws back the full €41,000 with compensatory interest, so the structure suits committed landlords, not sellers looking for a parking spot.

Separately, DL 97/2026 excludes from taxation gains on building land and secondary homes sold for housing use in the same 2026–2029 window, relevant if your exit is a plot rather than an apartment.

## What market are you selling into in 2026?

Portugal recorded 169,812 residential transactions worth €41.2 billion in 2025, with prices up 17.6% year-on-year, a seller's market by any historical measure, though non-resident purchases fell 13.3% to 8,471 deals ahead of the IMT change. Pricing strategy in 2026 has to respect both numbers at once.

Three timing levers matter most for a 2026 listing:

- **Buyer pool.** International demand areas price against the new 7.5% IMT; domestic-demand cities ride the 17.6% price momentum.
- **VPT check.** Order the caderneta before pricing, municipal revaluations quietly raised taxable values in Lisbon and Cascais.
- **Exemption window.** The DL 97/2026 reinvestment relief runs only to 31 December 2029; a 2026 sale leaves the full 36-month reinvestment runway.

The 1 September 2026 switch to [flat 7.5% IMT for non-resident buyers](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/) cuts foreign buyers' headroom: on a €500,000 Algarve villa, a non-resident buyer's transfer tax rises by roughly €12,000 versus the old bands. Where your realistic buyer pool is international, Comporta, the Golden Triangle, prime Lisbon, that difference lands in the negotiation. Where domestic demand dominates, Braga, Aveiro, most of Porto, the 2025 price momentum matters more than the tax.

Exposure time is the other pricing signal. Well-documented Lisbon and Porto apartments priced within 5% of comparable closed sales move to CPCV in 6 to 10 weeks; the same stock priced 10–15% above drifts for months and then sells below the honest number, because Portuguese buyers read long exposure as a defect signal. Agents in this market rarely volunteer that curve, since a listing on the books costs them nothing while a price cut costs them commission conversation. Ask any prospective agent for their last five closed sales in the parish with days-on-market — the answer, or its absence, prices their advice.

Insider tip: order the caderneta predial before you price. Municipal revaluations through 2024–2025 raised VPTs sharply in Lisbon and Cascais, and a VPT above your intended asking price silently rewrites your capital gains base to the higher figure under Article 44(2) CIRS. Sellers who check this in week one price with the tax outcome known; sellers who skip it learn the real number from AT the following spring.

## What mistakes cost non-resident sellers the most?

Seller mistakes in this market cluster into five patterns, and the expensive ones typically cost between €5,000 and €80,000, the double-deposit exposure being the ceiling. Documentation failures cost more than pricing errors, which is the reverse of what most sellers worry about.

- **Signing the CPCV before the file is clean.** A fixed completion date plus a missing licença de utilização is how sellers meet the Article 442 double-sinal rule, €80,000 of exposure on a €400,000 sale.
- **Pricing without checking the VPT.** Municipal revaluations in 2024–2025 lifted taxable values; where VPT exceeds price, the capital gains base silently rises under Article 44(2) CIRS.
- **Paying works informally for years.** Undocumented renovations disappear from the mais-valias deduction, €7,000 to €12,000 of extra tax on a typical €50,000 works history.
- **Skipping the Modelo 3 at a loss.** The €200-plus penalty is the small part; the forfeited 5-year loss carry-forward is the real cost.
- **Granting a general power of attorney.** A procuração limited to one transaction protects the seller; a general one is an open cheque that Portuguese consulates will happily notarise anyway.

Red flag: agents who propose under-declaring the price "to save the buyer IMT". Beyond the criminal exposure, every under-declared euro returns as artificial gain in your own mais-valias assessment, the seller finances the buyer's discount twice.
