It taxes eligible Portuguese employment and self-employment income at a flat 20% for 10 years, hence the NHR 2.0 nickname, though eligibility is far narrower."
  - question: "Who qualifies for IFICI in 2026?"
    answer: "New Portuguese tax residents who were not resident in any of the previous 5 years, were never NHR beneficiaries, and work in a qualifying activity: higher education and research, ICT specialists, engineers, medical doctors, company directors of eligible firms, and export-oriented companies certified by IAPMEI or AICEP, among the professions in Portaria 352/2024/1."
  - question: "What is the IFICI application deadline?"
    answer: "15 January of the year after you become tax resident. Become resident during 2026 and the application is due by 15 January 2027. Late registration does not kill the regime permanently, but the 20% rate then applies only from the year of registration, lost years never come back."
  - question: "Does IFICI cover rental income from Portuguese property?"
    answer: "No. The 20% rate covers only eligible employment (category A) and self-employment (category B) income. Portuguese rental income is taxed under the normal rules, 25% autonomous rate on long-term leases or optional aggregation, whether or not you hold IFICI status."
  - question: "How is foreign income treated under IFICI?"
    answer: "Most foreign-source income, dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains, foreign employment, is exempt in Portugal under IFICI. The major exception is foreign pensions, which are taxed at normal progressive rates of 14.5–48%. NHR's old 10% pension rate no longer exists for new entrants."
  - question: "Can I get IFICI if I had NHR before?"
    answer: "No. Anyone who benefited from NHR at any point is excluded from IFICI, as is anyone who was Portuguese tax resident in the 5 years before arrival, and anyone who opted for the former-resident (regressados) regime. The exclusion is absolute, there is no cure."
  - question: "How does IFICI interact with the 183-day residency test?"
    answer: "IFICI requires you to actually become Portuguese tax resident first, 183 days in any 12-month window, or a habitual residence available on 31 December with intention to keep it. Property ownership helps evidence the habitual-residence limb but does not by itself create residency."
  - question: "Does buying property affect IFICI eligibility?"
    answer: "Ownership is neutral for the regime itself, but becoming tax resident within 24 months of purchase triggers the IMT refund mechanics under DL 97/2026 for buyers who paid the 7.5% non-resident rate, a separate benefit that stacks with IFICI's 20% rate."
  - question: "What tax rate applies to income from blacklisted jurisdictions?"
    answer: "Income from jurisdictions on Portugal's blacklist (Portaria 150/2004, as amended) is taxed at an aggravated 35% rate even under IFICI. Structures routed through zero-tax territories should be unwound before establishing Portuguese residency, not after."
  - question: "How long does IFICI last and can it be renewed?"
    answer: "Ten consecutive years from the year of becoming resident, not renewable. Interrupting Portuguese residency pauses eligibility for the remaining years only if the conditions are still met on return within the original 10-year window."

IFICI is the tax regime Portugal built after shutting NHR, and it rewards a different person: the working professional rather than the pensioner. The 20% flat rate on Portuguese salaries and freelance income is generous, standard progressive rates reach 48% above €80,000, but the door is far narrower than most relocation content admits, and the 15 January deadline is unforgiving. Roughly ten qualifying routes exist, all tied to Portaria 352/2024/1 professions or IAPMEI and AICEP-certified employers, and the exclusions are absolute: five prior years outside Portugal, no NHR history, no regressados election. Portuguese Estate reviews the regime here strictly from a property buyer's angle, what the 20% covers, what it deliberately leaves out, and how it stacks with the DL 97/2026 IMT refund that can return 7.5% of a purchase price to a buyer who completes residency within 24 months.

## What does IFICI actually give you?

IFICI grants a flat 20% IRS rate on eligible Portuguese employment and self-employment income for 10 consecutive years, replacing progressive rates that reach 48% above €80,000. Most foreign-source income is exempt on top, which is why foreign buyers relocating with a business treat the regime as the anchor of this market's tax planning.

| Income type | Under IFICI | Standard regime |
|---|---|---|
| Portuguese salary (eligible activity) | 20% flat | 14.5–48% progressive |
| Portuguese self-employment (eligible) | 20% flat | 14.5–48% progressive |
| Foreign dividends, interest, royalties | Exempt | 28% |
| Foreign capital gains (most) | Exempt | 28% / progressive |
| Foreign pensions | 14.5–48% progressive | 14.5–48% progressive |
| Portuguese rental income | 25% / aggregation | 25% / aggregation |
| Blacklist-jurisdiction income | 35% aggravated | 35% aggravated |

Two rows in that table drive most planning mistakes. Foreign pensions get no relief at all, the old NHR 10% pension rate died with the old regime, so a retiree comparing 2026 Portugal against the NHR-era articles still circulating online is reading history. And Portuguese rental income stays outside the 20% envelope entirely: a landlord's [rental income is taxed at 25%](/guides/non-resident-rental-income-tax-portugal/) on long leases regardless of IFICI status.

Our data makes the stakes concrete. On a €120,000 Portuguese salary: standard IRS takes roughly €48,400 with the solidarity surcharge; IFICI takes €24,000. Over the 10-year life of the regime that spread compounds past €240,000, house money, in this market's prices.

Category B freelancers do even better on paper: the 20% rate combines with the simplified regime's coefficients, typically 75% of billings taxable for professional activities, pushing the effective burden on gross income toward 15%, before social security. The two regimes stack because IFICI fixes the rate while the simplified regime defines the base, and nothing in either diploma forbids the combination.

## Who qualifies under Portaria 352/2024/1?

Qualifying for IFICI requires three cumulative conditions: becoming Portuguese tax resident, having no Portuguese residency in the previous 5 years and no NHR history, and working in an activity listed in Portaria 352/2024/1 or for an eligible employer. Foreign buyers should verify the profession match before relocating, not after, the list is specific, not aspirational.

The recognised routes, as a pre-move checklist:

- **Higher education and scientific research**, teaching and research posts, and roles in entities within the national science and technology system.
- **Qualified professions in eligible companies**, ICT specialists, engineers, mathematicians and physical-science specialists, medical doctors, university-level teachers, industrial designers and directors, working for companies that export at least 50% of turnover or operate in listed industrial and service sectors.
- **IAPMEI / AICEP-certified employers**, jobs in entities recognised as relevant to the national economy, including certified startups under Law 21/2023.
- **Research and development roles**, personnel whose costs qualify for the SIFIDE R&D credit.
- **Madeira and Azores**, regional variants with their own recognition lists.

Certification is documentary: employment contract, NACE code of the employer, professional qualification proof, and where relevant the IAPMEI or AICEP recognition. Self-employed applicants add the activity opening declaration and evidence that the CIRS activity code matches the Portaria list; directors add the company's export share or sector evidence. Processing is free of charge, and the competent body's confirmation typically arrives by 31 March of the application year. Our clients' files show the highest rejection factor is a job title that matches the Portaria but an employer whose activity code does not, check both sides of the match before filing anything.

Exclusions are absolute. Former NHR beneficiaries, anyone Portuguese-resident in the prior 5 years, and anyone who used the regressados regime are out permanently. There is no discretionary route back in, and no grandfathering between regimes: NHR holders keep their old status to its own expiry, but cannot convert it into IFICI afterwards.

## How and when do you apply?

Applying for IFICI costs nothing in fees but is deadline-critical: registration is due by 15 January of the year after you become tax resident, filed through the Portal das Finanças to the competent body, AT for most professions, FCT for research, IAPMEI or AICEP for company routes. Missed years never return.

The sequence that works, from our clients' relocations:

1. **Secure residency first.** Spend 183 days in Portugal in any 12-month window, or hold a habitual home here on 31 December, [property choices and residency routes](/guides/portugal-residency-options-without-golden-visa/) interact here.
2. **Register as resident with AT** and update the morada; the residency start date anchors the whole timeline.
3. **File the IFICI application by 15 January** with contract, qualification and employer evidence attached.
4. **Wait for confirmation: decisions typically land by 31 March**, and file the first Modelo 3 under the regime the following spring.
5. **Keep evidence current.** Changing employer or activity mid-regime requires the new role to qualify too; the 20% rate follows the activity, not the person.

Red flag: late registration is not fatal but is expensive, the regime then applies only from the registration year, permanently shortening your 10-year window. A resident arriving in March 2026 who files in February 2027 has burned one of ten years for the price of a missed calendar entry. Diarise 15 January the day you land.

## How does IFICI fit a property purchase?

IFICI typically meets a property purchase at three points in 2026: the IMT refund for new residents, the financing file, and the rental taxation that the regime pointedly does not improve. Foreign buyers weighing this market against Spain or Italy should price all three, because the 20% headline rate answers only the income question.

- **IMT refund stack.** Non-resident buyers pay [flat 7.5% IMT from 1 September 2026](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/), but DL 97/2026 refunds it for buyers who become Portuguese tax resident within 24 months. An IFICI-track professional buying a €500,000 home recovers roughly €37,500 by completing the residency they were planning anyway, the [refund mechanics](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/) run on their own timetable.
- **Mortgage file.** Portuguese banks underwrite IFICI salaries at face value, and a 20% tax rate lifts net income, debt-service ratios improve by 8–12 points versus the same gross salary taxed progressively, which widens [non-resident mortgage](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-portugal/) headroom before residency even lands.
- **Rental blind spot.** Buy-to-let yield is untouched by IFICI: rents are taxed at 25% autonomous either way. A buyer whose plan is income property first and relocation later should not let the 20% rate colour the yield model.

Case study from our data: a Dutch software architect relocating in 2026 on a €140,000 eligible salary, buying a €450,000 Lisbon apartment before the residency switch. IMT at the 7.5% non-resident rate costs €33,750 at completion; the DL 97/2026 refund returns the full amount once tax residency lands inside the 24-month window; IFICI then saves about €31,000 every year against progressive rates from the first Modelo 3 onwards. Net effect across the first three years is roughly €125,000, more than the apartment's 20% deposit, and none of it depends on aggressive structuring, only on filing the residency declaration and the 15 January registration in the right order. Reverse the order, registering late after a March arrival, and the same file burns one of the ten regime years plus a spring of the 48% marginal rate. Sequencing, not sophistication, is where this money is made.

## Is IFICI worth relocating for?

Worth in this market depends on your income mix: IFICI is decisive for high Portuguese work income, marginal for portfolio income that most treaties already shelter, and worthless for pensions. A structured comparison beats the relocation-blog enthusiasm, the regime typically cuts tax on eligible income from up to 48% to a flat 20%, and saves nothing on everything else.

| Profile | IFICI value | Better lens |
|---|---|---|
| Tech/engineering salary €80k+ | High, 20–28 points saved | Apply; deadline discipline |
| Freelancer, eligible activity | High, plus expense regime choice | Category B planning |
| Retiree on foreign pension | None, progressive rates apply | D7 route and treaty analysis |
| Landlord building PT portfolio | None on rents | Yield and structure planning |
| Entrepreneur with foreign dividends | Real, exemption on distributions | Substance and blacklist check |

Insider tip: the regime's 10-year clock starts with residency, not with registration. Buyers who complete a purchase in autumn, winter in Portugal informally, and only formalise residency the following year gain a cleaner deadline runway and a full first year of the 20% rate, sequencing the escritura and the residency declaration three months apart costs nothing and removes every timing risk in the file. Portuguese Estate models the IFICI-versus-standard outcome as part of any relocation-linked shortlist, because the tax answer changes which cities and price bands make sense.

## What happens when the 10 years end?

Expiry planning is the least-written chapter of every flat-rate regime, and IFICI's is mechanical: the 20% rate and the foreign-income exemptions stop after year ten, Portuguese progressive rates of 14.5–48% take over, and no renewal or successor status exists in the current law. Foreign buyers who anchor a property purchase to the regime should model year eleven before signing anything in year one.

The transitions that matter, as a checklist:

- **Salary and freelance income** revert to progressive rates, the €120,000 salary that paid €24,000 under IFICI pays roughly €48,400 after expiry. The cliff is 20 points in one filing year.
- **Foreign dividends and gains** lose the exemption and move to 28%, portfolio structuring done in year nine is worth more than the same work done in year eleven.
- **The home you live in keeps its own reliefs.** The HPP reinvestment exemption on mais-valias and the [IMT refund already banked](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/) are independent of IFICI and survive it untouched.
- **Leaving Portugal is the other exit.** Ceasing residency before a sale changes the CGT frame back to non-resident filing, same 50% inclusion, but worldwide income counted for the rate, and treaty tie-breakers decide close cases.
- **Timing a property sale** inside or outside the regime changes nothing on the gain itself: mais-valias never enjoyed the 20% rate. What changes is the bracket your other income builds underneath it.

Buyer scenario from our clients: a couple in year eight of the regime, holding a €700,000 Lisbon apartment with a €200,000 unrealised gain, weighing sale-now against sale-after-expiry. The gain's tax is bracket-driven either way, but their €160,000 of eligible salaries at 20% keeps the household's marginal position two bands lower while the regime lasts, selling in year nine instead of year eleven saves about €9,000 on the same disposal. Expiry is not a cliff for property; it is a cliff for income that quietly reprices everything filed alongside it.

Social security runs on its own track and IFICI does not touch it. Employees pay 11% with the employer's 23.75% on top; category B freelancers pay 21.4% on a contribution base that follows billings, with a first-year exemption for new activity registrations. On the €120,000 salary in the earlier example, the employee-side contribution is roughly €13,200 a year regardless of the 20% income-tax rate, and relocation models that skip it overstate the net by a wide margin. Budget both lines before comparing Portugal against a flat-tax jurisdiction that bundles them.

Insider tip: the ten years are counted from the residency year, consecutively, whether or not you claimed the rate correctly each spring, an audit that disqualifies one middle year does not pause the clock. Keep each year's eligibility evidence (contract, employer NACE code, activity registration) in the same file as the original application; our data shows renewal-style disputes concentrate in years four to six, when first employers change and nobody re-verifies the match.
