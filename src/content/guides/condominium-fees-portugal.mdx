Condominium charges are the running cost Portuguese property listings mention last and owners feel first, because the quota arrives every month for as long as you hold the keys. Across this market the spread is wide: €30 monthly in a three-storey walk-up in Braga, €400 in an amenity-heavy Cascais residence, €600 and beyond where concierge and heated pools meet a small number of fractions splitting the bill. The legal machinery underneath is uniform, permilagem shares, an annual owners' assembly, a mandatory reserve fund of at least 10% of quotas, and since Law 8/2022 a compulsory debt disclosure at sale. What varies is governance: two buildings with identical equipment can charge identical quotas and be in opposite financial health. The documents that reveal which is which are all obtainable before you offer.

## What do condominium fees actually cost by building type?

Condominium quotas in Portugal typically run from €30 to more than €600 per month, and the equipment list drives the number far more than the apartment's size or the area's prestige in this market. A lift, a pool, a garage and a concierge each add a fixed operating layer that the fractions divide between them.

| Building type | Typical monthly quota | Main cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-up, no lift, no amenities | €30–80 | Cleaning, common electricity, insurance |
| Lift building, 1970s–2000s | €60–150 | Lift contract and inspections, cleaning |
| Recent build with garage | €100–200 | Lift, garage doors and ventilation, systems |
| Pool, gardens or concierge | €150–400 | Staff, water treatment, landscaping |
| Premium Lisbon and Cascais stock | €200–600+ | Concierge cover, gyms, heated pools, few fractions |
| Gated villa developments | €100–500 | Roads, security, shared leisure areas |

Small buildings with heavy equipment are the trap case. A boutique block of 8 fractions with a lift and a pool divides the same fixed contracts among 8 owners that a 60-fraction tower divides among 60, so the boutique quota runs multiples higher for the same amenity list. Fraction count is as important as the equipment when you compare two listings, and it belongs on the same checklist as the quota itself.

Quotas are also deductible where the property earns: condominium charges reduce taxable rent before the 25% autonomous rate, alongside IMI and insurance, as part of the wider [running-cost picture](/guides/property-maintenance-costs-portugal/).

## How does the permilagem system split the bill?

Permilagem is the per-thousand share assigned to each fraction in the propriedade horizontal title, and it fixes your slice of every budget line: a fraction holding 85‰ pays 8.5% of the cleaning contract, 8.5% of the lift maintenance and 8.5% of any works vote, regardless of who uses what, and prestige of the area never enters the formula.

The consequences are mechanical and worth checking before purchase:

- **The share is in the deed, not in negotiation.** Permilagem is set when the building is constituted and appears in the escritura and land registry; changing it needs unanimity, which in practice means it never changes.
- **Bigger fractions subsidise smaller ones on usage.** A ground-floor owner pays their permilagem share of the lift they never ride, unless the title or regulation says otherwise, which occasionally it does.
- **Garages and storage rooms carry their own permilagem** when deeded as autonomous fractions, so a listing with parking may involve two quotas, not one.
- **Works votes are counted in permilagem too.** Majorities at the assembly are computed by value share, not by headcount, so a few large fractions can carry or block a decision.

Red flag: a listing whose advertised quota looks improbably low against the equipment list. Either the budget underfunds maintenance, which surfaces later as a special assessment, or the advertised figure quietly excludes the garage fraction's quota. Both are visible in the approved budget, which is one of the documents to request before offering.

## What does the reserve fund tell you about a building?

Reserve funds are mandatory in Portuguese condominiums: the fundo comum de reserva must receive at least 10% of each owner's regular quota, exists specifically to pay for conservation works, and its balance divided by the number of fractions is the single fastest health check a buyer in this market can run on a building before offering.

Worked case study: two 1990s Lisbon buildings, each with 20 fractions and a €120 average quota. Building A has applied the minimum 10% for a decade and holds roughly €29,000 in reserve; a €60,000 façade repair therefore needs a top-up of about €1,550 per fraction. Building B distributed nothing beyond the legal minimum's paper entry, holds €4,000, and the same façade job lands as a €2,800 special assessment per fraction, voted in the same month the scaffolding goes up. The two flats looked identical on the portal, and the €1,250 difference per owner was sitting in the accounts all along.

Three numbers make the check concrete:

- **Reserve per fraction**: total balance divided by fraction count. Below €1,000 in a building over 30 years old is a warning.
- **Age of the big-ticket items**: roof and waterproofing around 25 to 30 years, lifts 20 to 25, façades variable by exposure.
- **Arrears percentage**: unpaid quotas above 10% of budget mean the reserve is theoretical.

Reading the reserve line takes minutes. Ask the administrator for the balance, divide by the number of fractions, and compare against the age of the roof, the lift and the façade. A building past 30 years with less than €1,000 of reserve per fraction is not cheaper to buy into; it is a deferred bill with your name on it. The [hidden costs guide](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-portugal/) covers the other charges that surface at the same moment.

## What changed with Law 8/2022 on debts and sales?

Law 8/2022 rewired the sale-time mechanics of condominium debt: the seller must obtain a written declaration from the administrator listing the fraction's charges and any arrears, the declaration is issued within 10 days of the request, and the debts belong to the person who incurred them rather than travelling silently with the keys — the old trap for foreign buyers.

| Before the reform | Since Law 8/2022 |
|---|---|
| Buyers discovered arrears after the deed | Administrator's declaration presented at sale |
| Debt allocation argued after completion | Charges stay with the owner who incurred them |
| No standard disclosure document | Written declaration with a 10-day issue deadline |
| Administrators chased whoever held the keys | Clear paper trail of who owes what at the deed date |

Practice still rewards attention. The declaration covers what the administration has recorded, so a works assessment voted but not yet billed can sit outside it; the minutes of the last two assemblies close that gap, because approved works appear there before they appear in anyone's account. A buyer who reads the declaration and the minutes together sees both the recorded debts and the committed future spending. On a typical €150 monthly quota, even 6 months of unrecorded liability is €900 before works votes, which is why the minutes matter as much as the declaration.

Insider tip: make the [CPCV](/guides/cpcv-promissory-contract-portugal/) conditional on the Law 8/2022 declaration showing zero arrears, rather than collecting it as a formality at the deed. Sellers resolve their condominium debts quickly when the deposit timeline depends on it, and the clause costs one sentence in the contract.

## How is a condominium actually governed?

Condominium governance is one annual meeting plus one executor: the assembleia de condóminos approves the budget and accounts, elects the administrator and votes works by majorities counted in permilagem, with 66.7% of building value required for innovations, while the administrator signs contracts, collects quotas and keeps the minutes that later tell foreign buyers the truth.

The decision thresholds are the part owners misread most often:

- **Ordinary management**, budget, accounts, administrator: majority of those present at a properly convened meeting.
- **Conservation works**: generally ordinary majorities, because keeping the building sound is a duty, not an option.
- **Innovations**, a new lift, a pool, closing balconies: 66.7% of the building's total permilagem value, counted in thousandths.
- **Changing the title or permilagem**: unanimity, which is why mistakes in the original constitution persist for decades.
- **Alojamento Local matters**: the 2023-2024 reforms gave condominiums a defined voice on short-let operation, which is why the regulation and minutes matter to anyone underwriting AL income against the [licensing rules](/guides/alojamento-local-license-portugal/).

Arrears enforcement runs on the same paperwork: the approved minutes recording an owner's debt support injunction proceedings, and interest accrues on unpaid quotas. For the building, every non-payer is a temporary levy on everyone else, which is why the arrears percentage in the accounts is a governance signal, not just a financial one. Above 10% of quotas unpaid, assume the building struggles to fund anything beyond routine cleaning, and treat the number as a red flag equal to any structural defect.

## How do quotas move over time, and what breaks the pattern?

Quota inflation in Portugal runs on contracts rather than on sentiment: cleaning, lift maintenance and building insurance typically reprice annually, and a budget rising 5% to 8% a year through the recent inflation cycle was tracking suppliers, not mismanagement, in most of this market. Three lines dominate the annual drift.

- **Labour-linked services** follow the minimum wage, which reached €920 in 2026 after years of increases above 5%.
- **Insurance** repriced sharply across the market after 2022.
- **Lift contracts** carry indexation clauses that compound quietly.

The break in the pattern is the special assessment, and it is predictable years ahead by anyone reading the accounts: the two inputs that produce it, the age of the expensive components and the reserve balance meant to fund their replacement, both sit in documents a buyer can request for free before ever making an offer. Worked case study: a 1998 building carries 2 original lifts with €45,000 of replacement ahead and a reserve fund holding €9,000 across 20 fractions. The €36,000 gap is tomorrow's special assessment, roughly €1,800 per fraction weighted by permilagem, and it is visible in one page of accounts years before the vote that levies it. The same building with €40,000 reserved reads as a €5,000 residual spread painlessly — identical lifts, opposite purchase decisions on the same accounts page.

Insider tip: request the last 3 approved budgets together, not just the current one; the year-on-year drift shows whether the lift conversation has started, and a quiet jump in the reserve line is usually the committee preparing the ground.

| Driver | Typical movement | Where it shows first |
|---|---|---|
| Contract repricing | 4–8% a year | Annual budget vote |
| Staff conversion | Salary + 23.75% employer contributions | Assembly minutes |
| Insurance repricing | Step change after claims | Budget insurance line |

Worked case study: an 18-fraction Porto building charging €95 monthly in 2020 reaches €118 by 2026 through ordinary contract repricing, roughly 4% a year. The same building's lift dates from 1998 and its reserve holds €14,000. A lift replacement quoted at €85,000 means a gap of €71,000, which is €3,900 per fraction, votable in a single assembly once the inspection report forces the issue. The owner who bought in 2025 knowing the lift's age and the reserve balance priced that €3,900 into the offer; the owner who read only the €112 quota did not. Nothing about the assessment was a surprise except to those who chose not to look, and every input sat in documents obtainable for free.

Two structural changes also move quotas discontinuously. A building that adds staff, converting occasional cleaning into a concierge arrangement, steps the budget up by the salary plus 23.75% employer social contributions. And insurance repricing after a claims-heavy year, or after mandatory cover updates, lands across every fraction at once. Both appear in assembly minutes a full cycle before they appear in the quota, which is one more reason the minutes are the highest-value pages in the whole file.

## Which documents should you read before buying in?

Document review is the entire method in this market and typically costs one afternoon: 10 papers, all producible by the seller or administrator within 5 to 10 days, describe a building's finances and politics more honestly than any viewing, and 6 of the 10 belong in your hands before the offer rather than before the deed.

Buyer scenario: comparing 2 similar Cascais T2s, one at a €95 monthly quota with no lift and one at €240 with lift, pool and concierge, the running difference is €1,740 a year — capitalised at a 5% yield, roughly €35,000 of price equivalent that the asking prices rarely acknowledge.

The pre-offer checklist:

1. **2 years of approved accounts**, showing income, spending and the arrears line.
2. **The current budget**, to compare the advertised quota against the real one, garage included.
3. **The reserve fund balance**, divided by fractions and read against the building's age.
4. **Minutes of the last 2 assemblies**, where voted works and disputes live.
5. **The condominium regulation**, for letting rules, AL position and use restrictions.
6. **The Law 8/2022 debt declaration** for the exact fraction, dated within 10 days of the CPCV.
7. **The propriedade horizontal title**, confirming permilagem and what the fraction includes.
8. **Insurance policy of the building**, since fire cover for the whole building is mandatory.
9. **Lift inspection certificate**, where there is one, with an inspection inside the last 2 years.
10. **Any pending works quotations** the administrator holds, voted or about to be.

Sequenced against the purchase itself, items 1 to 5 belong before the offer, item 6 before the CPCV within its 10-day issue window, and the rest before the deed, alongside the standard [buying cost stack](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-portugal/). An administrator who cannot produce these within a week is telling you how the building is run, and that answer is also information.
