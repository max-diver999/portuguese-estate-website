Quick Answer: The D8 is Portugal's remote work visa. In 2026 it asks for about EUR 3,680 a month of income earned outside Portugal, four times the national minimum wage of EUR 920. It comes in two variants with very different consequences, and only one of them leads to a residence permit and eventual citizenship eligibility.

The D8 arrived in late 2022 and did something unusual: it gave remote employees a route that did not require capital or a pension. It has since become the default answer for anyone under fifty who wants to live in Portugal and keeps their job.

It is also the visa most often described incorrectly online, for two reasons. The income threshold moves every January with the minimum wage, so stale figures propagate. And the fact that there are two different D8 visas, with different endpoints, gets flattened into one in most write-ups.

## The Two D8 Visas, and Why the Choice Matters

| | Temporary stay D8 | Residence D8 |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum duration | One year, renewable | Leads to a renewable residence permit |
| Leads to a residence permit | No | Yes |
| Counts toward citizenship | No | Yes |
| Typical tax outcome | Often stays non-resident | Normally becomes tax resident |
| Suits | A trial year, or genuinely mobile workers | People intending to settle |

The temporary stay variant is the lighter application and the one people drift into when they are unsure. It is the right choice for a trial year. It is the wrong choice if you intend to stay, because none of that year counts toward anything and you will have to start again on the residence track.

If you are weighing this against the income-based alternative for non-workers, see [the D7 passive income visa](/move-to-portugal/d7-visa/), and against the capital route, [the Portugal Golden Visa](/move-to-portugal/golden-visa/). The two visas differ on one axis: where the money comes from. D7 is passive income, D8 is active remote work.

## The 2026 Income Test

The D8 income threshold is 4 times the retribuição mínima mensal garantida, the guaranteed minimum monthly wage. That wage is EUR 920 a month in 2026, which puts the D8 test at roughly EUR 3,680 a month, or about EUR 44,160 a year, evidenced over a period rather than at a single moment.

| Year | Minimum wage | D8 threshold at four times |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | EUR 760 | EUR 3,040 |
| 2025 | EUR 870 | EUR 3,480 |
| 2026 | EUR 920 | EUR 3,680 |

That table is the reason to distrust undated advice on this visa. Two of those three figures are still quoted as current across the web, and an application built on the 2023 number is roughly EUR 640 a month short.

Beyond the monthly figure, consulates typically want:

- Twelve months of income history at or above the threshold
- An employment contract or client contracts showing the work is for entities outside Portugal
- Savings in addition to income, commonly benchmarked against the annual threshold
- Proof of accommodation in Portugal
- Health insurance and a criminal record certificate

Self-employed applicants have the harder case, because the burden is showing that revenue is both sufficient and stable. Three large invoices in a year reads worse than twelve regular ones totalling the same amount.

## Where D8 Holders Actually Settle

Most D8 guides stop at the paperwork and leave the harder question unanswered: where to actually live. Four locations absorb the large majority of D8 holders, and they differ by more than 50% on housing cost, by connectivity, and by whether an established remote-worker community already exists there.

| Location | Why nomads choose it | Cost signal |
|---|---|---|
| Lisbon | Connectivity, English-speaking work culture, density of coworking | Highest housing costs in the country |
| Porto | Materially cheaper than Lisbon, strong tech scene | Lower rents, smaller international job market |
| Funchal, Madeira | Digital nomad village at Ponta do Sol, mild winters, direct northern European flights | Notably lower cost base than Lisbon |
| Setubal peninsula | Under an hour from Lisbon, coastal | Lisbon access without Lisbon prices |
| Silver Coast | Space and coastline, popular with families | Lowest of the five, weaker connectivity |

City-by-city budgets, including what changes once you own rather than rent, are in [the cost of living in Portugal](/move-to-portugal/cost-of-living/).

Two practical filters cut this list quickly. The first is time zone: if your employer is on United States hours, Portugal at UTC or UTC+1 gives you a workable overlap with the east coast and a punishing one with the west. The second is flight frequency, which decides how often you can realistically go back. Lisbon and Porto both have dense long-haul networks; Funchal does not, and the Silver Coast means driving to Lisbon for anything intercontinental.

Madeira is the deliberate outlier. The island set out to attract remote workers, established the nomad village at Ponta do Sol, and built a community around it. That is a genuine difference from every other Portuguese location, and no top-ranking D8 guide mentions it.

## The Application Sequence

| Stage | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | NIF, Portuguese bank account, accommodation secured | Remotely or in Portugal |
| Consulate application | Income evidence, contracts, insurance, criminal record certificate | Consulate covering your residence |
| Visa issued | Entry visa granted for travel to Portugal | On approval |
| AIMA appointment | Biometrics and residence permit application, residence variant only | Portugal |
| Residence permit | Card issued, renewal cycle begins | Portugal |
| Tax registration | Register with Financas once resident | Portugal |

Two ordering points matter. The NIF comes before the bank account, and the bank account comes before most of the rest, so start there even if the visa decision is months away. And the accommodation proof required at the consulate stage is usually a lease, which means committing to a rental in a city you have not lived in, sight unseen. Twelve-month leases with a break clause are worth paying slightly more for.

Consulate requirements differ by jurisdiction more than most applicants expect. The savings figure requested, the acceptable form of health insurance and the age of the criminal record certificate all vary. Verify the checklist for your specific consulate before booking, because appointment slots are scarce and rebooking costs months.

## What the D8 Does Not Cover

The D8 grants residence on the basis of foreign remote income, and three things fall outside it entirely: taking local Portuguese employment, running a Portuguese-facing business, and any automatic tax advantage. Each has caught applicants who assumed the visa was broader than its wording.

Three gaps catch people out.

It does not let you work for a Portuguese employer. If your plan involves eventually joining a local company, that is a different permit and a different application, and doing it informally puts the permit at risk.

It does not shelter your income from tax. The residence variant makes you tax resident, and tax residency is worldwide. IFICI may or may not help depending on how your income is classified, and that classification is a technical question rather than an obvious one.

It does not include your partner's right to work locally. Family reunification brings dependants in, but a spouse wanting local employment needs their own route to it. For a couple where both work, model this before you move rather than after.

## Tax After You Arrive

The residence variant of the D8 normally makes you Portuguese tax resident, triggered by the 183-day presence test or a habitual home in Portugal. From that point Portugal taxes worldwide income, and the IFICI regime applies a flat rate to qualifying employment and self-employment income for 10 years. See [Portugal tax residency and the 183-day test](/guides/portugal-tax-residency-183-days/).

Once resident, Portugal taxes worldwide income. The regime that used to soften that landing, NHR, closed to new applicants at the end of 2024. Its replacement, IFICI, applies a flat rate to qualifying Portuguese employment and self-employment income for ten consecutive years, and excludes pensions and Portuguese rental income. The detail is in [IFICI, the NHR replacement](/guides/ifici-portugal-nhr-2-tax-regime/).

For a D8 holder the key question is whether their income is even in scope. Salary from a foreign employer, paid into a foreign account, is not automatically qualifying Portuguese employment income for IFICI purposes. That is a question for a Portuguese tax adviser before you move, not after your first return is due.

## Renting Versus Buying on a D8

Renting first is the right answer on the D8 more clearly than on any other route, because the visa itself requires no property and the reversal cost of buying is high. A purchase costs 7.5% IMT plus 0.8% stamp duty to enter and roughly 5% to exit, against a year of rent to learn the same thing.

- Remote work is contractual and can end. A mortgage does not.
- The temporary stay variant leads nowhere permanent, so buying against it is buying against a one-year permission.
- Buying before tax residency triggers the flat 7.5% IMT rate on residential property under DL 97/2026, plus 0.8% stamp duty, against a resident scale that starts far lower.
- You do not yet know whether Lisbon, Porto or Funchal is the answer, and the three are not interchangeable.

One pattern does work: rent for a year in the city you think you want, use that year to confirm the tax position with an adviser, and buy in the second year once you are tax resident and on the resident IMT scale. That sequence costs you twelve months of rent and saves you the difference between the flat 7.5% rate and the resident scale, which on a mid-market apartment is usually the larger number.

If you do buy, the sequence and the costs are the same as for any foreign purchaser: NIF first, then bank account, then promissory contract and deed. See [NIF for a Portugal property purchase](/guides/nif-portugal-property-purchase/) and [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

Our earlier [digital nomad visa and property guide](/guides/portugal-digital-nomad-visa-property/) covers the purchase mechanics in more depth once that decision is made.

## The Employer Problem Nobody Solves in Advance

The D8 assumes a remote worker with income from outside Portugal, and the awkward case is the employee of a foreign company that has no entity here. From the employer's side, having a member of staff resident in another country raises payroll, social security and permanent establishment questions that their HR department may never have faced.

| Situation | Typical friction |
|---|---|
| Large employer with global mobility | Usually workable, and slow |
| Small employer, single overseas worker | Often refused outright, on tax risk |
| Contractor invoicing the same company | Simpler for them, changes your status |
| Multiple freelance clients | The cleanest D8 profile |
| Employer with a Portuguese entity | Straightforward, but rare |

The pattern worth noticing is that the visa is easiest for the self-employed and hardest for the conventionally employed, which is the opposite of most people's assumption. A salaried applicant needs a letter from an employer willing to state in writing that the arrangement is permanent, and some employers will not sign that even when they have verbally agreed to the move.

Two mitigations. Establish the employer's written position before starting the application rather than after, because the letter is a hard requirement and not every employer will produce one. And if the answer is a move to a contractor relationship, understand what that changes: it affects your income evidence, your Portuguese tax treatment and your social security position, and each of those needs checking before you agree to it.

## Internet, and Where It Is Actually Good

Connectivity is the most under-researched variable in a visa built entirely on remote work. Fibre reaches most Portuguese cities and coastal towns at speeds well above 100 Mbps, but a city address does not guarantee a connection into a specific flat, and protected historic buildings are the common exception.

| Setting | Realistic position |
|---|---|
| Lisbon, Porto, Funchal, larger cities | Fibre widely available and fast |
| Coastal towns and resort developments | Generally fibre, but check the specific building |
| Older buildings in historic centres | Fibre may not reach the flat itself |
| Interior villages | Variable; some fibre, some not |
| Isolated rural property | Frequently mobile or satellite only |
| Mobile coverage | Good on the coast, patchy in the hills |

The historic-centre row is the one that catches people. A city address does not guarantee a fibre connection into the apartment: in protected buildings the installation may be constrained, and the building's internal cabling can be the bottleneck rather than the street.

Before signing any lease or making any offer, ask for evidence of the actual connection in the actual unit: the provider, the technology and the speed, ideally with a recent bill or a live test. A remote worker whose income depends on a video call cannot treat this as a detail to sort out after moving in.

## Pros and Cons

The D8 reaches the same residence permit and the same naturalisation clock as a EUR 500,000 Golden Visa fund, for an income test of roughly EUR 3,680 a month and no capital lock at all. What it asks instead is that you live here, with Portuguese tax residency and its worldwide reporting following automatically.

Advantages:

- No capital requirement, unlike the Golden Visa fund route
- Available to employees, which the D7 is not
- Residence variant leads to a permit and eventual citizenship eligibility
- Family reunification available under the standard rules
- Portugal has genuine remote work infrastructure, including Madeira's nomad village

Disadvantages:

- The threshold moves every January and stale figures are everywhere
- Self-employed applicants carry a heavier evidential burden
- Tax residency brings worldwide income into scope and IFICI may not cover you
- AIMA processing has been slow
- The temporary stay variant is a dead end if you later decide to settle

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Any guide quoting EUR 3,040 or EUR 3,480.** Those are the 2023 and 2025 thresholds. If that number is stale, assume the tax section is too.
- **Applying for the temporary stay visa by default.** It is quicker and cheaper, and it costs you the entire residence clock.
- **Assuming IFICI replaces NHR one for one.** It does not. It is narrower, and foreign salary is the open question.
- **Buying in the first year.** Wrong city, wrong tax status, wrong time.
- **Mixed client bases.** If a meaningful share of your revenue is Portuguese-sourced, the visa premise weakens.

Checklist before you apply:

- Confirm which of the two D8 visas you are actually applying for, in writing
- Twelve months of income evidence at EUR 3,680 or above
- Contracts naming entities outside Portugal
- A Portuguese address that will hold until the AIMA appointment
- A tax opinion on whether your income qualifies under IFICI, obtained before you move

## Who the D8 Suits

The D8 fits applicants with foreign remote income above roughly EUR 3,680 a month who intend to live in Portugal for most of the year. It fits worst where the employer will not put the arrangement in writing, which is the single most common reason an otherwise clean application never gets filed.

**The salaried remote employee.** The core case, and the profile the visa was designed for. One employer, one contract, regular payments over the threshold.

**The established freelancer.** Works, provided the client base is outside Portugal and the revenue is regular. Prepare for a heavier evidential burden than an employee faces.

**The trial-year mover.** The temporary stay variant is genuinely useful here, as long as you understand it is a trial and not a first step.

**The family relocating for one partner's job.** Viable, and the reunification rules cover it, but model the school costs and the second partner's work position before committing, because the second partner cannot simply take a local job on this permit.
