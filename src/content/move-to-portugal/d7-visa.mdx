The D7 is the route most people mean when they say they want to retire to Portugal. It has existed for years, it does not require capital, and it is deliberately unglamorous: prove you can support yourself without working, and Portugal will let you live there.

What the visa guides consistently skip is the property decision that sits alongside it. Almost every D7 applicant needs a Portuguese address, and the question of whether that address should be rented or owned has a five-figure tax consequence. This guide covers the immigration mechanics and then answers that question with numbers.

## What the D7 Is, and What It Is Not

The D7 is a long-stay national visa for applicants whose income does not depend on working. It leads to a residence permit and, in time, to eligibility for citizenship: since 19 May 2026 that means seven years of legal residence for EU and CPLP nationals and ten for everyone else, with an A2 Portuguese language test.

What it is not:

- It is not a property visa. There is no minimum purchase and no purchase requirement at all.
- It is not the Golden Visa. Real estate stopped qualifying for that programme under Law 56/2023 in October 2023, and the D7 never involved property in the first place.
- It is not automatic tax residency. Holding the card and being tax resident are two different statuses with two different tests.
- It is not a work permit for local employment. The income has to be passive.

If you are weighing this against the capital route, see [the Portugal Golden Visa in 2026](/move-to-portugal/golden-visa/). Consulates and AIMA assess whether income is stable, lawful and sufficient. Twenty-four months of pension credits at EUR 900 a month reads better than one transfer of EUR 40,000, even though the second is a larger number.

### The 2026 income benchmark

Portuguese practice indexes the D7 threshold to the retribuição mínima mensal garantida, which is EUR 920 a month in 2026.

| Household | Monthly passive income to demonstrate |
|---|---|
| Single applicant | EUR 920 |
| Applicant and spouse | EUR 920 plus 50%, about EUR 1,380 |
| Couple with one dependent child | About EUR 1,656 |
| Couple with two dependent children | About EUR 1,932 |

Treat these as the floor, not the target. Consulates have discretion, and an application sitting exactly on the line with irregular credits is weaker than one comfortably above it with a clean twenty-four month history.

Any figure you read below EUR 920 for 2026 comes from an earlier minimum wage. The threshold moved with the January uplift, and a large part of the published advice on this visa has not been updated since.

## What Counts as Passive Income

Passive income for D7 purposes means money that arrives without you working for it in Portugal: pensions, dividends, interest, royalties, and rent from property outside the country. The 2026 reference figure is roughly EUR 920 a month for a single applicant, with uplifts of 50% for a spouse and 30% per dependent child.

Commonly accepted:

- State and occupational pensions
- Dividends and regular portfolio distributions
- Rental income from property outside Portugal, evidenced by tax returns
- Royalties with a multi-year payment history
- Certain annuities and structured settlements

Commonly rejected:

- Salary from an employer, which belongs to the [D8 digital nomad route](/guides/portugal-digital-nomad-visa-property/)
- Trading profits without a demonstrable pattern
- Undocumented family transfers
- Cryptocurrency gains without a tax-filed history

The evidential standard is documentary. Bank statements showing the credits, tax returns showing the source, and where relevant a letter from the pension provider or fund administrator.

## Rent or Buy: The Decision Nobody Answers

Search demand shows people asking this directly. Bing's query log for this site includes "portugal d7 visa buy property instead of rent" and "d7 visa requirements property purchase vs rental". None of the firms ranking for the D7 head term answer it with figures.

Here is the arithmetic.

| Scenario | IMT on a EUR 400,000 home | Cash position | Recoverable later? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy before tax residency | Flat 7.5%, EUR 30,000 | Paid at the deed | Yes, if tax resident within 24 months and 183-day test met |
| Buy after tax residency | Resident progressive scale, materially lower | Paid at the deed | Not applicable |
| Rent for 12 months, then buy | None during the lease | Rent only | Resident scale applies if timed correctly |

The flat 7.5% non-resident rate applies to residential property from 1 September 2026 under DL 97/2026. Add 0.8% stamp duty on top, plus notary, registration and legal fees.

There is a refund route, set out in [IMT refund for tax residents](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/), but it is a refund: you fund the higher amount first and reclaim it after establishing residency, which for most applicants means a year or more of the money sitting with Finanças rather than with you.

Renting first also buys you information. Twelve months in a neighbourhood tells you things no viewing does: what the street sounds like in August, how far the nearest hospital actually is, whether the winter damp in that building is a problem. D7 applicants relocating permanently are making a housing decision, not just an investment one.

The case for buying immediately is narrower than the internet suggests. It holds when you already know the area well, when the rental market where you want to live is genuinely tight, or when you are buying with cash and the IMT difference is small relative to the transaction.

## Documents and the Application Sequence

| Stage | What you need | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | NIF, Portuguese bank account, proof of accommodation | Portugal or remotely |
| Consulate application | Income evidence, criminal record certificate, health insurance, accommodation proof | Consulate in your country of residence |
| Entry | Four-month visa issued for travel to Portugal | On approval |
| AIMA appointment | Biometrics, residence permit application | Portugal |
| Residence permit | Card issued, renewal cycle begins | Portugal |
| Tax registration | Register with Finanças if establishing tax residency | Portugal |

You will need a NIF before almost anything else, including the bank account. The process is covered in [NIF for a Portugal property purchase](/guides/nif-portugal-property-purchase/), which applies equally when the purpose is a lease rather than a deed.

Requirements vary by consulate. Verify the checklist for your specific jurisdiction before booking, because the differences are real and the appointment slots are not easy to rebook.

## Tax Once You Arrive

Tax residency, not the visa, is what changes your position, and it triggers on either the 183-day presence test or having your habitual home in Portugal. From that point Portugal taxes worldwide income, and a treaty prevents double taxation without removing either filing obligation. The detail is in [Portugal tax residency and the 183-day test](/guides/portugal-tax-residency-183-days/).

Once resident, Portugal taxes worldwide income. Pensions are the pressure point: the old NHR regime that sheltered them closed to new applicants at the end of 2024, and its replacement, IFICI, is narrower. IFICI applies a flat rate to qualifying Portuguese employment and self-employment income for ten consecutive years, and it explicitly excludes pensions and Portuguese rental income. See [IFICI, the NHR replacement](/guides/ifici-portugal-nhr-2-tax-regime/).

For a retiree living on a pension, that exclusion is the single most important line in Portuguese tax law, and a large amount of published D7 advice still describes the regime that no longer applies to them.

## Timing the Tax Residency Date

The window between arriving and becoming Portuguese tax resident is short and it matters financially. A non-resident completing on or after 1 September 2026 pays flat 7.5% IMT; a resident buyer moves onto progressive bands, and a buyer who becomes resident within 24 months can reclaim the difference. On EUR 400,000 that gap is worth five figures.

Tax residency starts when you meet the 183-day test in a calendar year, or earlier if you establish a habitual home in Portugal with the intention of keeping it. The practical consequence is that an applicant who lands in October and buys in November is almost certainly still non-resident for that calendar year, and will pay the flat 7.5% rate. The same applicant buying the following June, having lived there since October, is on the resident scale.

| Arrival month | Days in Portugal that calendar year | Likely status at a purchase in the same year |
|---|---|---|
| February | Over 183 by August | Resident from that year, resident scale available |
| June | Roughly 180 to 200, marginal | Depends on exact days, evidence matters |
| October | Under 100 | Non-resident, flat 7.5% applies |

Two things follow. First, arrival timing is a tax decision, not only a logistics one. Second, if you arrive late in the year and want to buy quickly, the refund route is the mechanism to plan around rather than the resident scale, and that means budgeting the cash for the higher rate up front.

Keep the evidence as you go. Flight records, lease agreements, utility bills and bank activity are what substantiate a residency claim later, and reconstructing them two years after the fact is considerably harder than filing them as they arrive.

## The Consulate Lottery, and Why Timelines Vary

D7 applications begin at the Portuguese consulate covering your place of residence, and the practical experience varies enormously between them. Two applicants with identical files, applying in different countries or even different cities, routinely report different appointment waits, different document expectations and different processing times.

| Variable | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|
| Appointment availability | From weeks to many months, by post |
| Document interpretation | Consulates differ on what satisfies the income test |
| Accommodation evidence | Some accept a lease, some want more |
| Apostille and translation | Requirements stated differently post to post |
| Processing after submission | Weeks to months, with little visibility |

The consequence is that any single reported timeline, including a confident one from a forum, describes one post at one moment. Build your own plan around the appointment date you can actually obtain, not around somebody else's experience.

Two practical mitigations. Start the appointment search before the file is complete, because the appointment is usually the longest pole. And prepare documents to the strictest interpretation you find rather than the most lenient, since surplus evidence costs a little and a rejected file costs a cycle.

Once in Portugal the residence permit stage runs through AIMA, and its own backlog is a separate queue with its own timeline. The visa is the beginning of the process rather than the end of it, and the naturalisation clock does not start until the permit is issued: see [Portuguese citizenship by investment](/move-to-portugal/citizenship-by-investment/) for how that counting works after the 2026 law.

## What Happens at Renewal

The D7 residence permit is issued for a fixed term rather than indefinitely, and each renewal re-tests the original grounds. Income must still be passive and still sufficient, absences must sit inside the permitted limits, Portuguese tax returns must be filed, and accommodation in Portugal must be current.

| At renewal | What is examined |
|---|---|
| Continued income | Still passive, still sufficient, still documented |
| Physical presence | Absences within the permitted limits |
| Accommodation | A current lease or ownership in Portugal |
| Tax compliance | Portuguese returns filed, contributions in order |
| Criminal record | Clean, in Portugal and abroad |

The presence requirement is the one that trips people. The D7 is a residence visa and it expects residence: extended absences risk the permit, and a household that treats it as a flexible travel document rather than a move is a household with a problem at renewal.

The income requirement is the second. Passive income that has shrunk, or a source that has changed character since the original application, has to be re-evidenced on the new facts rather than the old ones. Keep the documentation current year by year rather than reconstructing it in the month before the appointment.

Renewal appointments run through AIMA and inherit its scheduling, so start early and expect the process to take longer than the notice period suggests. Under the counting rules introduced in 2026, a gap between one permit expiring and the next being issued is a gap in the residence that counts toward naturalisation, which makes early renewal a substantive matter rather than an administrative preference.

## Pros and Cons

The D7 buys the same residence permit and the same naturalisation clock as a EUR 500,000 Golden Visa fund, for an income test of roughly EUR 920 a month and no capital lock. What it costs instead is presence: the D7 expects you to actually live in Portugal, with the tax consequences that follow.

Advantages:

- No capital lock-up, unlike the fund-based Golden Visa route
- Family reunification available under the standard rules
- Straightforward path to the resident IMT scale and the refund route
- Genuine residence, which is what most applicants want anyway
- Five-year path to citizenship eligibility with A2 Portuguese

Disadvantages:

- You have to actually live there, which is not a route for occasional visitors
- Income must be documented, which excludes people whose wealth is in assets rather than flows
- Consulate processing and AIMA appointments both take time
- IFICI does not shelter pensions, so the tax outcome for retirees is worse than under the old regime
- Renewal requires continued evidence of income and residence

## Risks and Red Flags

D7 refusals and renewal problems cluster around evidence rather than eligibility. Income that is genuinely passive but poorly documented, absences that exceeded the limits, and a tax residency date nobody planned are the three that recur across consulates.

- **Advice quoting a threshold below EUR 920.** It predates the 2026 minimum wage and the rest of that page is likely as stale.
- **Firms recommending a purchase before approval.** It commits capital and triggers the non-resident IMT rate before you know the visa is granted.
- **Manufactured income.** Buying a Portuguese rental property specifically to create qualifying income is visible and treated sceptically.
- **NHR promises.** Any adviser still selling the pension exemption is describing a regime closed to new applicants since the end of 2024.
- **Ignoring the tax residency date.** The gap between getting the card and being tax resident is where the IMT difference lives.

Checklist before you file:

- Twenty-four months of bank statements showing the income arriving
- Tax returns from your home country matching those credits
- A Portuguese address that will still be valid at the AIMA appointment
- Health insurance meeting the consulate's stated cover
- A decision, in writing to yourself, on whether you are buying this year or next

## Who the D7 Suits

The D7 suits households with documented, regular income that does not depend on working, and who intend to spend more than 183 days a year in Portugal. It fits badly for anyone wanting European residence without relocating, which is the problem the Golden Visa exists to solve at 100 times the capital.

**The retired couple on pensions.** The core case. Income is documented, regular and clearly passive. The main planning question is the pension tax position after IFICI, not the visa itself.

**The landlord with foreign property.** Rental income from outside Portugal qualifies cleanly. The complication is the treaty position between the property's country and Portugal; for British buyers that is covered in the [UK Portugal double taxation treaty guide](/guides/uk-portugal-double-taxation-treaty/).

**The dividend investor relocating.** Works, provided the distributions are regular rather than lumpy. The tax outcome after arrival needs modelling before, not after, the move.

Whether the income clears local costs is a separate question, set out in [the cost of living in Portugal](/move-to-portugal/cost-of-living/), and the retirement case specifically in [retiring in Portugal](/move-to-portugal/retire-in-portugal/).

**The remote employee.** Not a D7 case. Salary is active income, and the D8 exists for exactly this profile at a threshold of about EUR 3,680 a month, four times the minimum wage.
