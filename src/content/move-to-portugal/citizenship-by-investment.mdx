Quick Answer: Portugal does not sell citizenship and never did. It sells residence, and since 19 May 2026 that residence has to be held for ten years before most foreign nationals can apply for the passport, up from five.

<TldrBlock text="Lei Organica 1/2026, published 18 May 2026 and in force from 19 May, replaced the five-year naturalisation rule with seven years for EU and CPLP nationals and ten for everyone else. The clock now starts when the residence permit is issued, not when it was applied for. Only nationality applications already filed on 19 May 2026 keep the old terms. Real estate stopped qualifying for the Golden Visa in October 2023. Permanent residency at five years is unchanged and is what most property buyers actually need." />

The phrase carries a promise Portugal has never made. Citizenship by investment, in the sense the Caribbean programmes use it, means paying a defined sum and receiving a passport at the end of a defined process. That is a product, and Portugal has never sold it.

What Portugal sells is a residence permit in exchange for a qualifying investment. Naturalisation comes later, from a separate law, on separate conditions, and in May 2026 those conditions changed more sharply than at any point since the Golden Visa began in 2012.

## What Changed on 19 May 2026

| Element | Before | From 19 May 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Residence required, EU and CPLP nationals | 5 years | 7 years |
| Residence required, all other nationals | 5 years | 10 years |
| Start of the clock | Date the permit was applied for | Date the permit is issued |
| What counts | Legal residence | Lawful residence under a valid permit only |
| Integration test | Language and basic conditions | Plus culture, history, symbols, civic duties |
| Declaration | Not required | Formal adherence to democratic principles |

The instrument is Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18 de maio, amending the Nationality Law of 1981. Parliament approved it on 1 April 2026 by 152 votes to 64, the President signed it on 3 May, and it applies to applications submitted on or after 19 May 2026.

The change to the clock deserves separate attention, because it is worth years on its own. A 2024 amendment had started the count at the date of application, which protected applicants from AIMA's own processing backlog. The 2026 law reverses that. Since permits have taken 18 to 24 months to issue, the backlog now sits in front of the ten years rather than inside them.

## Seven Years or Ten: Which Group You Are In

| Nationality | Group | Residence required |
|---|---|---|
| Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde and other CPLP states | CPLP | 7 years |
| Any EU member state | EU | 7 years |
| United States | Other | 10 years |
| United Kingdom | Other | 10 years |
| Canada | Other | 10 years |
| United Arab Emirates | Other | 10 years |

The United Kingdom is worth flagging. British applicants sat in the EU group until Brexit and sit in the ten-year group now, which for a British buyer weighing Portugal against Spain or Greece is a material difference that did not exist a decade ago. See [moving to Portugal from the UK](/move-to-portugal/from-uk/) for the rest of the position.

## Who Is Protected, and Who Is Not

Article 7 of the new law preserves the previous wording for administrative procedures pending on the date it entered into force. Read narrowly, and that is how it is being read, this protects a nationality application that was already filed on 19 May 2026.

It does not protect the larger group: people who hold a Portuguese residence permit, have accrued three or four years toward the old five-year mark, and had not yet reached the point of applying. For them the years already served do not carry across into a shorter route. They face the new period, counted from issuance.

| Situation on 19 May 2026 | Position |
|---|---|
| Nationality application already filed | Assessed under the previous law |
| Permanent residency already held | Unaffected; permanent residency is a separate status |
| Residence permit held, 4 years accrued | New period applies |
| Golden Visa approved, permit not yet issued | New period, counted from issuance |
| Application to AIMA pending, no permit | New period, counted from issuance |

This is the part that generated litigation. A challenge to the retroactive effect was filed by Golden Visa investors in December 2025 and remains undecided. Separately, a decree treating loss of nationality as a criminal penalty is suspended pending preventive constitutional review, after the Constitutional Court struck down several provisions in December 2025. Neither outcome should be assumed in a financial model.

## No EU Country Sells Passports Any More

No European Union member state now sells citizenship directly, and the date that changed is 29 April 2025. The context matters because buyers comparing Portugal against Malta or against a Caribbean programme are comparing three different products, only one of which still exists inside the EU.

On 29 April 2025 the Court of Justice of the European Union, sitting as the Grand Chamber, held in Commission v Malta that Malta's investor citizenship scheme was incompatible with EU law. The Court found the scheme amounted to a commercialisation of Union citizenship and breached the obligation of sincere cooperation between member states. Malta's programme closed that day, and with it the last genuine golden passport inside the EU.

That ruling is the reason the phrase "citizenship by investment" now means something different in Europe than it does in St Kitts or Antigua. In the EU there is investment migration, which buys residence, and there is naturalisation, which is earned. The two are joined only by time.

## The Routes That Still Lead to Residence

Since October 2023 the Golden Visa has excluded real estate acquisition and capital transfer. Funds that themselves invest in Portuguese real estate are excluded too, which closed the obvious workaround. AIMA's current guidance lists five qualifying routes: investment funds, company investment, arts and heritage support, research funding, and job creation.

| Route | Capital | Physical presence | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMVM investment fund | EUR 500,000 | About 7 days a year on average | Capital-rich, staying abroad |
| Job creation | 10 posts | Operational involvement | Founders and operators |
| D7 | Passive income, no lump sum | Substantial, over 183 days | Retirees, landlords, dividend income |
| D8 | Remote income threshold | Substantial, over 183 days | Remote employees and freelancers |

The fund route is the one most investors mean when they say Golden Visa, and its mechanics are set out in [Portugal Golden Visa investment funds](/guides/portugal-golden-visa-fund-investment-2026/). The full route list and what closed in 2023 sit in [the Golden Visa real estate route, ended](/guides/portugal-golden-visa-real-estate-ended/), and the non-investment alternatives in [residency options without the Golden Visa](/guides/portugal-residency-options-without-golden-visa/).

The comparison worth making is not between routes to a passport, since they all end at the same nationality law. It is between the cost of getting the residence permit in the first place. A D7 applicant with a pension reaches the identical ten-year clock without locking half a million euros in a fund.

## The Real Timeline, End to End

The honest end-to-end figure for a non-EU investor starting in 2026 is about 13 to 14 years, roughly double what the same calculation produced before May 2026. Two of the four stages are outside anyone's control, and both of them sit at the ends of the sequence rather than in the middle.

For a non-EU, non-CPLP investor starting the fund route in 2026:

| Stage | Duration | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| NIF, bank account, source of funds, subscription | 2 to 4 months | 4 months |
| AIMA processing until the permit is issued | 18 to 24 months | 22 to 28 months |
| Ten years of legal residence, counted from issuance | 120 months | 142 to 148 months |
| Naturalisation processing at the Conservatoria | 12 to 24 months | 154 to 172 months |
| Total | | About 13 to 14 years |

Two figures in that table are outside anyone's control, and both sit at the ends. The AIMA wait is now purely dead time, because the clock does not start until it finishes. The Conservatória wait comes after the ten years are complete.

Permanent residency arrives far earlier, at five years, and the 2026 law left it alone. For most buyers that is the milestone that actually changes daily life: an indefinite right to live in Portugal, without the passport. Treating it as the objective and citizenship as an optional second stage is the realistic way to plan.

## What Property Has To Do With It

Property has had nothing to do with Portuguese residency since October 2023, and saying otherwise is the most common misrepresentation in this market. A purchase is not an immigration step, does not shorten the 10-year clock, and qualifies for no Golden Visa route, including indirectly through funds that hold Portuguese real estate.

What buying does affect is where you live while the clock runs, and that is not trivial: the D7 and D8 both require substantial physical presence, so a residence to live in is a practical necessity rather than an immigration instrument. Non-residents completing on or after 1 September 2026 pay a flat 7.5% IMT plus 0.8% stamp duty, set out in [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/), and the regional picture is in [property for sale in Portugal](/property-for-sale/).

Buy the property because you want the property. Choose the residence route on its own arithmetic. Anyone selling the two as a single package is selling a product that stopped existing three years ago.

## The Conditions Beyond the Clock

Time is necessary and not sufficient, and the other conditions tightened alongside it in May 2026. A certified A2 Portuguese test, a clean criminal record, documented residence under a valid permit throughout, a broadened integration test and a formal declaration of adherence to democratic principles all apply.

Time is necessary and not sufficient. The other conditions have also tightened.

- **A2 Portuguese.** A certified language test at A2 level of the Common European Framework, unchanged in level.
- **Clean criminal record.** Assessed against Portuguese sentencing thresholds, in Portugal and in every country of prior residence.
- **Integration.** The 2026 law added knowledge of Portuguese culture, history, national symbols, and civic rights and duties.
- **Declaration.** A formal affirmation of adherence to the democratic principles of the Portuguese state.
- **Documented residence.** Only periods covered by a valid permit count, so a gap between a permit expiring and a renewal issuing is a gap in the count.

That last point is administrative rather than legal, and it is where a decade-long plan is most easily lost. Renewal gaps caused by AIMA scheduling are common, and under the new counting rule they are no longer harmless.

## What the Extra Five Years Cost

Doubling the naturalisation period from 5 years to 10 does not change the EUR 500,000 headline, and it does double most of what sits on top: management charges, renewal cycles, legal fees and the opportunity cost of the capital. That second layer is the part rarely priced at the outset.

| Component | Over 5 years | Over 10 years |
|---|---|---|
| Capital locked | EUR 500,000 | EUR 500,000, for twice as long |
| Fund management charges | Five annual cycles | Ten annual cycles |
| Renewal legal fees | Two renewals | Four or five renewals |
| Government and biometric fees | Per renewal cycle | Per renewal cycle, doubled |
| Opportunity cost on the capital | Five years | Ten years |
| Legislative exposure | Five years of risk | Ten years of risk |

The last line is the one that has just proved itself. Anyone who subscribed to a fund in 2021 on a five-year plan is now looking at a ten-year one, without having done anything differently. The same could happen again between now and 2036, in either direction.

The practical response is to price the fund on its own investment merits rather than on the passport at the end. If a EUR 500,000 CMVM fund is a position you would hold anyway, the residence permit is a benefit on top. If it is only tolerable because of a passport a decade away, the plan carries a decade of legislative risk with no hedge.

## Renewals Are Where Plans Break

Ten years of legal residence means ten years of unbroken documented residence, and the counting rule makes that stricter than it sounds. Permits are renewed on a statutory cycle, each renewal needs refreshed criminal checks, proof the qualifying investment is maintained, and evidence of the stay requirement.

A gap between one permit expiring and the next being issued is now a gap in the count, not an administrative inconvenience. Given AIMA's scheduling record, the safe assumption is that renewals must be started early and chased, every time, for a decade. Budget legal representation for each cycle rather than for the initial application alone.

## Pros and Cons

Portugal is the only one of the three main European programmes where a low-presence investor accrues a naturalisation clock at all, at roughly 7 days a year. What it now costs is time: 10 years for most non-EU nationals, with 18 to 24 months of AIMA processing sitting in front of that decade rather than inside it.

Advantages of the Portuguese route:

- Permanent residency at five years is unchanged and is what most households need
- The fund route requires about seven days a year of presence, the lightest in Europe
- Family members are included, spouse and minor children on every route
- The passport, when it arrives, carries full EU free movement
- The rules are published law rather than discretionary programme terms

Disadvantages:

- Ten years for most non-EU nationals, double the previous period
- Years already served under the old rule do not shorten the new one
- The AIMA wait is dead time, sitting in front of the clock
- Two live legal challenges mean the position may move again
- EUR 500,000 locked in an illiquid fund for a horizon that just doubled

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Any adviser quoting five years.** That rule ended on 19 May 2026. It is the fastest way to identify material written before the change and not updated since.
- **"Your existing years count."** They do not, unless your nationality application was already filed.
- **Property sold as a residency route.** Closed in October 2023, including through real estate funds.
- **A timeline that omits AIMA.** Eighteen to twenty-four months before the clock even starts is not a rounding error.
- **A plan that only works if the law holds.** It has moved twice in two years, in 2024 and again in 2026.

Checklist before committing capital:

- Which group your nationality falls in, seven years or ten
- Whether permanent residency at five years already meets your actual objective
- The fund's CMVM registration, and its exclusion from Portuguese real estate
- Written confirmation of how renewal gaps will be handled and evidenced
- Your position if the constitutional challenge succeeds, and if it fails

## Who This Suits

The right route depends entirely on whether the objective is a base or a passport, and most enquiries conflate the two. Permanent residency at 5 years is unchanged and meets what most households actually need; citizenship is a separate, longer and now less certain second stage that only some buyers genuinely require.

**Households who want to live in Portugal.** The five-year permanent residency milestone is intact, and it is the one that matters day to day. See [the D7 visa](/move-to-portugal/d7-visa/) if your income is passive.

**Investors who want EU optionality with minimal presence.** The fund route still does that, at EUR 500,000 and about seven days a year, provided you can hold the position for a decade. The mechanics are in [the Golden Visa guide](/move-to-portugal/golden-visa/).

**Americans weighing Portugal against alternatives.** The ten-year clock changes the comparison materially, and the rest of the position is in [moving to Portugal from the US](/move-to-portugal/from-usa/).

**Buyers who want a passport quickly.** Portugal is not that, and since the Malta ruling no EU country is. Anyone promising otherwise inside the EU is describing a programme that no longer exists.
