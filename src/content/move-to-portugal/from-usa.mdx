The guides that rank for the first question, from tax firms to moving companies to the embassy, cover visas, shipping and healthcare well. ## Yes, You Can Buy. That Is the Easy Part

Portugal imposes no nationality restriction on residential property. An American buys on the same legal terms as a Portuguese citizen: freehold title, registered at the Conservatória do Registo Predial, with the same protections and the same obligations.

You do not need a visa to buy. You do not need to be resident. You do not need permission. What you need is administrative:

- A NIF, the Portuguese tax number, which comes before everything else
- A Portuguese bank account for the transfer and the ongoing charges
- A lawyer, independent of the agent and the seller, to run title and encumbrance checks
- Evidence of the source of funds, which banks and notaries both require

The sequence is set out in [how to buy property in Portugal step by step](/guides/how-to-buy-property-portugal-step-by-step/) and the foreign buyer specifics in [buying property in Portugal as a foreigner](/guides/buy-property-portugal-foreigner/). Start with [the NIF](/guides/nif-portugal-property-purchase/), because nothing else moves until it exists.

## What It Actually Costs a Non-Resident American

| Cost | Rate or basis | On a EUR 500,000 home |
|---|---|---|
| IMT transfer tax, non-resident | Flat 7.5% on residential property from 1 September 2026 | EUR 37,500 |
| Stamp duty | 0.8% of price | EUR 4,000 |
| Notary and land registry | Fixed and variable elements | Low four figures |
| Legal fees | Commonly around 1% to 1.5% | EUR 5,000 to EUR 7,500 |
| Total acquisition cost | Roughly 9% to 11% of price | Around EUR 47,000 to EUR 55,000 |

The flat 7.5% non-resident rate is the number that catches American buyers. A Portuguese tax resident buying the same home moves along a progressive scale that starts far lower. The full mechanics are in [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

There is a way back. Become Portuguese tax resident within 24 months of the purchase and satisfy the 183-day presence test, and the excess IMT can be reclaimed. See [the IMT refund route](/guides/imt-refund-tax-resident-portugal/). Two cautions: you fund the higher amount first, and the refund only works if you genuinely relocate.

## Which Visa Fits an American Household

| Route | Test | Presence expected | Capital locked |
|---|---|---|---|
| D7 passive income | About EUR 920 a month, pensions, dividends, foreign rents | High, genuine residence | None |
| D8 remote work | About EUR 3,680 a month from a foreign employer or clients | Medium to high | None |
| Golden Visa fund | EUR 500,000 into a CMVM-regulated fund | About 7 days a year | Yes, five years |

Both income thresholds track the Portuguese minimum wage, which is EUR 920 a month in 2026. The D8 test is four times that.

Each route has its own guide: [the Golden Visa](/move-to-portugal/golden-visa/), [the D7](/move-to-portugal/d7-visa/) and [the D8](/move-to-portugal/d8-digital-nomad-visa/).

The Golden Visa is the route Americans ask about most and need least. It stopped accepting property in October 2023 under Law 56/2023, and for anyone actually relocating, the D7 or D8 delivers the same residence rights without tying up half a million euros. It earns its place only when you want EU residence and are not moving.

## The Part Nobody Else Covers: Two Tax Systems

The United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live. That does not stop when you move, and it does not stop when you become Portuguese tax resident. It stops if you renounce, and that is a different conversation.

So an American who owns a home in Portugal and lives there is inside two systems at once:

| Event | Portugal | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Buying | IMT and stamp duty at the deed | Nothing at purchase; the basis is set for later |
| Holding | IMI annually, AIMI above EUR 600,000 of taxable value | Foreign asset reporting obligations may apply |
| Letting | Portuguese-source rental income taxed in Portugal | Rental income reported on the federal return, foreign tax credit available |
| Selling | Portuguese capital gains treatment | US capital gains, with currency movement affecting the calculation |

The last line is the one that surprises people. The US calculates gain in dollars. If the euro moves against the dollar between purchase and sale, you can record a taxable dollar gain on a property that barely moved in euros. That is not a Portuguese problem and no Portuguese adviser will raise it.

The Portuguese side is covered in [capital gains tax on Portuguese property](/guides/portugal-capital-gains-tax-property/) and [rental income tax for non-residents](/guides/non-resident-rental-income-tax-portugal/). The American side needs a US cross-border adviser, and the two need to be talking to each other.

### IFICI is not the old NHR

A large share of American relocation advice still describes NHR, the regime that gave favourable treatment to foreign pensions. It closed to new applicants at the end of 2024.

Its replacement, IFICI, applies a flat rate to qualifying Portuguese employment and self-employment income for ten consecutive years, and it excludes pensions and Portuguese rental income. For a retiree, that exclusion is the whole story. See [IFICI, the NHR replacement](/guides/ifici-portugal-nhr-2-tax-regime/).

If your move was modelled on the pension treatment under NHR, the model needs redoing.

## What It Costs to Live There, Against a US Baseline

About 590 Americans a month search specifically for the Portugal versus United States cost comparison, and it is the number that decides most moves.

The honest answer has three parts. Housing is dramatically cheaper than a coastal US metro and roughly comparable to a mid-sized inland American city, with Lisbon the exception at the top. Healthcare and utilities are cheaper by a wide margin. Imported goods, cars and electronics are more expensive, and salaries, if you were ever to earn locally, are far lower.

| Category | Direction against a US coastal city | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Housing, rent | Materially lower | Lisbon narrows the gap, Porto and inland widen it |
| Healthcare | Substantially lower | Private cover costs a fraction of US premiums |
| Utilities and internet | Lower | Winter heating in older stock is the exception |
| Groceries | Lower | Imported and speciality items are not |
| Cars and fuel | Higher | Vehicle taxes are significant |
| Local salaries | Far lower | Irrelevant on D7 and D8, decisive if you ever work locally |

Our [cost of living in Portugal guide](/move-to-portugal/cost-of-living/) breaks this down by city and separates renting from owning, which matters once IMI, condominium charges and maintenance enter the budget.

## The Practical Move

Beyond visas and property, four items consume more time than Americans expect.

Banking comes first and is slower than it looks. Portuguese banks apply full source-of-funds diligence to US citizens, and FATCA reporting obligations make some institutions reluctant. Allow weeks, not days, and start before you need the account.

Healthcare registration follows residence. Private insurance is required for the visa application, and residents can also register with the public system once they have a residence permit and a social security number. Most American households run both for the first year.

Driving licences must be exchanged rather than simply used. The window for doing so is limited and starts from your residence date, and missing it means taking a Portuguese test.

Shipping and pets are the visible costs and the easiest to plan. The less visible one is what you should not ship: appliances on the wrong voltage, and furniture sized for rooms that do not exist in Portuguese apartments.

## Pros and Cons of Buying Early

Advantages of buying in year one:

- You lock a price in a market that has been rising
- No landlord risk, no lease renewal, no forced move
- The purchase can proceed while the visa is still in process
- Mortgage rates for non-residents are available, covered in [Portugal mortgage rates for foreigners](/guides/portugal-mortgage-rates-foreigners-2026/)

Disadvantages of buying in year one:

- Flat 7.5% IMT instead of the resident scale, a five-figure difference on most purchases
- You are choosing a neighbourhood you have not lived in
- Capital committed before the visa is granted
- Selling costs and Portuguese capital gains treatment make a quick reversal expensive

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Advice that treats the Golden Visa as a property route.** It has not been one since October 2023. Anyone still selling that package is three years out of date.
- **Any page quoting NHR pension benefits.** Closed to new applicants at the end of 2024.
- **A single adviser covering both tax systems.** Almost nobody is genuinely qualified on both. Insist on two, and insist they speak to each other.
- **Buying before the visa decision.** It can work, but it converts a reversible decision into an expensive one.
- **Agents who represent the seller.** In Portugal the agent's commission comes from the vendor. Your lawyer is the only party working for you.

Checklist before you commit:

- NIF issued and bank account open
- Lawyer instructed independently, not introduced by the selling agent
- Source of funds file assembled in a form a Portuguese bank will accept
- Purchase modelled at the non-resident IMT rate, not the resident scale
- US adviser briefed on the intended purchase before the promissory contract is signed

## Who This Works For

**The retiring couple on pensions.** D7 fits, the income test is comfortable, and the planning question is the pension tax position after IFICI rather than the visa. Rent for a year, buy once resident, and take the resident IMT scale.

**The remote employee in their thirties or forties.** D8 fits. Keep renting while the employment contract is the thing holding the visa up, and revisit buying in year two.

**The family with school-age children.** The visa is the easy part. School places and their cost are the constraint, and they vary sharply between Lisbon, Cascais and Porto. Solve that before choosing a neighbourhood.

**The investor who is not moving.** Buy the property on its merits, price in the flat 7.5% IMT, and treat residence as a separate decision. Do not buy a home in order to get a visa, because that route closed in 2023.

Cascais and Estoril take a disproportionate share of American buyers, on the strength of international schools, coastline and a thirty-minute train into Lisbon. Porto costs materially less for the same specification. Both are worth a year of renting before a deed.
