## Your future competition is a public document

In most property markets the question "what will be built next to me" has no answer. Applications arrive parcel by parcel, from owners nobody can enumerate, and a buyer is reduced to looking at gaps on a map and hoping. Vilamoura is different in kind, not degree, because it was conceived as a single planned resort and its development runs under an approved urbanisation plan that fixes land use and building parameters across the area.

For a buyer, this converts a speculation into a search. The empty ground behind a block is not an unknown; it is a parcel with a designated use and a density, and both are stated. If the plan permits a further apartment phase there, that phase is your competition on resale whether or not anyone has broken ground. If the plan permits open space or golf, it is not.

This is the single most valuable and least used piece of Vilamoura due diligence. It costs nothing beyond the time to read a planning document, it is available before any offer, and it answers a question that in Albufeira or Lagos genuinely cannot be answered.

## What the plan fixes, and what it leaves open

| The plan settles | The plan does not settle |
|---|---|
| Which uses are permitted on each parcel | When a landowner chooses to build |
| Density and building parameters | What the finished product will be priced at |
| The structure of open space and infrastructure | How a phase is marketed or to whom |
| Where further residential capacity exists | Whether financing supports a start |
| What cannot appear at all | How long a build takes once begun |
| The framework a revision would have to change | Whether a revision will be sought |

The left column is why the exercise is worth doing. The right column is why it is not a crystal ball, and buyers who overread a plan make the opposite error to those who ignore it: they treat permitted capacity as scheduled supply and panic about competition that no one has any commercial reason to build for another 10 years.

The correct reading sits between. Permitted capacity is a ceiling on how much can compete with you, and the position of that capacity tells you which properties are exposed to it. A resale surrounded by parcels designated for open space or existing golf has a structurally different competitive position from one surrounded by parcels still zoned for apartments, and the 2 can be identical in size, condition and asking price.

## Why concentration cuts both ways

A planned resort tends to leave remaining development capacity in relatively few hands, because the land was assembled as a unit before it was built out. That structure has consequences a buyer should hold honestly rather than optimistically.

- **Supply arrives in phases, not in a trickle.** A single owner releasing a phase adds competing stock in a block rather than gradually, which matters most to anyone selling in the same 12 months.
- **A commercial owner has an incentive to protect pricing.** Releasing into a weak market damages their own inventory, so restraint is often rational rather than benevolent.
- **Infrastructure is maintained to a resort standard**, which is a genuine amenity and a genuine cost, and it reaches owners through service arrangements alongside the ordinary [condominium budget](/guides/condominium-fees-portugal/).
- **Decisions about the resort's shared assets are commercial decisions**, taken by owners of those assets rather than voted by residents.

None of that is a reason to avoid the resort. It is the reason its stock behaves differently from an ordinary Algarve town's, and it explains why comparisons between the two on price per square metre alone tell you very little.

## The charging structure is the second document

Buyers arriving from an ordinary apartment market underestimate this consistently. In a planned resort, part of what maintains the environment sits outside the individual building: roads, landscaping, lighting, water features, security across the estate. Those are maintained, they cost money, and the money reaches owners through arrangements that are not simply a condominium quota.

The practical instruction is to obtain 2 documents rather than 1 before committing:

1. **The building's own condominium budget and accounts**, read as anywhere else in Portugal, with the reserve fund position checked against the age of the expensive components.
2. **The resort-level charging arrangement**, read for what it covers, how it is set, and how it has moved over the last 3 years.

Buyer scenario. Two apartments at the same price, one inside the resort and one in a conventional block 4 km away. The resort unit carries a higher total annual charge and a maintained environment that supports its resale to buyers who want exactly that. The conventional unit carries a lower charge and competes with every other apartment in the municipality. Over a 10 years hold, the resort unit's premium is defensible if the environment is maintained and indefensible if it is not, and the accounts are where that question is answered rather than the brochure.

## How to read remaining capacity in an afternoon

The exercise sounds technical and is not. It takes an afternoon, needs no professional, and answers the question that most determines a resale price 10 years out.

1. **Fix the property's position on the plan's zoning map**, then draw a circle covering everything visible from it and everything within a 5 minutes walk.
2. **List each parcel in that circle by its designated use.** Residential capacity, open space, golf, commercial: each has a different implication for you.
3. **Note the density parameters** where residential capacity remains, since a parcel permitting 12 units and one permitting 60 are not the same neighbour.
4. **Ask the câmara what is already licensed** on those parcels, which converts permitted capacity into probable capacity.
5. **Write down what cannot happen.** This is the half buyers skip, and it is the half that supports price: a parcel designated open space is a view you have actually bought.

The output is a single paragraph you can put in front of a decision: what can appear, how much of it, and what is protected. Anyone selling you the property will describe the view. Only the plan tells you whether the view is a designation or an accident of timing.

## Where the resort sits against its neighbours

| Question | Vilamoura | An ordinary Algarve town |
|---|---|---|
| Can you know future nearby supply? | Yes, from the plan | Not reliably |
| Who decides when it arrives | Commercial landowners, in phases | Many small owners, gradually |
| Who maintains the setting | The resort structure, funded by owners | The municipality, funded by IMI |
| Total annual charges | Higher, and not optional | Lower, and mostly building-level |
| What the premium buys | Environment and predictability | Location alone |
| Exit pool | Buyers who want a resort | Any second-home or resident buyer |

The last 2 rows are the trade. A resort premium is a payment for an environment somebody keeps up and for a supply picture you can read. Both are real, both are worth money, and both stop being worth money if maintenance slips. That is why the accounts matter more here than the brochure, and why 3 years of charging history tells you more about the next decade than any yield quote.

## What a resort premium has to keep earning

The premium on Vilamoura stock over an equivalent flat outside it is not a brand charge. It pays for a physical environment that somebody maintains continuously, and it stops being defensible the moment that maintenance falters. That makes the premium a subscription rather than a purchase, and it is worth underwriting as one.

Over a hold of 10 years the question is not whether the resort is attractive today. It is whether the arrangements that keep it attractive are funded, governed and stable. Three things carry that answer, and all 3 are documentary:

- **The trend in charges over the last 3 years**, which shows whether costs are being met or deferred.
- **The reserve position at building level**, since deferred capital work is the standard way a resort environment degrades slowly and then suddenly.
- **How decisions about shared assets are taken**, because an owner buying into a resort accepts that some of the environment is outside their vote.

A buyer who checks those and is satisfied is paying a premium for something real. A buyer who checks none of them is paying it on the strength of the landscaping visible on the day of the viewing, which is exactly the input least likely to still be true in year 8.

## Pros, cons and the checklist

The advantages are substantial and specific: a maintained resort environment, marina and golf infrastructure that individual owners could never fund, a long-established international buyer pool, and — uniquely in the Algarve — a readable answer to what can still be built nearby. Letting demand is deep and skews to a higher-spending visitor than the mass-tourism towns.

The disadvantages are the mirror image. Total running costs are higher than an equivalent flat outside the resort, and the difference is not optional. Supply timing sits with commercial owners rather than with the market. And the price premium is a premium for maintenance and setting, which means it depends on both being kept up over the whole of a hold period.

**Before an offer, in this order:**

1. Read the urbanisation plan for the parcels within sight of the property, not for the resort as a whole.
2. Establish what is already licensed nearby, which is a câmara question rather than a plan question.
3. Obtain the condominium accounts and the resort-level charging arrangement together.
4. Check the reserve position against the age of lifts, roofs and pool plant, per the [condominium guide](/guides/condominium-fees-portugal/).
5. Run the standard title pack through a Portuguese lawyer, per the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-portugal-property/); for an off-plan phase, add the protections in the [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-property-portugal-guide/).
6. Model running costs at the resort level rather than at the national average, using the [maintenance cost guide](/guides/property-maintenance-costs-portugal/) as the floor rather than the estimate.

Insider tip: walk the boundary of the parcel behind the building and then look it up. Do it before the second viewing rather than after the offer, because the answer sometimes changes which unit in the same block you want.

One further habit is worth forming here and nowhere else on this coast. Because the plan is public and the resort is finite, it is possible to rank the stock by exposure rather than by finish: units whose outlook is protected by designation, units whose outlook is currently open ground with residential capacity, and units already enclosed by existing buildings. Those 3 categories behave differently on resale in a way that the estate agent's description of a kitchen never captures, and the ranking survives redecoration, changes of ownership and 2 or 3 market cycles. Buyers reliably assume that open ground inside a resort is permanent because it looks landscaped, and landscaping is not a land use. The plan will tell you in 5 minutes whether that view is protected by designation or merely by the fact that nobody has built there yet, and those are completely different things to have paid for.
