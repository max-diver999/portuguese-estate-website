## The document that is not about your apartment

Most Algarve due diligence is about the unit: the caderneta predial, the licença de utilização, the registry extract, the condominium's accounts. In Albufeira there is one more, and it belongs to somebody else. Every commercial premises operates under a municipal opening-hours licence, the horário de funcionamento, which states when it may open and when it must close. That licence is granted under the national regime for commercial establishments and administered locally, which means the câmara sets the framework by regulation and then applies it premises by premises.

For an owner two floors up, this is the whole question. A bar licensed until the early hours is a different neighbour from the same bar licensed to close before midnight, and nothing about the apartment changes between those two cases. The apartment's own paperwork will be identical. The price per square metre will be identical. The seasonal letting numbers will look identical on a spreadsheet built in February.

There are 2 counterparties here and buyers routinely address the wrong one. Those who worry about noise are told the condominium can deal with it, and the condominium cannot. An assembly governs the building's common parts and the conduct of its own owners; it has real powers, including over how owners use their fractions, and it has no power at all over the licence of a commercial tenant answering to the municipality. Two counterparties, two procedures, and only one of them meets you at the assembly.

## Where the perimeter runs

Albufeira also does something few Portuguese municipalities do: it regulates behaviour in public space directly, through a conduct regulation that applies over a defined area rather than across the whole council. The regulation carries an annexed map, and the map is the operative part. An address inside the perimeter sits in a zone the council has decided needs active policing of street behaviour; an address outside it is governed by the ordinary noise and public-order regime like anywhere else in Portugal.

The temptation is to read the perimeter as a warning label. It is better read as a disclosure. Inside it, the council has told you in writing where it expects trouble and has taken powers to deal with it. Outside it, you have no such statement either way. Neither position prices itself automatically, and an agent who tells you the regulation "protects" a street is over-reading a document that mostly defines where enforcement attention goes.

What the perimeter does reliably is sort the housing stock into two underwriting problems:

- **Inside, with commercial premises in the building.** The licence position dominates. Everything else is secondary, including the view.
- **Inside, without commercial premises in the building.** Street-level noise carries, but you are not exposed to a single licence renewal.
- **Outside, near the tourist core.** The general regime applies, and the practical protection is distance and building fabric rather than regulation.
- **Outside, in the residential fringe.** The question stops being noise and becomes winter demand, which is a different page's argument entirely.

## What can change after you own it?

An opening-hours licence is not a permanent state of the world. Municipal regulation can be revised, zones can be treated differently, and an individual licence can be conditioned or withdrawn following enforcement. That cuts both ways, and honest underwriting has to hold both directions at once.

The 2 directions are not symmetrical, and the asymmetry is the thing to plan around:

- **A tightening** improves life above the premises and can lift the residential appeal of stock bought at nightlife prices. It arrives slowly and publicly, through a consultation and a published regulation, so an owner can see it coming and take part in it.
- **A relaxation, or simply a change of tenant** from a restaurant to a late bar under the same licence class, puts noise under a bedroom that was quiet on the day you viewed. It can arrive with a new lease and no notice to you whatsoever.

For a buyer, that argues for a specific habit: treat the horário as a term of the purchase, not a piece of background colour. Ask what it is now, ask what class of premises it covers, and read the last two assembly minutes for whether soundproofing works or complaints are already running. A building that has argued about noise before will have left a paper trail, and that trail is more predictive than any conversation on a viewing.

## Prices, seasonality and where the licence sits in the model

Albufeira's mainstream apartment and townhouse stock trades in a band commonly quoted between €3,400 and €4,400 per square metre, with Old Town walk-to-restaurant units and front-line Praia da Oura stock above it and inland parishes below. Gross yields on well-run letting sit in the 4% to 6% range before costs, and the gap between that headline and the realised figure is the usual one: IMI, condominium charges, management, platform fees and non-resident rental tax all land before anything reaches the owner. The [gross versus net yield guide](/guides/gross-vs-net-yield-portugal/) sets out that arithmetic; it is not repeated here.

What the licence position changes is not the band but the variance inside it.

| Position in the block | What the buyer is actually exposed to | What to obtain before the CPCV |
|---|---|---|
| Directly above licensed late-night premises | The horário and any conditions attached to it | Current licence terms, class of premises, enforcement history |
| Same building, upper floor, street side | Aggregate street noise plus the same licence | Assembly minutes on soundproofing and complaints |
| Same building, courtyard or rear aspect | Building fabric more than licensing | Window and glazing specification, any works decided |
| Adjacent building inside the perimeter | Public-space conduct enforcement | The regulation's annexed map for the exact address |
| Residential block outside the perimeter | Winter occupancy rather than noise | Two prior years of letting statements, if let |
| Inland parish stock | Long-let demand and thin peak premium | Local tenancy comparables, not resort nightly rates |

Non-resident acquisition cost is a national question rather than an Albufeira one: from 1 September 2026 a flat 7.5% IMT applies under DL 97/2026, with stamp duty at 0.8% on top, and the arithmetic for any price point is in the [non-resident IMT guide](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/). Short-let registration is likewise national, with municipal overlays; the [Alojamento Local guide](/guides/alojamento-local-license-portugal/) carries the regime and the condominium's power to object, which is a separate question from the licence discussed here and is frequently confused with it.

## How the exposure shows up across the letting year

The licence question is not evenly distributed through the calendar, and that is what makes it easy to underestimate at a viewing. Albufeira's late-night trade concentrates in a season that runs roughly from May to September, with the sharpest weeks in July and August. A buyer who views in February hears an empty street and prices an apartment that does not exist for 5 months of the year.

The inverse error is just as common. An owner who lets short-term through the season and visits in winter never experiences the asset the way a long-term tenant or an eventual owner-occupier buyer would, so the exposure stays invisible until exit, when the resale pool turns out to be narrower than the purchase pool was.

Three practical consequences follow, and none of them is about the price per square metre:

- **A year-round tenancy on a licensed street prices differently from a seasonal let.** Long-term tenants discount for noise every month; a short-let guest in August is buying the noise on purpose. Two strategies on the same unit produce two different rents for the same reason.
- **The winter months are when building works get voted.** Soundproofing, glazing and façade decisions pass at assemblies held out of season, which is why the last 2 sets of minutes matter more than the current year's budget.
- **Enforcement history is seasonal too.** Complaints and inspections cluster in the same 4 or 5 months, so a licence that survived 2 consecutive summers without incident is telling you more than one that has only been tested in winter.

## What do you actually do when the position changes?

Owners discover a licensing change in one of two ways: a published municipal decision, or a new tenant behind an unchanged shopfront. The first arrives with a procedure and a consultation window. The second arrives on a Friday night.

Neither is a reason to avoid the market, and each has its own route:

1. **A regulation under revision** carries a public consultation, and an owner can participate in it directly.
2. **A specific premises breaching its terms** is a complaint to the câmara supported by evidence, not a condominium motion, because the assembly has no jurisdiction over the licence.
3. **A building that transmits noise regardless** is a works question, and works are voted by owners at the assembly.

Owners who take route 3 to the municipality, or route 2 to the assembly, lose months learning which door they were standing at.

Where the building itself is the weak point, the lever sits with the assembly after all, but on a different question: glazing, façade insulation and the sealing of common-part openings are building works, and works are voted by owners. That is the one place where the two counterparties meet, and it is worth knowing which of your two problems you actually have before you start arguing at the wrong meeting.

## Pros, cons and who this actually suits

The advantages of Albufeira for an investor are real and rarely the ones in brochures. Tourism density is deep and long-established, which means letting demand is not speculative and exit liquidity to British and Irish second-home buyers is genuine. Service infrastructure is in English. Entry per square metre on older apartment stock sits below the Golden Triangle.

The disadvantages are equally specific. Seasonality is sharp, so a model built on peak weeks flatters the year. The reputational overlay caps the buyer pool on exit for anyone seeking quiet retirement stock. And the licensing exposure described above is genuinely uninsurable: no survey reveals it, no condominium fixes it, and it does not appear in the price.

Buyer scenario, and the reason to run it before the offer rather than after: two apartments of the same size, the same age and the same asking price, one above a licensed late bar and one four doors along above a bakery, are not the same asset and will not have the same owner-occupier resale pool. If you intend to let year-round or ever to live there, that difference is worth more than the per-square-metre negotiation most buyers spend their energy on.

**Verification checklist before signing:**

1. Current horário in force for every commercial unit in the building and either side.
2. The conduct regulation's annexed map, checked against the exact address rather than the street name.
3. Last two condominium assembly minutes, read for noise complaints, soundproofing works and live disputes.
4. RNAL position if a short-let strategy is intended, plus the condominium's stance on it.
5. Standard title pack — caderneta predial, certidão de teor, licença de utilização — through a Portuguese lawyer, as set out in the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-portugal-property/).
6. Whether any works on the building's façade or glazing have been voted and funded, which is the assembly's lever on street noise.

Red flag: a seller who cannot say what the ground-floor premises is licensed for. That is not a gap in their knowledge, it is a gap in yours, and it is the one item on this page that cannot be resolved after the deposit has moved. Neighbouring markets price differently for reasons that have nothing to do with licensing — [Lagos](/areas/lagos-property-investment/) on water, [Portimão](/areas/portimao-property-investment/) on tourist-development title — and each is worth reading before deciding the Algarve is one market.
