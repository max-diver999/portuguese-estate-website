Quick Answer: Lisbon is the most expensive market in Portugal and the one where the district decides almost everything. The historic core, the regeneration districts, the modern riverfront and the Cascais line are four different products at four different prices, and short-term letting is restricted across much of the centre.

Lisbon decoupled from the rest of Portugal after 2015. Foreign demand met constrained supply in a city whose historic core cannot physically expand, and the result is a market where the ratio between local wages and housing costs is unusually wide.

For a buyer that has one practical consequence: the district is the decision. A budget that buys a small flat in Chiado buys a family apartment in Parque das Nacoes or a house with a garden on the Cascais line, and those are not variations on one choice.

Search demand splits along the same line. `lisbon real estate` runs at 1,300 a month in the United States and `lisbon portugal real estate` at 1,000, while British buyers search `lisbon property for sale` at 590 and `lisbon apartments for sale` at 390. Two vocabularies, one city, and both sets of buyers arrive at the same district question.

## The Four Lisbons

| Market | Districts | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Historic core | Baixa, Chiado, Principe Real, Alfama, Graca | Prime, oldest, most restricted for letting |
| Central residential | Avenidas Novas, Campo de Ourique, Estrela | Where Lisbon actually lives, lifts and schools |
| Regeneration and river | Alcantara, Marvila, Beato, Parque das Nacoes | More space per euro, newer stock, uneven in places |
| The line | Oeiras, Carcavelos, Cascais, Estoril | Space, schools, beaches, 20 to 40 minutes in |

## District by District

**Baixa, Chiado and Principe Real.** The prime historic core, and the top of the price per square metre range. Buildings are old, lifts are not guaranteed, and condominium and maintenance budgets are correspondingly heavy. Short-term letting is contained here.

**Alfama and Graca.** The postcard districts, and the most difficult logistically: narrow streets, limited parking, and buildings that frequently need work. Beautiful, and not the easy option.

**Avenidas Novas and Campo de Ourique.** Where residents actually buy. Lift-served apartment blocks, schools, shops and a metro line, at prices below the historic core for larger flats. The most straightforward part of central Lisbon to own.

**Parque das Nacoes.** Built for the 1998 Expo, so the whole district is modern: lifts, parking, wide pavements, river frontage, an international school and a metro terminus. It is the practical choice for families and the least Lisbon-looking part of Lisbon.

**Alcantara.** Former industrial riverfront to the west, in regeneration, with conversions and newer stock and a growing food and office scene. More space per euro than the core, and still uneven street by street.

**Marvila and Beato.** The eastern equivalent, earlier in the same trajectory. The upside is larger and so is the variance, which makes walking the specific street non-negotiable.

**Oeiras and Carcavelos.** On the Cascais line, roughly half an hour into the centre, with beaches, space, parking and the densest concentration of international schools in Portugal. The standard answer for relocating families, covered further in [international schools in Portugal](/move-to-portugal/international-schools/).

## What It Costs to Buy

| Cost | Basis | On a EUR 500,000 apartment |
|---|---|---|
| IMT, non-resident | Flat 7.5% from 1 September 2026 | EUR 37,500 |
| Stamp duty | 0.8% | EUR 4,000 |
| Notary and registry | Fixed and variable | Low four figures |
| Legal fees | Commonly 1% to 1.5% | EUR 5,000 to EUR 7,500 |
| Total | Roughly 9% to 11% | About EUR 47,000 to EUR 55,000 |

A Portuguese tax resident buying the same apartment moves onto a progressive scale that starts far lower, and a buyer who becomes resident within 24 months can reclaim the excess. See [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

Take absolute prices per square metre by parish from the INE series with a date, as set out in [Portugal house prices](/guides/portugal-house-prices/). A municipal average for Lisbon covers parishes that differ by more than two cities do.

## What It Costs to Hold

| Charge | Lisbon character |
|---|---|
| IMI | 0.3% to 0.45% of rateable value, set by the municipality |
| AIMI | 0.7% above EUR 600,000 of taxable value per owner, reached often in the core |
| Condominium | Highest on old buildings with lifts and facades, lowest in modern blocks |
| Maintenance | Historic stock carries structural and facade obligations that modern stock does not |

The old-building maintenance line is the one that separates a Chiado purchase from a Parque das Nacoes one at the same price. A nineteenth-century building with a shared facade and a retrofitted lift generates works, and those works arrive as condominium assessments. Ask for the last two years of accounts and any approved but unbilled works before you offer.

Running costs in detail are in [Portugal property running costs](/guides/property-management-portugal-cost/).

## Letting: Read the Containment Map First

Lisbon operates Alojamento Local containment across much of the historic centre, restricting new registrations in the parishes where short-term demand is highest. The rule is geographic and it changes, so the only reliable answer is the current municipal position for the specific address.

Verify it before the promissory contract, not after. If a seller presents an existing registration as part of the value, get written confirmation of whether it survives the sale in that parish, and a price mechanism if it does not. The detail is in [Lisbon Alojamento Local containment zones](/guides/lisbon-alojamento-local-containment-zones/).

## Old Stock or New

Lisbon's supply splits into three kinds of building, and the choice drives the running cost more than the district does.

| Stock | What you get | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| Unrenovated historic | The lowest price per square metre in the core | A licensed works project, a queue, and an obras permit |
| Renovated historic | Period fabric with modern services | A premium, and a condominium that still owns an old facade |
| Purpose-built modern | Lifts, parking, insulation, predictable charges | Located where land was available, rarely in the core |

The unrenovated option is where buyers most often misprice. A low headline figure on a building in Alfama or Graca is not a discount until the works, the licence and the licence queue are costed, and in protected zones the permitted scope is narrower than a foreign buyer assumes.

The insulation point matters more in Lisbon than the climate suggests. Older stock was built for mild outdoor winters and is cold and damp indoors from December to February. That is an annual running cost, and it falls on the owner rather than a landlord.

## The Rental Market Underneath the Prices

Two rental markets run in parallel and they behave differently.

Long-term letting is regulated, the tenant protections are real, and the yields are modest because purchase prices have risen faster than rents since 2020. It is the stable option and the one that keeps working regardless of the containment map.

Short-term letting is where the returns looked attractive and where the regulation landed. Containment in the central parishes exists precisely because that market bid up housing, and any model built on it needs the current municipal position rather than a historical one.

For an owner who will use the property part of the year and let it the rest, the practical question is which of the two the building and the parish actually permit, and that is a question for the diligence stage. The yield arithmetic, net of IMI, condominium, management and tax, is set out in [the Portugal rental yield guide](/guides/portugal-rental-yield-guide/).

## Pros and Cons

Advantages:

- The deepest rental and resale market in Portugal
- Long-haul and European flights from an airport inside the city
- The strongest international professional community in the country
- Metro, trains and the Cascais line make much of the city car-optional
- Regeneration districts still offer space per euro below the core

Disadvantages:

- The highest prices in Portugal, and the widest gap to local wages
- Short-term letting contained across much of the centre
- Historic stock carries maintenance obligations that surprise foreign buyers
- Parking is scarce and expensive in the older districts
- Tourist pressure in the core affects daily life as well as prices

## Risks and Red Flags

- **A municipal average price.** Lisbon parishes differ enough that the average describes none of them.
- **Assuming a letting licence.** Containment is geographic and current, and yesterday's answer may not hold.
- **Buying into an old building without the accounts.** Facade and lift works are assessed, not absorbed.
- **A lift described as planned.** Retrofitting a lift into a historic building is a project, not a decision.
- **Regeneration districts viewed only online.** The variance between adjacent streets is the whole risk.

Checklist before you offer:

- Registered comparable sales in the same parish, not the municipality
- Current VPT and last year's IMI on the specific property
- Two years of condominium accounts and any approved works
- The municipal AL position for the address, in writing
- Whether the building has a lift, and if not, whether one is legally possible

## Who Each District Suits

**Buyers who want the historic city.** Chiado, Principe Real, Baixa, accepting old-building costs and letting restrictions.

**Residents rather than visitors.** Avenidas Novas, Campo de Ourique, Estrela: lifts, schools, and daily life that works.

**Families relocating.** Parque das Nacoes for modern city living, or Oeiras and the line for space and schools.

**Buyers wanting upside and willing to look.** Alcantara and Marvila, street by street. Both reward a walk at three different times of day, because a riverside conversion and a still-industrial block can sit two hundred metres apart.

**Buyers letting long-term.** Avenidas Novas and Campo de Ourique, where the tenant pool is deepest and the containment map is irrelevant, rather than the core.
