Quick Answer: Madeira is a steep volcanic island where the side of the mountain and the altitude matter more than the town. The south coast is sunny and where the market is, the north is wetter and cheaper, and almost every house sits on a slope with the maintenance that implies.

British and American buyers arrive at Madeira from different phrases. `madeira property for sale` runs at 720 a month in the UK and `madeira portugal real estate` at 590 in the United States, with `property for sale in madeira` adding another 590 in the UK. It is one of the few Portuguese markets where both vocabularies carry real volume.

What neither phrase tells you is the thing that decides everything here: which side of the mountain, and how high up.

## The Island in Five Markets

| Area | Character | Weather |
|---|---|---|
| Funchal | The capital: hospital, schools, marina, the widest choice and the highest prices | Sunny south, sheltered bay |
| Camara de Lobos and west | A working harbour town minutes from Funchal, terraced hillside | As Funchal, slightly wetter inland |
| Calheta and Ponta do Sol | The sunny south-west, more space, the digital nomad village | The driest and sunniest on the island |
| Santa Cruz and Machico | Airport side, east, practical and better value | Sunny, windier near the airport |
| North coast and interior | Sao Vicente, Porto Moniz, Santana: dramatic and cheap | Wet, green, cloud at altitude |

Two variables cut across that table and matter more than the town name.

**The side.** A mountain spine runs the length of the island and splits the weather in two. The south is Mediterranean in feel, the north is Atlantic and green. They are twenty minutes apart by tunnel and completely different places to live.

**The altitude.** A house 400 metres above Funchal can sit inside cloud on a day when the seafront is in full sun. Buyers viewing in July often do not see this at all. Ask what the property does in a typical January, and ask someone who was there.

## What You Pay on Top

| Cost | Basis | On a EUR 400,000 house |
|---|---|---|
| IMT, non-resident | Flat 7.5% from 1 September 2026 | EUR 30,000 |
| Stamp duty | 0.8% | EUR 3,200 |
| Notary and registry | Fixed and variable | Low four figures |
| Legal fees | Commonly 1% to 1.5% | EUR 4,000 to EUR 6,000 |
| Total | Roughly 9% to 11% | About EUR 38,000 to EUR 44,000 |

Madeira is an autonomous region with its own legislative assembly, and some matters are set regionally rather than nationally. The national purchase taxes apply, but ask your lawyer to confirm the current regional position on anything that touches licensing or letting rather than assuming the mainland answer. The national mechanics are in [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

## What It Costs to Hold on a Slope

| Charge | Madeira character |
|---|---|
| IMI | 0.3% to 0.45% of rateable value, set by each council |
| AIMI | Reached mainly on Funchal sea-view property |
| Condominium | Moderate in Funchal blocks, absent on standalone hillside houses |
| Retaining walls and terracing | The island-specific line, and the expensive one |
| Drainage | Heavy rain tests it annually, and failures are structural |
| Humidity | High on the north side and at altitude, which affects fabric and finishes |

The slope is the running cost that no listing mentions. Terraced sites are held up by retaining structures, access is often a private track, and both need maintenance that a flat mainland plot never generates. Before buying anything on a hillside, get the retaining walls and the drainage inspected specifically, and ask what has been repaired in the last decade.

The general framework is in [Portugal property running costs](/guides/property-management-portugal-cost/).

## Letting, and the Nomad Effect

Madeira has a real short-term rental market driven by year-round mild weather rather than a summer season, which is unusual in Portugal and is a genuine advantage for an owner who lets.

It also has something no other Portuguese region has: a regional initiative that established a digital nomad village at Ponta do Sol, with coworking and an organised community. That has fed a longer-stay rental market on the sunny south-west coast which behaves differently from a holiday let, with longer bookings and lower turnover.

Letting requires an Alojamento Local registration under the national framework, applied regionally. Confirm the current position for the specific address before the promissory contract. See [the Alojamento Local guide](/guides/alojamento-local-license-portugal/), and for the visa side of the nomad market, [the D8 digital nomad visa](/move-to-portugal/d8-digital-nomad-visa/).

## Porto Santo, the Other Island

Forty minutes by air or two and a half hours by ferry, Porto Santo has the one thing Madeira does not: nine kilometres of golden sand.

That single fact creates a separate second-home market, mostly bought by Madeirans and mainland Portuguese, with a summer season and a very quiet winter. Prices are below Madeira's, the choice is narrow, and the island has a few thousand residents out of season.

Three practical points before anyone treats it as a cheaper Madeira. Services are limited, so anything medical or specialist means a flight. The winter is genuinely empty, not merely quiet. And the resale market is small enough that the exit depends on a handful of buyers a year rather than on a price.

It works as a summer house for someone already anchored on Madeira or the mainland. It works poorly as a sole base.

## Building and Buying Off Plan Here

Island logistics change construction economics. Materials arrive by sea, specialist trades are a smaller pool, and steep sites need engineering that a flat mainland plot does not.

| Factor | What it means on Madeira |
|---|---|
| Build cost per square metre | Higher than the mainland for equivalent specification |
| Groundworks | Terracing, retaining and access frequently exceed expectations |
| Timelines | Longer, and weather-sensitive on the north side |
| Trades | A smaller pool, so scheduling matters more |
| Licensing | Municipal, with protected landscape adding scope questions in places |

The practical consequence is that a plot with planning is not the bargain it looks like unless the groundworks have been costed by someone who has built on that gradient. Ask for a groundworks estimate before valuing the land, not after.

Off-plan purchases carry the usual completion risk with an island-specific twist: a delayed shipment can move a schedule in a way it would not on the mainland. Check what the contract says about staged payments if the schedule slips, and check it is in the contract rather than in a conversation.

## Pros and Cons

Advantages:

- Mild all year, with no real off-season for letting
- A genuine hospital, international school and airport on an island of this size
- Prices below the Lisbon metro for comparable quality
- The digital nomad village gives the south-west a year-round rental base
- Direct flights to several northern European cities

Disadvantages:

- No sandy beaches in the mainland sense
- Almost everything is on a slope, with the maintenance that implies
- Buildable land is genuinely scarce, so good sites are priced
- The north coast is wet enough to change how a house is built and kept
- Island logistics: everything arrives by sea or air, which shows in build costs

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Viewing only in summer.** The altitude and the side of the island are winter questions.
- **A slope taken on trust.** Retaining walls and drainage are structural, and their failure is expensive.
- **Assuming the mainland rulebook throughout.** Madeira is autonomous; confirm anything regional.
- **A view priced without checking the cloud line.** Altitude changes what you actually see, and how often.
- **Access tracks assumed to be public.** Private access on this terrain is common and needs documenting.

Checklist before you offer:

- Establish the side of the island and the altitude, and ask what January looks like there
- Commission a specific inspection of retaining structures and drainage
- Confirm the legal status of the access
- Get the current VPT and last year's IMI for the property
- Confirm the letting position for the address with the municipality

## Who Each Area Suits

**Buyers who want services.** Funchal, and specifically the parishes with level access, which cost more for exactly that reason.

**Buyers optimising on price near the capital.** Camara de Lobos, minutes west, with the same hospital and airport access.

**Remote workers and sun-seekers.** Calheta and Ponta do Sol on the south-west, where the nomad community and the driest weather both are.

**Practical buyers.** Santa Cruz and Machico on the airport side, where the value is and the flights are close.

**Buyers who want drama and low prices.** The north coast, after a winter visit rather than before one. Sao Vicente and Porto Moniz are spectacular and genuinely wet, and the houses there are built for it in a way a south-coast villa is not.
