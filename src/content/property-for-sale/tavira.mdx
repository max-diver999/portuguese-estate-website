Quick Answer: Tavira is the eastern Algarve's answer to the question of where you can actually live all year. It has a resident population, a hospital, a market and services that run in February, at prices below the central resort strip. The beach is on an island across the lagoon, which is either a feature or a dealbreaker.

The central Algarve sells a season. Tavira sells a year.

## The Tavira Markets

| Area | Character | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| Old town, both banks of the Gilao | Cobbled streets, townhouses, the Roman bridge | Protected, so works are constrained; parking is hard |
| Newer town quarters | Apartments and modern townhouses, walkable | Less character, more practicality |
| Riverside and Santa Luzia | Water frontage, the octopus village, the footbridge island | Priced for the water |
| Cabanas de Tavira | Waterfront village facing its own island | More holiday-oriented, quieter in winter |
| Inland toward Santo Estevao | Quintas, land, the lowest prices | A car, and a drive to everything |
| Conceicao and Cabanas hinterland | Newer villa developments with pools | Suburban rather than town |

## Area by Area

**The old town.** Two banks of the Gilao joined by the bridge, with the castle and Santa Maria do Castelo above. The housing is townhouses, often narrow, frequently in need of work, and inside a protected area where the municipality decides what may be altered. Charming, and the least straightforward purchase in the town.

**The newer quarters.** Apartments and modern townhouses within walking distance of the market and the river. This is where most practical buyers end up: lifts, parking, and a five-minute walk to a supermarket that is open in January.

**Riverside and Santa Luzia.** Santa Luzia is the octopus-fishing village a few kilometres west, with a footbridge to Praia do Barril, and it prices for the setting. Riverside property in Tavira itself is the town's premium tier.

**Cabanas de Tavira.** A waterfront strip facing the lagoon and its island, with a boardwalk and a short ferry. Smaller, more seasonal and more holiday-oriented than Tavira, with front-line apartments above the Tavira average.

**Inland.** Quintas, olive groves and the lowest prices in the municipality. The trade is honest: land and quiet in exchange for driving to everything, including the bakery.

Many Tavira buyers are relocating rather than buying a second home, which makes the timing point worth stating: buying after establishing Portuguese tax residency puts you on the resident scale instead of the flat 7.5%. See [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

## What It Costs to Hold

| Charge | Tavira character |
|---|---|
| IMI | 0.3% to 0.45% of rateable value, at the municipality's rate |
| AIMI | Rarely reached at Tavira price levels |
| Condominium | Low in the town, higher in villa developments with pools |
| Damp and salt | Old-town townhouses and lagoon-front property both need attention |
| Pool and garden | A recurring service contract wherever there is one |

Old-town damp is the specific local issue. Traditional construction, a river, and a lagoon combine to make moisture management an ongoing job rather than a one-off repair. Ask the current owner what they actually do about it each year. General running costs are in [Portugal property running costs](/guides/property-management-portugal-cost/).

## The Island Beaches, and What They Mean Daily

The Ria Formosa is a lagoon system protected behind barrier islands, and the beaches sit on those islands rather than on the mainland shore.

| Access | How | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tavira Island | Ferry from the town or from Quatro Aguas | A short crossing, and a timetable |
| Praia do Barril | Footbridge and a small train from Santa Luzia | Walkable, and popular in summer |
| Cabanas Island | Ferry from the Cabanas boardwalk | Minutes across, seasonal frequency |

This is the eastern Algarve's defining trade. You get a protected natural park, calm water, birdlife and no beachfront development, and you give up walking out of the house onto sand. Buyers who want the second should look at the central strip instead, and it is better to establish that before viewing than after.

The lagoon also constrains development, which is part of why the eastern Algarve has kept its character while the central strip built out.

## Renovating in a Protected Town

A large share of what is for sale in the old town needs work, and the constraint is not the builder.

Three things belong in the diligence. The scope of what you may alter is set by the municipality, and in the protected area it is narrower than most foreign buyers expect, particularly for facades, windows and rooflines. The obras licence is a municipal process with a queue, and a renovation planned around a summer letting season that starts before the permit issues will miss it. And documented, invoiced works reduce your taxable gain when you eventually sell, which makes proper invoicing worth more than a cash discount.

Get a written indication of the permitted scope from the camara before the offer, not after the survey.

## Getting There and Getting Around

The eastern Algarve is better connected than its quiet reputation suggests, and the detail matters for anyone flying in and out regularly.

| Route | Practical position |
|---|---|
| Faro airport | 35 to 45 minutes by the A22 motorway |
| Rail | A coastal line runs Tavira to Faro and on to Lagos, slow but useful |
| Spanish border | Vila Real de Santo Antonio and the ferry to Ayamonte, about 30 minutes |
| Seville airport | Roughly two and a half hours, and often cheaper in winter |
| Lisbon | Two and a half to three hours by motorway |

The Seville option is the one buyers discover late. When the Faro winter schedule thins, Seville frequently has flights that Faro does not, and the drive is manageable for a planned trip. For a household flying home several times a year, that changes the calculation about winter accessibility.

Inside the town, the old centre is walkable and the parking is not. If you are buying in the historic quarter, establish where you will actually leave a car before you commit, because the answer is often a paid lot several minutes away rather than the street outside.

## Pros and Cons

Advantages:

- A genuine year-round town with a resident population and services
- The mildest winters in mainland Portugal
- Faro airport 35 to 45 minutes away, with a coastal railway too
- Prices below the central Algarve strip for comparable property
- The Ria Formosa natural park caps development, which protects the setting

Disadvantages:

- No beach at the door: the sand is across the water
- Old-town stock is protected and frequently needs structural work
- Parking in the historic centre is difficult
- A shorter and narrower letting season than the central strip
- Damp is an ongoing job in traditional construction

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Assuming a mainland beach.** The islands are the beaches, and the ferry is the access.
- **Buying an old-town house without the permitted scope.** The municipality decides, not the architect.
- **Renovation costed without the licence queue.** The permit is part of the timeline and the price.
- **Holiday-let income assumed.** Verify the municipal position and model a shorter season.
- **Damp treated as a one-off.** In this construction it is annual maintenance.

Checklist before you offer:

- A survey on anything in the old town, and specifically for damp and roof
- A written indication of permitted works from the camara
- The current VPT and last year's IMI on the property
- The municipal Alojamento Local position for the address
- A winter visit, and a summer one, before deciding between Tavira and Cabanas

## Who Tavira Suits

**Retirees and year-round residents.** The core case, and the reason the town has the foreign population it does. Services, a hospital and a winter that is genuinely mild.

**Buyers who want a Portuguese town rather than a resort.** Tavira still functions as a town for its own residents, which is increasingly rare on this coast.

**Renovators with patience.** The old town rewards it, provided the permitted scope is established first.

**Holiday-home buyers who want quiet.** Cabanas, accepting a shorter season and a smaller village.

**Buyers who want to walk to a beach.** Not here. The central Algarve, covered in [property for sale in the Algarve](/property-for-sale/algarve/), is the right search.
