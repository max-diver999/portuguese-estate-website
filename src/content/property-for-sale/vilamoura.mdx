## A Resort, Not a Town

It is worth being clear about what Vilamoura is, because the usual town vocabulary does not apply.

The development began in the 1970s on farmland at the mouth of a creek, next to the Roman site at Cerro da Vila. Everything since has been planned: the marina basin was dug, the road layout was drawn, the height limits were set low, the green space was designed in, and the golf courses were laid out as part of the scheme rather than added later.

The consequences for a buyer are specific.

**There is no historic core**, so the walkable-old-town premium that shapes prices in Lagos, Tavira or Albufeira does not exist here. What replaces it is the marina.

**The zoning holds.** Low-rise, generous spacing and landscaping are enforced by the resort plan, which is why Vilamoura does not look like the Albufeira Strip despite being eight kilometres away.

**Maintenance is collective and visible.** Roads, verges, lighting and landscaping are maintained to a standard, and the cost of that standard appears in condominium budgets and resort charges.

Whether that adds up to a pleasant, well-run environment or a place without character is a genuine matter of taste, and buyers tend to know within an hour of arriving.

## Area by Area

**The marina.** The largest marina on the Algarve and the resort's centre of gravity, with around eight hundred berths, restaurants along the quay and the highest apartment prices in Vilamoura. Front-row apartments overlook the boats; a row or two back the price drops appreciably for a short walk. This is also the part of Vilamoura with the most life outside summer.

**The golf belt.** Five courses sit within the resort, and the property around them is the villa market: detached houses with pools, townhouses in gated schemes, and golf apartments on the fairway edge. The Old Course is the original and the most established address; Victoria has hosted the Portugal Masters and anchors the newer development to the north.

Golf frontage is priced as a premium, and it is worth checking whether the outlook is protected by the resort plan or simply current. A fairway is not going to be built on; a strip of scrub between you and it might be.

**The beach side.** Praia da Marina sits immediately east of the harbour wall, and beyond it the sand runs into Praia da Falesia, the long red-cliff beach shared with Albufeira. Resort apartment developments dominate, most with pools and gardens, and the condominium budgets reflect that.

**Quarteira.** Immediately west, and a different place entirely: a Portuguese town with a working fish market, a long seafront promenade, older apartment blocks and a resident population that does not leave in October. Prices are materially below Vilamoura for comparable space. Buyers frequently discover Quarteira after pricing Vilamoura and find it suits them better.

## Condominium Charges Are the Story

Vilamoura's running costs are dominated by one line, and it is worth understanding before rather than after.

| What the budget funds | Why it costs what it does |
|---|---|
| Pools | Servicing, chemicals, pump electricity, periodic resurfacing |
| Landscaped gardens | Irrigation and year-round labour |
| Lifts | Maintenance contracts and statutory inspections |
| Private roads and lighting | Within gated and semi-private developments |
| Security and gates | Where present, a substantial staffed cost |
| Reserve fund | Or the absence of one, which is the risk |

Resort developments here carry among the highest condominium budgets in Portugal, and they arrive whether the apartment is occupied or empty. That is not a flaw; it is what maintains the standard the resort sells. It is a flaw only when a buyer models the purchase and not the charge.

Two documents settle it before you offer: two years of accounts, and the minutes of the last two general meetings. A pool refurbishment, a lift replacement or a facade project approved and not yet billed is a liability that transfers at completion and has no ceiling. See [condominium fees in Portugal](/guides/condominium-fees-portugal/).

| IMT, non-resident | Flat 7.5% on residential property from 1 September 2026 | EUR 33,750 |
| Stamp duty | 0.8% of price | EUR 3,600 |
| Notary and land registry | Fixed and variable | Low four figures |
| Legal fees | Commonly 1% to 1.5% | EUR 4,500 to EUR 6,750 |
| Total acquisition | Roughly 9% to 11% | About EUR 43,000 to EUR 50,000 |

Most Vilamoura buyers are second-home purchasers who remain non-resident, which means the flat 7.5% rate is the one that applies rather than the resident scale. See [IMT for non-residents](/guides/imt-tax-non-resident-portugal-2026/).

At villa and marina-front prices, AIMI is also in play: 0.7% annually on taxable value above EUR 600,000 per owner, with each joint owner holding their own allowance. That is a planning point for before the deed. See [AIMI, the wealth surcharge](/guides/aimi-wealth-tax-portugal-property/).

## Letting: Loule Sets the Rules

Vilamoura has one of the most established short-term letting markets in the Algarve, with professional management companies operating at scale and a booking pattern that begins with golf shoulder seasons rather than only summer.

That last point is a genuine advantage. Golf pulls visitors into March, April, October and November, months that are dead in a beach-only resort, which lengthens the usable season considerably compared with a comparable Albufeira apartment.

Three practical points. The licensing position is set by **Loule** municipality, so verify there rather than assuming a neighbouring council's rules. Golf and marina proximity are the two strongest rate drivers, and a property with neither is competing on price. And a professionally managed let carries a management percentage that belongs in the model from the start. The framework is in [the Alojamento Local guide](/guides/alojamento-local-license-portugal/), and the net yield arithmetic in [the Portugal rental yield guide](/guides/portugal-rental-yield-guide/).

## New Build and Off Plan in a Planned Resort

Vilamoura still has land in its plan, which makes it one of the few Algarve markets where new build is a normal option rather than an occasional one.

| | New build here | Resale here |
|---|---|---|
| Energy performance | Built to current standards, which shows in winter running costs | Anything from the 1980s is usually poor |
| Pool and plant | New, under warranty | Age matters, and is rarely volunteered |
| Position | Wherever the remaining plots are, usually further from the marina | The established addresses were built first |
| Condominium | A new budget with no reserve history | A known budget with a track record |
| Price | A clear premium | Cheaper per square metre, with a works allowance behind it |
| Completion risk | Real, and staged against milestones | None |

The condominium point cuts against new build in a way buyers rarely consider. A brand-new development has no reserve fund and no history of what its pool, lift and landscaping actually cost to run, so the first three years of budgets are estimates. An established development has both, which is why its accounts are worth reading and a new one's are worth questioning.

On off plan, ask what the contract says happens to staged payments if the schedule slips, and check that the answer is in the contract rather than in a conversation with the sales office.

## What Was Here Before

One detail worth knowing, because it explains something about the place. The resort was built around Cerro da Vila, a Roman site with villa remains, baths and mosaics that sits a few minutes from the marina and is open to visitors.

That matters for two reasons beyond the visit. It is the reason parts of the resort were laid out as they were, since archaeology constrains what can be dug and where. And it is a corrective to the idea that Vilamoura is a place with no history: what it lacks is a Portuguese town centre, which is a different thing from lacking a past.

For a buyer the practical version of this is that ground conditions and archaeology occasionally shape what may be built on a specific parcel here, which is worth asking about on any plot purchase or major extension rather than assuming the resort plan is the only constraint.

## Resale, and Why It Is Reliable Here

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Standardised stock in known developments | Comparable sales are plentiful, so pricing is a calculation |
| A large British and Irish buyer pool | Consistent demand across cycles |
| 25 minutes from the airport | Widens the pool of realistic owners |
| Professional management infrastructure | An investor buyer can operate from abroad |
| Enforced zoning | The environment does not degrade, which protects value |

Vilamoura is among the more liquid Algarve markets, second only to Albufeira in transaction depth and arguably ahead of it on the quality of the stock. For an owner who may need to sell within a defined window, that is worth real money against a comparable spend on a rural or western Algarve property.

## Pros and Cons

Advantages:

- The shortest airport transfer on the Algarve, about 25 minutes
- Golf extends the letting season into spring and autumn
- Enforced low-rise zoning and maintained public realm
- A deep, liquid resale market with plentiful comparables
- Established professional management for absentee owners

Disadvantages:

- No historic centre, and buyers who want one will not find it here
- Condominium budgets among the highest in Portugal
- AIMI applies to a meaningful share of the stock
- Quiet out of season, and quieter than a real town
- Prices well above neighbouring Quarteira for a comparable specification

## Risks and Red Flags

- **Condominium accounts not requested.** Pool and lift projects are assessed, not absorbed.
- **A golf or marina view assumed permanent.** Ask what the resort plan says about the land in between.
- **Loule's rules confused with Albufeira's.** Vilamoura is not in Albufeira municipality.
- **A peak-week yield annualised.** Golf lengthens the season; it does not fill the calendar.
- **A development judged in July.** Ask what it looks like, and how many neighbours are there, in February.

Checklist before you offer:

- Two years of condominium accounts and minutes, plus any approved but unbilled works
- The pool plant age and resurfacing history for the development
- The Loule Alojamento Local position for the specific address, in writing
- Current VPT, last year's IMI, and whether AIMI applies
- Registered comparable sales within the same development where possible

## Who Each Area Suits

**Frequent short-stay owners.** The marina ring, on the airport proximity and the walkability, accepting the price.

**Golfers and families wanting space.** The golf belt, where the villas and townhouses are and where the outlook is generally protected.

**Investors letting professionally.** Beach-side and golf apartments in known developments, with the management percentage and the void months modelled.

**Buyers who want a Portuguese town.** Quarteira, which is a genuine one, at a materially lower price and with a winter.

**Buyers who want an old town.** Not Vilamoura, by design. [Lagos](/property-for-sale/lagos/) and [Tavira](/property-for-sale/tavira/) have one, and the regional comparison is in [property for sale in the Algarve](/property-for-sale/algarve/).
